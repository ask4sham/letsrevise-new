9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Photosynthesis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction—plants absorb energy from sunlight.</strong></p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Plants use light energy trapped in chloroplasts to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The chlorophyll in chloroplasts captures light energy.</li>  
<li>Energy is used to join carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) and water (H<sub>2</sub>O) to make glucose (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub>) and release oxygen (O<sub>2</sub>).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll pigments to absorb sunlight efficiently.</li>  
<li>Light provides energy to break and remake chemical bonds, making photosynthesis endothermic.</li>  
<li>Glucose serves as an energy store and building block for growth.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Limiting factors affecting photosynthesis:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Light intensity:</strong> More light increases photosynthesis until another factor limits it.</li>  
<li><strong>Carbon dioxide concentration:</strong> The more CO<sub>2</sub> available, the faster glucose is made.</li>  
<li><strong>Temperature:</strong> Photosynthesis needs enzymes; too cold slows enzymes, too hot denatures them.</li>  
<li><strong>Chlorophyll amount:</strong> Leaves with less chlorophyll photosynthesise slower.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always link the factor to its effect on enzyme activity or energy availability, then explain how this limits glucose or oxygen production.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 <strong>A common mistake is</strong> just naming factors that affect photosynthesis (like "light") without explaining how they limit the rate.</p>  
<p>👉 <strong>The correction is</strong> to say light provides energy, so low light means less energy to make glucose, which slows photosynthesis.</p>  
<p>👉 <strong>This earns marks</strong> because it links cause (light) to effect (rate of photosynthesis).</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Photosynthesis reaction and limiting factors

Step 1:  
Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll in the chloroplasts.

↓  

Step 2:  
Energy splits water molecules into oxygen and hydrogen; oxygen is released.

↓  

Step 3:  
Carbon dioxide from the air is combined with hydrogen using energy to form glucose.

↓  

Step 4:  
Rate depends on light intensity, CO<sub>2</sub> concentration, temperature, and chlorophyll amount; if any are low, photosynthesis rate is limited.

Exam link:  
Explaining limiting factors in terms of enzyme action or energy availability scores marks in exams.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Photosynthesis is crucial for life because it produces glucose, which provides energy for plants and animals, and oxygen, which is essential for respiration.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exams, don’t just list factors affecting photosynthesis – always explain how each factor changes enzyme activity or energy availability and how that limits the glucose or oxygen produced.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Light affects photosynthesis.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Light affects photosynthesis because the plant needs energy from light to make glucose.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Light intensity affects photosynthesis because chlorophyll absorbs light energy required to make chemical bonds in glucose; if light is low, less energy slows enzyme activity, reducing glucose and oxygen production.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It links light to enzyme activity and glucose production.</li>  
<li>It uses precise terms like 'chlorophyll', 'energy', and 'chemical bonds.'</li>  
<li>It explains the cause → process → effect chain clearly.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A student measures oxygen bubbles from pondweed at different CO<sub>2</sub> concentrations and sees the rate of bubbling plateau after a point. Explain why the rate stops increasing.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Photosynthesis rate initially increases as more CO<sub>2</sub> is available for glucose production. But after a point, CO<sub>2</sub> is not the limiting factor; either light or temperature limits enzyme activity, so rate plateaus despite more CO<sub>2</sub>.  
</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>Glucose produced in photosynthesis has multiple uses:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li>Used in respiration to release energy.</li>  
<li>Converted to starch and stored for energy reserves.</li>  
<li>Turned into fats or oils for long-term energy storage.</li>  
<li>Used to make cellulose to strengthen cell walls, supporting growth.</li>  
<li>Combined with nitrate ions from soil to make amino acids and then proteins.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>🎯 Understanding these uses links photosynthesis to plant growth and survival, emphasising its fundamental biological role.</p>  

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Why do plants convert glucose into starch rather than storing glucose directly?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Because starch is insoluble and does not affect water potential, allowing safe storage without upsetting cell water balance, unlike soluble glucose.  
</details>

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>👉 Photosynthesis produces glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water using light energy.</p>  
<p>👉 It is an endothermic process happening inside chloroplasts containing chlorophyll.</p>  
<p>👉 The rate depends on light intensity, carbon dioxide levels, temperature, and chlorophyll amount, each of which can be limiting factors.</p>  
<p>👉 Practical experiments like using pondweed can measure photosynthesis rate by counting oxygen bubbles.</p>  
<p>👉 Glucose made is vital: it's used in respiration, stored as starch, converted to oils, turned into cellulose, or used to make proteins.</p>  
<p>👉 Remember: explain cause → effect chains in exams for full marks.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Photosynthesis</strong> – Process where plants make glucose and oxygen using light energy.  
<strong>Endothermic</strong> – A reaction that absorbs energy, like photosynthesis.  
<strong>Chloroplast</strong> – Cell organelle containing chlorophyll where photosynthesis occurs.  
<strong>Limiting factor</strong> – Anything that slows down the rate of a process when in short supply.  
<strong>Glucose</strong> – A simple sugar produced by photosynthesis as food/energy.  
<strong>Starch</strong> – Insoluble storage form of glucose in plants.  
<strong>Respiration</strong> – Process of releasing energy from glucose.  
<strong>Light intensity</strong> – Amount of light available to drive photosynthesis.  
<strong>Carbon dioxide concentration</strong> – Amount of CO<sub>2</sub> available for photosynthesis.  
<strong>Cellulose</strong> – A structural carbohydrate forming plant cell walls made from glucose.
