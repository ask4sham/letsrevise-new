9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Atomic Structure</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that atoms have a central nucleus containing protons and neutrons, with electrons arranged around them in shells.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Protons carry a positive charge and define the element’s identity by their number — this number is called the proton number or atomic number.</li>  
<li>Neutrons have no charge; they add mass and affect isotope identity but don’t change the element type.</li>  
<li>Electrons carry a negative charge and orbit the nucleus in layers called shells, balancing the positive charge.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The proton number tells us which element an atom is because it is unique to that element.</li>  
<li>The mass number equals the total number of protons plus neutrons, showing how heavy the atom is.</li>  
<li>Electrons arranged in shells determine how atoms interact chemically because chemical reactions involve electron transfer or sharing.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When describing atomic structure, clearly state the particle involved, its charge, and its role. Always link the proton number to the element and the mass number to total nucleons.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say electrons orbit the nucleus like solid shells or fixed paths, like planets orbiting the sun.</p>  
<p>Electrons actually exist in regions called shells or energy levels, which are not solid orbits but areas where electrons are most likely to be found.</p>  
<p>This matters because examiners look for understanding of electron cloud models, not fixed orbits.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding atomic structure is vital because exam questions often ask you to explain why an element behaves a certain way based on its particles.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When asked to explain something about atoms, always link the number of protons to the element’s identity, and explain mass number as the total of protons and neutrons — be precise and use these terms.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The proton number tells you what element it is.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The proton number tells you the number of protons in the atom, so it shows the element because each element has a unique proton number.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The proton number, also called the atomic number, is the number of protons in the nucleus. It uniquely identifies the element because no two elements have the same proton number. The mass number is the total number of protons plus neutrons and shows the isotope of the element.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It defines key terms precisely (atomic number, mass number).</li>  
<li>Explains uniqueness of proton number for element identity.</li>  
<li>Mentions isotopes to show deeper understanding.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how you can use the proton number and mass number to identify the element and isotope of an atom with 17 protons and 18 neutrons.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: The proton number is 17, so the element is chlorine because chlorine’s atomic number is 17.  
Step 2: The mass number is protons + neutrons = 17 + 18 = 35.  
Step 3: Therefore, this atom is the isotope chlorine-35.  
Step 4: This means it is chlorine with a mass number of 35 due to its neutrons.  

</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>👉 To sum up atomic structure:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>Atoms are made of protons, neutrons, and electrons.</li>  
<li>Protons and neutrons form the nucleus at the center.</li>  
<li>Electrons orbit in shells around the nucleus.</li>  
<li>The proton number identifies the element uniquely.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Atom</strong> – The smallest unit of an element, made up of protons, neutrons, and electrons.  
<strong>Proton</strong> – A positively charged particle in the nucleus defining element identity.  
<strong>Neutron</strong> – A neutrally charged particle in the nucleus adding mass.  
<strong>Electron</strong> – A negatively charged particle orbiting the nucleus in shells.  
<strong>Proton number (Atomic number)</strong> – The number of protons in an atom, defining the element.  
<strong>Mass number</strong> – The total number of protons and neutrons in an atom’s nucleus.  
<strong>Isotope</strong> – Atoms with the same proton number but different numbers of neutrons.  
<strong>Nucleus</strong> – The center of an atom containing protons and neutrons.  
<strong>Shell</strong> – Energy levels where electrons orbit the nucleus.  
<strong>Charge</strong> – The electrical property of particles (positive, negative, or neutral).
