9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Algebra</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 The key idea is: Algebra is the language of generalized maths, letting us create formulas, solve equations, and express relationships.</p>  

<h3><strong>Method → Reason:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Start with expressions: combinations of numbers and variables without an equals sign.</li>  
<li>Combine like terms to simplify expressions, reducing complexity.</li>  
<li>Use algebraic expansion to remove brackets and write expressions in standard form.</li>  
<li>Factorising reverses expansion, essential for solving quadratic equations.</li>  
<li>Set up equations by equating expressions to values or to each other.</li>  
<li>Solve linear equations by isolating variables using inverse operations.</li>  
<li>For quadratics, use methods like factorising, completing the square, or the quadratic formula.</li>  
<li>Inequalities show ranges of possible values, solved similarly but paying attention to reversing inequality signs when multiplying/dividing by negatives.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Use clear steps: write the original equation, show each algebraic step logically, and state the solution clearly with units or context where needed.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is forgetting to multiply every term inside brackets when expanding. For example, writing 3(x + 5) as 3x + 5 instead of 3x + 15.</p>  
<p>This loses marks because the expression is simplified incorrectly.</p>  
<p>Always multiply each term inside the brackets by the outside factor to avoid this error.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra is central to GCSE maths. Understanding language and methods means you can tackle a broad range of exam questions, from simplifying expressions to solving complex equations.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always show every algebraic step clearly and justify key actions like expanding, rearranging, or factoring — examiners reward thorough, logical method over rushed answers.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The student writes down the answer to an equation but skips steps and does not show working.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The student shows some working steps, like expanding brackets or moving terms, but skips important justification or makes minor errors.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The student begins with the original equation, clearly expands or rearranges correctly, isolates the variable step-by-step, and ends with a solution and a check or interpretation.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It follows logical, clear steps an examiner can follow easily.</li>  
<li>It shows mastery of algebraic techniques, earning full method marks.</li>  
<li>It avoids careless errors and explains reasoning, which strengthens argumentation for higher marks.</li>  
</ul>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE

Question:  
Solve 2(x − 3) + 4 = 14.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Expand brackets: 2x − 6 + 4 = 14  
Step 2: Simplify left side: 2x − 2 = 14  
Step 3: Add 2 to both sides: 2x = 16  
Step 4: Divide both sides by 2: x = 8  
Step 5: Check by substitution into the original equation if time permits.

</details>

<h2><strong>💡 Key Insight</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In algebra, think of expressions as “phrases” and equations as “sentences” with an equals sign. Always simplify expressions fully before solving equations. 24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Expression</strong> – A combination of variables, numbers and operations without an equals sign.<br />  
<strong>Equation</strong> – A mathematical statement showing two expressions are equal.<br />  
<strong>Formula</strong> – An equation that relates variables to each other.<br />  
<strong>Inequality</strong> – A statement showing one quantity is larger or smaller than another.<br />  
<strong>Expand</strong> – Removing brackets by multiplying out.<br />  
<strong>Factorise</strong> – Writing an expression as a product of its factors.<br />  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – Combining like terms to make expressions easier.<br />  
<strong>Solve</strong> – Finding the value(s) of the variable(s) that make an equation true.<br />  
<strong>Substitute</strong> – Replacing variables with numbers.<br />  
<strong>Like terms</strong> – Terms that have the same variable parts and can be combined.</p>
