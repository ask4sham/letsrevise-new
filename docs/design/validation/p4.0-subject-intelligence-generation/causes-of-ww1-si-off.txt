<p>👉 <strong>Cause → Process → Effect is the core GCSE historical model: a cause (reason why something happened) triggers a process (what actions or changes it set off), resulting in an effect (the final outcome or impact, like war).</strong></p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Causes of WW1</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>📘 The key idea is:</strong></p>  
<p>WW1 broke out because a mix of long-term tensions and short-term events interacted to start war in 1914.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Long-term causes like Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, and Nationalism built up tension for decades.</li>  
<li>The assassination in 1914 was the short-term trigger that started the war.</li>  
<li>Countries quickly mobilised and declared war because their alliances meant they had to support their partners.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Cause → process → effect chains:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Militarism → arms race → increased fear and readiness for war → tension rises.</strong></li>  
<li><strong>Alliances → treaties promising support → countries forced into conflict → war spreads fast.</strong></li>  
<li><strong>Assassination → Austria's harsh demands on Serbia → Serbia refused → war declaration → war breaks out.</strong></li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain why a cause mattered, what happened because of it, and what the final result was, like: “Militarism caused an arms race, which made countries more ready to fight, so when the crisis came, war became likely.”</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying <strong>"WW1 was caused just by the assassination."</strong></p>  

<p>👉 The correction is to explain that the assassination was a trigger, but only because long-term causes like militarism and alliances made the situation explosive.</p>  

<p>👉 This correction earns marks because examiners want understanding of both short-term and long-term causes, not just one event.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the causes of WW1 is key for GCSE History because it shows you how to connect long-term tensions with immediate events and write clear cause → process → effect answers.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In the exam, always structure your answers to explain why a cause mattered, describe what happened because of it, and finish with the effect or consequence. This shows clear historical reasoning and wins top marks.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 happened because of alliances.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 happened because alliances made countries promise to help each other in war.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 happened because the Alliance System caused countries to support their allies. For example, when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, Russia mobilised to help Serbia, which then caused Germany to declare war on Russia, spreading the conflict rapidly across Europe.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains cause → effect clearly and fully.</li>  
<li>It uses precise terminology like “Alliance System” and “mobilised”.</li>  
<li>It gives a specific example to support the point.</li>  
</ul>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The outbreak of WW1 was not caused by one factor but by a combination working together.</p>  
<p>👉 Militarism made countries ready for war. Alliances forced many into conflict. Imperialism and nationalism created rivalries and internal tensions. The assassination was the trigger, activating these explosive conditions.</p>  
<p>👉 This combined model explains why a single event led to a massive world war.</p>  

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Explain why the Alliance System increased the risk of a large-scale war.  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

The Alliance System created agreements between countries to protect each other. This meant that if one country was attacked or went to war, its allies were bound to join. Therefore, a conflict involving one country quickly spread to others, turning a small dispute into a large-scale war.  

</details>

<strong>Q4:</strong> Imperialism was an important cause because competition for colonies increased rivalry between countries like Britain and Germany. However, it was not the main cause because militarism and alliances also built tension. The assassination triggered war only because these factors made countries ready and willing to fight, so I partly agree imperialism contributed but was not the main cause.

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  

<ul>  
<li>WW1 had multiple causes working together, not just one event.</li>  
<li>Militarism increased armies and created fear, pushing countries toward war.</li>  
<li>Alliances divided Europe and forced countries into conflict.</li>  
<li>Imperialism led to competition for colonies, increasing rivalries.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Militarism</strong> – Building up armed forces and preparing for war.  
<strong>Alliances</strong> – Agreements between countries to defend each other.  
<strong>Imperialism</strong> – When countries compete to build empires and colonies.  
<strong>Nationalism</strong> – Strong pride and loyalty to one’s country or ethnic group.  
<strong>Arms race</strong> – Competition to build more and better weapons.  
<strong>Assassination</strong> – The murder of a political leader to cause change.  
<strong>Diplomacy</strong> – Managing relationships between countries peacefully.  
<strong>Mobilisation</strong> – Preparing and moving troops for war.  
<strong>Blank cheque</strong> – Germany’s promise to support Austria-Hungary fully in 1914.  
<strong>Balance of power</strong> – An equal distribution of military and political power between countries to prevent war.
