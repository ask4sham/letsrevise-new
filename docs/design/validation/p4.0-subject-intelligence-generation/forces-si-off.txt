3 — DEFINITION  
<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A force is a push or a pull that acts on an object due to interaction with another object.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Forces explain how and why objects move, stop, or change shape, which is essential for understanding everything from a car accelerating to planets orbiting.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Force (push or pull) → change in motion (acceleration or deceleration) → energy transfer (kinetic or potential energy changes)</strong></p>  
<p>In Higher Tier, this includes balanced and unbalanced forces, resultant forces, and using Newton’s second law:</p>  
<p><strong>Resultant force (N) = mass (kg) × acceleration (m/s²)</strong></p>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES

<li>A person pushing a shopping trolley causes it to speed up due to an unbalanced force.</li>  
<li>Gravity pulling objects downwards causing free-fall acceleration.</li>  
<li>Friction between car tires and the road slowing down the car when brakes are applied.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
<h2><strong>Teaching: Forces</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>📘 The key idea is: forces cause changes in motion by creating acceleration.</strong></p>  

<h3><strong>Quantity → relationship:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Force is measured in Newtons (N).</li>  
<li>Mass is how much matter an object contains, measured in kilograms (kg).</li>  
<li>Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).</li>  
<li>Newton’s second law links these: Resultant force = mass × acceleration.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Cause → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>An unbalanced force on an object causes it to accelerate.</li>  
<li>If forces balance out, the object remains at constant speed or stationary.</li>  
<li>The bigger the force, or the smaller the mass, the greater the acceleration.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Formula → meaning:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Use F = m × a to calculate either force, mass, or acceleration.</li>  
<li>Remember units clearly to avoid mistakes (kg for mass, m/s² for acceleration, N for force).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Don’t just state the formula. Explain why a force causes acceleration, relate it to mass, and include units for full marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to confuse mass and weight. Mass is the amount of matter; weight is a force due to gravity acting on mass.</p>  
<p>👉 Do NOT say mass is measured in Newtons — mass is in kilograms; weight is measured in Newtons.</p>  
<p>👉 This matters because weight depends on the gravitational field strength, but mass does not.</p>

Step 2: Note the object's mass in kilograms.

Step 4: Calculate acceleration and include units (m/s²).

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Question:  
Why does a heavier object accelerate less than a lighter object when the same force acts on both?  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Because acceleration = force ÷ mass, when the same force acts, the larger mass means smaller acceleration.  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
<h2><strong>Exam Practice Questions</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What unit is force measured in?</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> A 5 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². Calculate the resultant force acting on it.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain why a skydiver eventually reaches terminal velocity.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
As the skydiver falls, gravity pulls them downward, causing acceleration. Air resistance acts upward, increasing as speed increases until it balances gravity. At this point, the forces are balanced, so the skydiver stops accelerating and falls at a constant terminal velocity.  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A car travelling at 20 m/s applies brakes and comes to rest in 5 seconds. The mass of the car is 1200 kg. Calculate the acceleration and the force exerted by the brakes. Explain why the car slows down.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Acceleration a = (final velocity - initial velocity) ÷ time = (0 - 20) ÷ 5 = -4 m/s² (negative shows deceleration).  
Force F = mass × acceleration = 1200 × (-4) = -4800 N (opposite to motion).  
The force from brakes applies an unbalanced force opposite to the car’s motion, causing it to slow down and eventually stop.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
<strong>Force</strong> – a push or pull acting on an object.  
<strong>Resultant force</strong> – the overall force after all forces combine.  
<strong>Balanced forces</strong> – forces that cancel out; no change in motion.  
<strong>Unbalanced forces</strong> – forces that cause acceleration.  
<strong>Friction</strong> – a contact force opposing motion between surfaces.  
<strong>Weight</strong> – force of gravity acting on an object’s mass.  
<strong>Mass</strong> – amount of matter in an object, measured in kg.  
<strong>Acceleration</strong> – change in velocity per second (m/s²).  
<strong>Newton’s second law</strong> – relationship F = m × a.  
<strong>Contact force</strong> – force requiring objects to touch.
