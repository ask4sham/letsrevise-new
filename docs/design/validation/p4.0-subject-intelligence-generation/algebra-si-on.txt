9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Algebra</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is to treat algebra as a process of carefully manipulating symbols to solve problems.</p>  
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Understand what type of algebra problem you have: simplifying expressions, expanding brackets, rearranging formulae, or solving equations.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>When simplifying, combine like terms by adding or subtracting coefficients.</li>  
<li>When expanding, multiply each term inside the brackets by the number outside.</li>  
<li>When solving equations, isolate the variable step by step by performing inverse operations.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Expression structure → applying operations → simplified expression or answer.</li>  
<li>Equation structure → isolate variable using inverse steps → value of variable.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always show each calculation step clearly and write the final answer with units or required form to gain full marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to combine unlike terms, for example writing 3x + 5 as 8x.</p>  
<p>This is wrong because the 5 is a constant, not a term with x, so it cannot be combined with 3x.</p>  
<p>Instead, only add coefficients with the same variable: 3x + 5 stays as it is.</p>  
<p>Doing this correctly earns marks for correct simplification and avoids confusion.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra questions appear often in GCSE Maths Higher Tier exams and require clear, stepwise working to gain full marks.</p>  
<p>Showing every step helps avoid careless errors and makes it easier for examiners to award marks, even if the final answer is wrong.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down each algebra step logically from the original problem, using proper notation and checking your answer by substitution.</p>  
<p>Do NOT just write the answer without showing how you got it — examiners want to see your reasoning.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Solves equation quickly but writes only the answer with no working or explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Shows some steps like moving numbers but misses proper notation or does not check the answer.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Writes each step clearly, uses algebraic notation correctly, rearranges the equation logically, and includes a check by substitution.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Shows logical method thoroughly for examiners to follow.</li>  
<li>Includes verification, demonstrating understanding and accuracy.</li>  
<li>Uses correct algebraic terminology and notation throughout.</li>  
</ul>

Simplify the expression 7y + 2 – 4y + 9 and then find the value of y if the expression equals 15.  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

First simplify: 7y – 4y + 2 + 9 = 3y + 11  
Set 3y + 11 = 15  
Subtract 11 from both sides: 3y = 4  
Divide both sides by 3: y = 4/3 or approximately 1.33  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Simplify 4a + 3a – 7.</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Expand and simplify: 3(x + 4) – 2(x – 1).</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Solve the equation 5x – 8 = 2x + 10. Show all steps.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
5x – 8 = 2x + 10  
Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x – 8 = 10  
Add 8 to both sides: 3x = 18  
Divide both sides by 3: x = 6  
Check by substitution: 5(6) – 8 = 30 – 8 = 22; 2(6) + 10 = 12 + 10 = 22; both sides equal.  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Rearrange the formula for volume of a cuboid, V = lwh, to make h the subject and then calculate h if V = 240 cm³, l = 10 cm, w = 6 cm.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
V = lwh  
Divide both sides by lw: h = V / (lw)  
Substitute values: h = 240 / (10 × 6) = 240 / 60 = 4 cm  
Write final answer with units: h = 4 cm  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Expression</strong> – A combination of terms (numbers and variables) without an equals sign.  
<strong>Equation</strong> – A mathematical statement that two expressions are equal using an equals sign.  
<strong>Variable</strong> – A symbol, usually a letter, that represents an unknown number.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To combine terms and reduce an expression to its simplest form.  
<strong>Expand</strong> – To multiply out brackets so there are no brackets left.  
<strong>Factorise</strong> – To write an expression as a product of its factors (reverse of expand).  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number in front of a variable term that shows how many times it is multiplied.  
<strong>Subject</strong> – The variable that a formula or equation is made into, to isolate it on one side.  
<strong>Inverse operation</strong> – An operation that reverses another (e.g., subtraction reverses addition).  
<strong>Substitution</strong> – Replacing variables in an expression or formula with numbers to calculate a value.
