9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Causes of WW1</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing to remember is that WW1 did not start suddenly; it was caused by a long build-up of tensions across Europe.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Long-term problems set the stage, a short-term crisis made matters worse, and one event triggered the full conflict.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Long-term causes like alliances, militarism, nationalism, and imperialism created distrust and rivalry between major powers.</li>  
<li>This increased tension (short-term causes), such as crises in the Balkans and growing arms races.</li>  
<li>Finally, the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand acted as the immediate trigger event.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Long-term: Alliance system → countries promise to protect each other → divided Europe into two armed camps → increased suspicion.</li>  
<li>Militarism → arms race → military planning (like Germany's Schlieffen Plan) → war preparedness added pressure.</li>  
<li>Nationalism → ethnic groups seeking independence (especially in Balkans) → increased violence and protests.</li>  
<li>Trigger: Assassination → Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia → declared war → alliances activated → full-scale war.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering why WW1 started, explain a cause, describe its effect or consequence, and link it clearly to how it made war likely or inevitable.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the difference between long-term causes, short-term causes, and the trigger event is essential to write high-scoring exam answers on the causes of WW1.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always explain cause → effect clearly. For example, don’t just say "alliances caused war." Instead, say "alliances caused countries to promise to defend each other, which meant that a local conflict quickly escalated into a larger war."</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The assassination caused WW1.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The assassination caused WW1 <em>because</em> it led Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggered WW1 because it provided Austria-Hungary with a reason to declare war on Serbia, which activated the system of alliances, causing countries across Europe to declare war and leading to the outbreak of a major conflict.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the trigger → effect clearly.</li>  
<li>It links the event to the wider cause of alliances activating.</li>  
<li>It uses precise historical terms and shows cause → effect chains.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why the Alliance System was a cause of WW1.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The Alliance System divided Europe into two main blocs—the Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia) and the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).  
2. These alliances promised countries would defend each other if attacked, so any conflict involving one member could involve others quickly.  
3. Therefore, when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, Russia supported Serbia, Germany supported Austria-Hungary, and the war escalated to involve all major European powers, making WW1 inevitable.  
</details>

<p><strong>Exam Practice</strong></p>  

<p>Q1 (1 mark): Name one country involved in the Alliance System before WW1.</p>  

<p>Q2 (2 marks): Explain one way in which nationalism increased tension in Europe before 1914.</p>  

<p>Q3 (3 marks): Explain why militarism was a cause of WW1.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Militarism meant countries competed to build bigger armies and navies. This made countries suspicious and fearful of each other. Therefore, when war broke out, countries were ready and willing to fight, making the conflict much more likely and more deadly.  
</details>  

<p>Q4 (4 marks): ‘The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand was the most important cause of WW1.’ How far do you agree? Explain your answer.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
On one hand, the assassination was the immediate trigger for the war because it caused Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia, activating alliances and starting the conflict. However, this was only possible because of the long-term causes like militarism, nationalism, and especially the Alliance System, which divided Europe and made war more likely. Therefore, while the assassination was important, it cannot be seen as the only or most important cause—the long-term tensions were crucial in making the assassination a spark that ignited the war. Overall, the long-term causes had the biggest impact in causing WW1.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Alliance System</strong> – Agreements between countries to defend each other in case of attack.  
<strong>Militarism</strong> – Building up armed forces to be ready for war.  
<strong>Nationalism</strong> – Devotion to your nation or ethnic group, often leading to competition or conflict.  
<strong>Imperialism</strong> – Competing for colonies and global influence.  
<strong>Long-term cause</strong> – A cause that builds up over many years.  
<strong>Short-term cause</strong> – A cause that happens close to the outbreak, increasing tension quickly.  
<strong>Trigger</strong> – An event that immediately sparks a conflict.  
<strong>Assassination</strong> – The killing of a key person, in this case, Archduke Franz Ferdinand.  
<strong>Tensions</strong> – Strained relations or distrust between countries.  
<strong>Outbreak</strong> – The start of war or conflict.
