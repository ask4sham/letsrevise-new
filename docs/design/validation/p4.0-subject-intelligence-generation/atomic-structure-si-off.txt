9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Atomic Structure</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that all atoms are made of three tiny particles: protons, neutrons, and electrons.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Protons and neutrons are packed tightly in the nucleus at the center of the atom.</li>  
<li>Electrons orbit this nucleus in layers called shells.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Protons have a positive charge, neutrons are neutral, and electrons have a negative charge.</li>  
<li>The number of protons (called the proton number) defines the element—if you change the proton number, you get a different element.</li>  
<li>The mass number is the total of protons and neutrons, telling us about the atom’s mass.</li>  
<li>Electrons arrange in shells because they occupy energy levels around the nucleus.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining atomic structure, always link the proton number to element identity and define mass number clearly as protons plus neutrons. Use particle names and write about their charges and positions.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying "mass number is protons plus electrons" instead of "protons plus neutrons."</p>  
<p>Neutrons add to the mass but have no charge, so they are counted with protons for mass number, not electrons.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is: "Mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus."</p>  
<p>This earns marks because it uses correct particle names and correctly links them to mass.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Determining proton number and mass number from element symbols

Step 1:  
Look at the smaller number on the element symbol (usually at the bottom) — this is the proton number.

↓

Step 2:  
This proton number shows the number of protons in the nucleus and identifies the element.

↓

Step 3:  
Look at the bigger number on the element symbol (usually on top) — this is the mass number.

↓

Step 4:  
Calculate the number of neutrons by subtracting the proton number from the mass number (mass number – proton number = neutrons).

Exam link:  
This method helps you answer exam questions on isotopes and atomic structure.

16 — EXAM TIP

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding atomic structure is essential to explain an element’s identity and how atoms react, which forms the foundation of all chemistry exams.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
The isotope Carbon-14 has 6 protons and 8 neutrons. What is its proton number and mass number?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Proton number = 6 (the number of protons)  
Mass number = 6 (protons) + 8 (neutrons) = 14  
</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<p>👉 The key to mastering atomic structure is remembering that protons define the element, neutrons add mass but no charge, and electrons orbit negatively charged around the nucleus.</p>  
<p>👉 Together, these particles explain both the identity and mass of atoms, laying the groundwork for understanding chemistry.</p>

<h2><strong>💡 Key Insight</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Atoms are defined by their number of protons (proton number), surrounded by electrons in shells, with neutrons adding mass alongside protons; mass number is always protons plus neutrons.</strong></p>  
<p>👉 This simple rule helps you identify elements and isotopes quickly in exams.</p>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> How many electrons does an atom of oxygen-16 have?</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Explain why the proton number is important for identifying an element.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A chlorine atom has 17 protons and 18 neutrons. Write the mass number and describe the atomic structure.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Explain the differences between protons, neutrons, and electrons in terms of charge, mass, and position within the atom.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  
<p><strong>Q1:</strong> 8 electrons (because oxygen has 8 protons, atoms are neutral so electrons equal protons).</p>  
<p><strong>Q2:</strong> The proton number tells us how many protons are in the nucleus, which defines the element’s identity because no two elements have the same proton number.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3:</strong> Mass number = 17 + 18 = 35. The atom has a nucleus containing 17 protons and 18 neutrons, with 17 electrons orbiting in shells around the nucleus.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4:</strong> Protons have a positive charge and are found in the nucleus; neutrons have no charge and are also in the nucleus; electrons have a negative charge and orbit the nucleus in shells. Protons and neutrons have similar mass, but electrons are much lighter.</p>  
</details>  

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>Atoms contain protons, neutrons, and electrons.</li>  
<li>Protons and neutrons make up the nucleus.</li>  
<li>Electrons orbit in shells outside the nucleus.</li>  
<li>The proton number defines which element an atom is.</li>  
<li>The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons.</li>  
<li>Atoms of the same element can have different numbers of neutrons (isotopes).</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: mass number = protons + neutrons, and the proton number = element identity.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always explain why proton number matters and use correct particle names.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Atom</strong> – The smallest part of an element retaining its chemical properties.</p>  
<p><strong>Proton</strong> – A positively charged particle in the nucleus.</p>  
<p><strong>Neutron</strong> – A neutral particle in the nucleus that adds mass.</p>  
<p><strong>Electron</strong> – A negatively charged particle orbiting the nucleus.</p>  
<p><strong>Proton number</strong> – The number of protons, defining the element.</p>  
<p><strong>Mass number</strong> – The total number of protons and neutrons.</p>  
<p><strong>Isotope</strong> – Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.</p>  
<p><strong>Nucleus</strong> – The center of the atom containing protons and neutrons.</p>  
<p><strong>Shell</strong> – Energy levels where electrons orbit the nucleus.</p>  
<p><strong>Charge</strong> – Property of particles that can be positive, negative, or neutral.</p>
