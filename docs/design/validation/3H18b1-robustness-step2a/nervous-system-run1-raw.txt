9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system uses specialised cells called neurones to send rapid electrical messages.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect specific stimuli (such as light, heat, pressure).</li>  
<li>These receptors send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the impulses and decides on a response.</li>  
<li>Impulses then travel along motor neurones to effectors - muscles or glands.</li>  
<li>Effectors produce a movement or secretion as the response.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Neurones have dendrites to receive impulses and an axon to send impulses rapidly.</li>  
<li>Myelin sheath surrounds axons, insulating them and speeding up impulse transmission by forcing impulses to jump between nodes (saltatory conduction).</li>  
<li>Synapses are small gaps where impulses cross chemically to the next neurone.</li>  
<li>The CNS integrates the sensory information via relay neurones, coordinating precise responses.</li>  
<li>This system allows fast, controlled reactions essential for survival.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “messages are sent.” Instead say: “The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses to the CNS, which processes the information and sends impulses via a motor neurone to an effector, producing a response.” Use terms like receptor, sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, effector, and response.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing electrical impulses from leaking out.  
2. Impulses jump quickly between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier. This is called saltatory conduction.  
3. This jumping reduces the distance impulses must travel continuously along the axon.  
4. As a result, impulse transmission is much faster, allowing rapid responses essential for survival.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects a specific stimulus.</p>  
<p><strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.</p>  
<p><strong>Response</strong> – The reaction by effectors to a stimulus.</p>  
<p><strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.</p>  
<p><strong>Relay neurone</strong> – A neurone inside the CNS that connects sensory and motor neurones.</p>  
<p><strong>Motor neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.</p>  
<p><strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap where neurotransmitters carry impulses between neurones.</p>  
<p><strong>CNS</strong> – Central Nervous System, made up of the brain and spinal cord.</p>  
<p><strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral Nervous System, consisting of sensory and motor neurones.</p>  
<p><strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response to a stimulus.</p>
