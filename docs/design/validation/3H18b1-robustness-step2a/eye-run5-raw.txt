5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → the cornea refracts (bends) the light → the iris controls the pupil size affecting light intensity → the lens changes shape (accommodation) to fine-focus light → light rays converge on the retina → photoreceptors detect light and send signals to the brain.</strong></p>  
<p><strong>Step-by-step reasoning chain:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters through the transparent cornea, which refracts the light to change its direction.</li>  
<li>The iris adjusts the size of the pupil to control how much light enters based on brightness.</li>  
<li>The lens fine-tunes focus by changing its curvature using ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</li>  
<li>Light rays converge so that they meet precisely on the retina at the back of the eye.</li>  
<li>The retina’s photoreceptor cells detect the light and convert it into electrical signals.</li>  
<li>These signals are sent to the brain, where they are processed as visual images.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Right, let’s break this down. The eye is designed to focus light sharply so your brain gets a clear picture of the world.</p>  
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The main parts of the eye work together by bending light and adjusting focus to form a clear image on the retina.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light first passes through the cornea, a curved transparent layer. This bends (refracts) light towards the eye’s interior.</li>  
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating how much light enters, protecting the eye in bright conditions or letting in more light when it’s dark.</li>  
<li>The lens sits just behind the pupil and changes shape to focus light from objects at different distances; this is accommodation.</li>  
<li>Ciliary muscles contract or relax to change the tension on suspensory ligaments, which alters the lens shape.</li>  
<li>The light is focused onto the retina, which contains photoreceptor cells that convert light into electrical signals sent to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The cornea’s curvature and transparency allow it to refract light into the eye precisely.</li>  
<li>The ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments adjust the lens thickness: contracting muscles slacken ligaments and make the lens thicker for near objects; relaxing muscles tighten ligaments, thinning the lens for distant objects.</li>  
<li>Accommodation ensures light rays converge exactly on the retina, producing a sharp image.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "the lens focuses light." Say "the lens changes shape caused by ciliary muscles contracting or relaxing, which changes the tension on suspensory ligaments, allowing light from different distances to focus sharply on the retina."</p>  
<p>👉 Use the terms <strong>cornea</strong>, <strong>lens</strong>, <strong>retina</strong>, <strong>accommodation</strong>, <strong>ciliary muscles</strong>, and <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> explicitly.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying "The lens just focuses the light" without explaining how.</p>  
<p>This loses marks because the examiner expects a clear explanation of the process of accommodation: that ciliary muscles contract or relax to change tension in suspensory ligaments, which changes lens shape.</p>  
<p>👉 Another error is saying "The retina sees the image" — this is incorrect because the retina detects light but the brain interprets the image.</p>  
<p>Explain these mechanisms with precise terms to earn full marks.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding how the eye focuses light and the role of accommodation is key for answering exam questions on vision and eye defects.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>When explaining eye focusing or accommodation, use a clear sequence like "ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments loosen → lens thickens → light refracts more" to show cause and effect.</p>  
<p>Always use correct terminology like <strong>cornea</strong>, <strong>lens</strong>, <strong>retina</strong>, <strong>accommodation</strong>. Avoid vague phrases like "the eye zooms in."</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light using the lens.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light because the lens changes shape.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ciliary muscles contract, which slackens the suspensory ligaments, making the lens thicker so it refracts light more and focuses the image on the retina for near objects.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It gives a clear step-by-step cause → effect chain.</li>  
<li>It uses precise terminology required by the mark scheme.</li>  
<li>It explains *how* the focusing mechanism works, not just what happens.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – The transparent outer layer that bends light into the eye.  
<strong>Iris</strong> – The coloured part that controls the size of the pupil.  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening that allows light to enter the eye.  
<strong>Lens</strong> – The structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina.  
<strong>Retina</strong> – The light-sensitive layer where images are formed.  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape.  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that hold the lens and adjust its tension.  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus light.  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different materials.  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Cells in the retina that detect light and colour.
