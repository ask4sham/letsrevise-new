9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis helps keep internal conditions stable so enzymes and cells work efficiently.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors sense a change (stimulus), such as temperature rising or blood sugar changing.</li>
<li>These receptors send information to a coordination centre like the brain or pancreas.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes and decides the response needed.</li>
<li>Effectors then act to counteract the change, restoring optimum conditions.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect temperature changes → information sent to hypothalamus (brain area) → activates sweat glands to release sweat → evaporation cools the body → temperature moves back to optimum.</li>
<li>Pancreas acts as a coordination centre for blood glucose → releases insulin → liver cells remove glucose from blood → blood sugar decreases to optimum level.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always include receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to optimum. Use precise terms like “thermoreceptors detect a stimulus” and explain how effectors cause the response (e. g.</p>
<p>, sweating cools by evaporation).</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how negative feedback helps control blood glucose concentration after eating a sugary meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Blood glucose rises after eating sugar (stimulus).
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect the high blood glucose.
3. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases the hormone insulin.
4. Insulin causes liver and muscle cells (effectors) to absorb glucose from the blood, reducing blood glucose to the optimum level.
This negative feedback stops the glucose level from staying too high.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal model answers</summary>
Q1 Answer: Receptors detect changes in internal conditions such as temperature or glucose concentration.
Q2 Answer: The hypothalamus is a coordination centre that processes information from receptors and sends signals to effectors to restore balance.
Q3 Answer: When core temperature rises, thermoreceptors detect this change and send signals to the hypothalamus. The hypothalamus activates sweat glands, which produce sweat. When sweat evaporates, it cools the body down and returns temperature to optimum.
Q4 Answer: Negative feedback works by detecting a stimulus that moves conditions away from optimum. For blood glucose, after a meal glucose rises (stimulus). Receptors in the pancreas detect this and the pancreas releases insulin (coordination centre role). Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose (effector action), reducing blood glucose to optimum. The response then stops once balance is restored.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The maintenance of a stable internal environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – Any change in the environment that is detected.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells that detect stimuli and send signals.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information and directs responses (e.g., brain, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that produce a response.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action that restores optimum conditions.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or ideal conditions for enzymes and cells to work.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process where a change triggers a response that reverses the change.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released by glands to regulate body functions.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Proteins that speed up chemical reactions and work best in specific conditions.
