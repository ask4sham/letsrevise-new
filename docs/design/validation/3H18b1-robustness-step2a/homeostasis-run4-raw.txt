4 — WHY IT MATTERS

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Maintaining stable conditions is essential because enzymes only work efficiently within narrow optimum conditions, so cells and the whole organism can function properly.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis relies on a communication chain: receptors detect stimuli (changes), coordination centres process this information, and effectors generate responses to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>  
<p>Look at this step-by-step chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Something changes inside or outside the body (stimulus).</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this change (e.g., temperature receptors in the skin).</li>  
<li>The coordination centre (brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) processes the information.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) act to reverse the change.</li>  
<li>The internal condition returns towards the optimum.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis is a continuous process that keeps your body's internal conditions steady despite external or internal changes.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus disrupts the internal balance (for example, blood glucose level rises after a meal).</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus and send signals to a coordination centre (like the pancreas).</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes this information and triggers effectors (such as cells in the pancreas releasing insulin).</li>  
<li>Effectors act to restore conditions to their optimum (insulin causes cells to absorb glucose, lowering blood glucose).</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are sensitive cells adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., temperature receptors in skin have nerve endings that respond to heat).</li>  
<li>The brain or pancreas acts as a coordination centre because it receives signals and decides how to respond.</li>  
<li>Effectors like muscles (which can contract) or glands (which can release hormones) bring about a response that reverses the change. For example, muscles contracting to shiver generate heat.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering, do NOT just say "the body sweats" or "the blood glucose decreases." Instead, say: "Thermoreceptors detect a rise in temperature → the hypothalamus (coordination centre) activates sweat glands (effectors) → sweat evaporates, removing heat → temperature returns to optimum."</p>  
<p>Use this cause → process → effect language for full marks.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how the body responds when core temperature rises during exercise. (5 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Core body temperature rises above the optimum (37°C).  
2. Thermoreceptors in the skin detect this increased temperature (stimulus).  
3. The hypothalamus in the brain acts as the coordination centre and receives this information.  
4. Sweat glands (effectors) are activated to release sweat onto the skin surface.  
5. Sweat evaporates, removing heat energy and cooling the body, so the core temperature returns to the optimum.  

This sequence shows negative feedback restoring conditions.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The regulation of internal conditions in the body to keep them steady and optimal.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change detected inside or outside the body.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or organ that detects a stimulus.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – The part of the body (like the brain or pancreas) that processes information and coordinates a response.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that act to restore optimum conditions.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to reverse a change.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or ideal condition for cells and enzymes to function.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control system where the response reverses a change to maintain stability.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – A chemical messenger often released by glands to coordinate homeostasis.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – A protein that speeds up biological reactions and works best under optimum conditions.
