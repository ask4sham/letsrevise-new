9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The nervous system is essential for rapid communication and control in the body.</p>

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The nervous system detects stimuli and sends electrical signals to coordinate a fast response.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus is detected by a <strong>receptor</strong>, such as a temperature receptor detecting heat.</li>  
<li>The receptor converts the stimulus into an electrical impulse that travels along a <strong>sensory neurone</strong>.</li>  
<li>The impulse reaches the <strong>central nervous system (CNS)</strong>, which includes the brain and spinal cord, where it is processed.</li>  
<li>The CNS sends an impulse along a <strong>motor neurone</strong> to an <strong>effector</strong> such as a muscle or gland.</li>  
<li>The effector produces a physiological <strong>response</strong>, for example, muscle contraction to remove your hand from danger.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised cells that detect specific stimuli, allowing accurate detection of environmental changes.</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites to receive stimuli and carry impulses quickly towards the CNS.</li>  
<li><strong>Relay neurones</strong> in the CNS connect sensory neurones to motor neurones, coordinating complex responses.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> have long axons to carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors, allowing rapid responses.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> are muscles or glands that produce the response—muscles contract to move limbs, glands secrete hormones or enzymes.</li>  
<li><strong>Myelin sheath</strong> around neurones insulates them, speeding up impulse transmission via saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining, always use the chain stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response to show understanding of how the nervous system works.</p>  
<p>👉 Use the correct terms for neurone types and direction of impulses ("sensory neurone carries impulses <strong>to</strong> the CNS" and "motor neurone carries impulses <strong>away</strong> from the CNS").</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like "nerves send messages" without explaining electrical impulses and neurone types.</p>

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing loss of electrical signal.  
2. Electrical impulses jump between gaps in the myelin sheath called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This jumping reduces transmission time compared to continuous transmission along unmyelinated neurones.  
4. Faster impulse transmission allows quicker responses to stimuli, which improves survival.  
</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>The nervous system coordinates fast responses using electrical impulses between receptors, neurones, and effectors.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information and controls body responses.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect stimuli and sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles or glands).</li>  
<li>Neurones have dendrites, axons, cell bodies, and myelin sheaths that speed signal transmission.</li>  
<li>Synapses are gaps where chemical signals pass impulses between neurones.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialised cell that detects a stimulus.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The reaction produced by an effector following stimulation.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone that transmits impulses within the CNS.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The small gap between two neurones where impulses pass chemically.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, includes brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, made of nerves outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to a stimulus.
