3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the process by which living organisms regulate their internal conditions to maintain a stable, optimum environment for cells and enzymes, despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Maintaining stable internal conditions is crucial because enzymes and cells only work properly within narrow limits, so without homeostasis, life processes cannot function effectively.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works by a simple pathway: a change in conditions is detected by receptors → this information is sent to a coordination centre → the coordination centre sends instructions to effectors that act to reverse the change and restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>  

<p>Look at this full reasoning chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus causes internal conditions to change away from the optimum.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this change (stimulus).</li>  
<li>The receptors send information to a coordination centre (like the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas).</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes the information and sends instructions to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) respond to restore the internal conditions.</li>  
<li>The condition returns to the optimum level, and the response is switched off.</li>  
</ol>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Body temperature control:</strong> Thermoreceptors detect temperature changes → hypothalamus (coordination centre) initiates sweating or shivering (effectors) to maintain around 37°C.</li>  
<li><strong>Blood glucose regulation:</strong> Receptors detect blood sugar changes → pancreas releases insulin or glucagon (effectors) to return glucose to optimum levels.</li>  
<li><strong>Water balance:</strong> Osmoreceptors detect blood water levels → brain signals pituitary gland to release ADH (effector) altering how kidneys conserve or release water.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions steady so cells function properly.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A change inside or outside the body acts as a stimulus detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>The receptors send this information to a coordination centre, which processes it and sends signals to effectors.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are cells or groups of cells specialised to detect specific changes, such as temperature or glucose concentration.</li>  
<li><strong>Coordination centres</strong> (like the brain or pancreas) interpret the information and send signals quickly or via hormones.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) carry out a response that reverses the initial change, restoring optimum conditions.</li>  
<li>For example, thermoreceptors in the skin → hypothalamus → sweat glands produce sweat → evaporation cools the body → temperature returns to optimum.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In your answers, don’t just say “the body gets too hot.” Say “thermoreceptors detect a rise in temperature and send signals to the hypothalamus (coordination centre), which activates sweat glands (effectors) to produce sweat. Evaporation of sweat removes heat, cooling the body back to optimum.” Use the terms receptor, coordination centre, effector clearly and explain how each step causes the next.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE

Question:  
Explain how the blood glucose concentration is controlled when you eat a sugary meal. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Eating sugary food causes blood glucose levels to rise (stimulus).  
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this increase.  
3. The pancreas acts as a coordination centre and releases insulin (effector response).  
4. Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose from the blood, lowering blood glucose back to the optimum.  

</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Homeostasis is a life-supporting system that relies on detecting changes (stimuli) by specialised cells (receptors), which send messages to centres that process and coordinate a response. The effectors carry out actions to restore optimum conditions.</p>  

<p>📘 This means your body constantly monitors and adjusts conditions like temperature and blood glucose to keep cells healthy and enzymes working efficiently.</p>

21 — FINAL MEMORY RULE

<h2><strong>💡 Key Insight</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Homeostasis is a negative feedback system:</strong> when a condition moves away from its optimum, receptors detect the change and send signals to a coordination centre, which activates effectors to reverse the change, restoring the condition to optimum — then the response stops.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The process of maintaining stable internal conditions.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in internal or external conditions detected by the body.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or group of cells that detect a stimulus.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – A part of the body like the brain that processes information from receptors.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that carry out responses to restore conditions.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to return conditions to optimum.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or most favourable internal condition for enzyme and cell function.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A system where a change triggers a response that reverses it, restoring balance.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released into the blood to bring about a response.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological catalyst, sensitive to conditions like temperature and pH.
