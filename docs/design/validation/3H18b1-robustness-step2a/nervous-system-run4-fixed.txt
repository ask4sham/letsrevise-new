9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses through specialised cells called neurones.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus (change in environment) is detected by receptors specialised to that stimulus.</li>
<li>Receptors generate electrical impulses that travel along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>
<li>The CNS processes this information and sends impulses along motor neurones to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce a response, like contracting or secreting.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors:</strong> Specialised cells with structures adapted to detect specific stimuli, e.g. light, pressure, temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Sensory neurones:</strong> Have dendrites to receive impulses and long axons to transmit impulses fast to the CNS.</li>
<li><strong>CNS:</strong> Contains relay neurones that connect sensory and motor neurones to process impulses quickly.</li>
<li><strong>Motor neurones:</strong> Carry impulses from CNS to effectors, with long axons and myelin sheath for fast conduction.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors:</strong> Muscles contract or glands secrete substances to create a reaction or response.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 When you answer questions, say exactly which neurone carries impulses where: sensory neurone carries impulses to the CNS and motor neurone carries impulses away from the CNS to effectors.</p>
<p>Explain the response as contraction or secretion.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying "nerves send messages to the brain" without explaining what type of messages or the direction of impulse travel.</p>
<p>Students often confuse sensory and motor neurones or just write "messages" without specifying electrical impulses and the path taken.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Examiners expect a clear explanation of impulses traveling as electrical signals along sensory neurones <em>to</em> the CNS and motor neurones <em>away</em> to effectors.</p>
<p>A better way to say it is: "Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS, and motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to muscles or glands."</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing electrical signal loss.
2. Impulses jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier in a process called saltatory conduction.
3. This jumping speeds up transmission compared to continuous conduction.
4. Faster impulses allow quick responses to stimuli, improving survival chances.
</details>

24— SUMMARY
Paste into: Text (concept)
<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>The nervous system uses electrical impulses for rapid communication.</li>
<li>The CNS consists of the brain and spinal cord; the PNS is made of nerves connecting receptors and effectors.</li>
<li>Receptors detect stimuli and send impulses via sensory neurones to the CNS.</li>
<li>The CNS processes the impulses and sends signals through motor neurones to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles and glands) carry out the response through contraction or secretion.</li>
<li>Neurones’ features—like dendrites, axons, and myelin sheath—are adapted to efficiently transmit electrical impulses.</li>
<li>Synapses are gaps between neurones where chemical transmission occurs.</li>
</ul>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – a cell that detects a stimulus in the environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – a change in the environment that can be detected.
<strong>Response</strong> – an action or change in behaviour caused by a stimulus.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – nerve cell carrying impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – nerve cell in the CNS connecting sensory neurones to motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – nerve cell carrying impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – a gap between two neurones where impulses are transmitted chemically.
<strong>CNS</strong> – central nervous system, including brain and spinal cord.
<strong>PNS</strong> – peripheral nervous system, nerves outside the CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – muscles or glands that respond to nerve impulses with contraction or secretion.
