<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works through this pathway: a stimulus changes a condition → receptors detect the change → coordination centres process this information → effectors produce a response → the condition returns towards the optimum.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Cause → effect chain:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A change (stimulus) occurs in an internal condition.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus.</li>
<li>The coordination centre (e.g., brain, spinal cord, pancreas) processes the signal.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) generate a response to correct the change.</li>
<li>The internal condition moves back to its optimum level.</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps the internal environment steady so our cells and enzymes function properly.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus is any change that moves a factor outside its optimum range, like a rise in temperature.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus and send information to a coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre interprets this info and signals effectors to produce a response.</li>
<li>The response works to reverse the change, bringing conditions back to the optimum—this is called negative feedback.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect heat → send nerve impulses to the hypothalamus → which triggers sweat glands → sweat evaporates → heat energy is lost → core temperature drops towards optimum.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the body sweats.</p>
<p>” A stronger answer would say: “Thermoreceptors detect a rise in core temperature → the hypothalamus (coordination centre) activates sweat glands (effectors) → sweat evaporates, removing heat energy → the core temperature returns to the optimum.</p>
<p>”</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the pancreas helps to control blood glucose when it rises after eating a meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The receptors in the pancreas detect a rise in blood glucose concentration.
2. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin.
3. Insulin (effector) causes body cells to take in more glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen.
4. This lowers blood glucose levels back towards the optimum.
</details>

19— SYNTHESIS
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)
<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Homeostasis is a continuous process where receptors detect a stimulus, coordination centres process this information, and effectors act to restore the internal environment to optimum conditions. This keeps enzymes working well and cells healthy.</p>
<p>👉 The negative feedback loop ensures that once conditions are corrected, the response stops, preventing overcorrection.</p>
<p>👉 Examples like temperature control and blood glucose regulation show how different systems coordinate to maintain balance.</p>

<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

Q2: Sweat glands and muscles (for example, muscles causing shivering).
Q3: The receptors in the pancreas detect the rise in blood glucose. The pancreas, acting as the coordination centre, releases insulin. Insulin causes cells to take in more glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen, lowering blood glucose towards optimum.
Q4: When water levels fall below optimum, osmoreceptors detect this and signal the brain. The brain signals the kidneys (effectors) to reduce urine production, conserving water. When water levels rise, the kidneys produce more urine. This negative feedback keeps water balance steady.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions for proper function.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment detected by receptors.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells or organs that detect a stimulus.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – The brain, spinal cord, or pancreas that process information from receptors.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that respond to restore optimal conditions.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to return conditions to optimum.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal conditions for enzyme and cell activity.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process that reverses a change to return conditions to the optimum.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released into the blood to regulate conditions like blood glucose.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Proteins that speed up reactions and require specific conditions to work well.
