9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The nervous system enables fast communication throughout the body using specialised cells called neurones.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses through electrical impulses travelling along neurones.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus such as heat or pressure is detected by receptors, which are cells specialised to respond to specific stimuli.</li>  
<li>The receptor converts the stimulus into an electrical impulse.</li>  
<li>This impulse travels along the sensory neurone to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>  
<li>Within the CNS, relay neurones process the information and send impulses along motor neurones to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles contract, glands secrete chemicals) produce a response to deal with the stimulus.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors in the eye, temperature receptors in skin).</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites to carry impulses from receptors to the CNS quickly.</li>  
<li><strong>Relay neurones</strong> connect sensory and motor neurones within the CNS, enabling processing of information.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> carry impulses from CNS to effectors; their long axons enable rapid communication to muscles or glands.</li>  
<li><strong>Myelin sheath</strong> wraps around axons of neurones, insulating them and increasing the speed of electrical impulse transmission.</li>  
<li><strong>Synapses</strong> are gaps between neurones where neurotransmitters carry impulses chemically from one neurone to the next, enabling complex signalling.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In exam answers, be specific about the direction of impulses: sensory neurone carries impulses <em>to</em> the CNS and motor neurone carries impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors.</p>  
<p>👉 Use clear cause → effect chains like: receptor detects stimulus → impulse sent by sensory neurone → CNS processes signal → motor neurone sends impulse → muscle contracts → response happens.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<h2><strong>Common mistake: confusing sensory and motor neurones</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake students make is saying "motor neurones carry impulses to the CNS" instead of sensory neurones.</p>  

<p><strong>Correction:</strong> Sensory neurones carry impulses from receptors <strong>to</strong> the CNS, while motor neurones carry impulses <strong>away</strong> from the CNS to effectors.</p>  

<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> This mix-up loses marks because the direction of impulse transmission is crucial in the nervous system.</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system's structure and function is key for explaining rapid responses to danger or changes in the environment, which is a core part of biology and often tested in GCSE exams.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When exam questions ask "describe" or "explain," focus on cause and effect using correct terminology, e.g., "sensory neurone carries impulses to the CNS," rather than vague terms like "messages."</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid writing incomplete answers such as just naming parts without explaining what they do.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Nerves send messages from the body to the brain.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS because they detect stimuli.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors <em>to</em> the CNS, where relay neurones pass the impulses to motor neurones. Motor neurones then carry impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors, which respond by contracting muscles or secreting glands.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Clear use of correct directional terms (‘to’ and ‘away from’ the CNS).</li>  
<li>Includes all types of neurones involved in the pathway.</li>  
<li>Shows cause → process → effect for the marks.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why impulses travel faster along myelinated neurones than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. The myelin sheath acts as an electrical insulator around the axon.  
2. This insulation allows impulses to jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier.  
3. Jumping from node to node (saltatory conduction) reduces the time taken for impulses to travel.  
4. As a result, impulses travel much faster along myelinated neurones, allowing quicker responses.  
</details>

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>The nervous system coordinates rapid responses using electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information, while the PNS (nerves) carry impulses to and from the CNS.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect stimuli; sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles and glands), which produce responses.</li>  
<li>Neurones have dendrites (receive impulse), cell body, and an axon (carries impulse), often insulated by a myelin sheath to increase speed.</li>  
<li>Synapses between neurones allow impulses to cross chemically, enabling complex communication.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: clear pathways and fast impulses are what make nervous coordination so effective in survival.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects a stimulus and generates an electrical impulse.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by an effector after receiving an impulse.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – A neurone within the CNS that connects sensory and motor neurones.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between two neurones where chemical transmission occurs.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, consisting of the brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, made up of all neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to impulses from motor neurones.
