5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Central Nervous System (CNS) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>Watch this cause → effect chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>The receptor sends an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone to the CNS (brain or spinal cord).</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the information and sends impulses out along motor neurones.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses to effectors (muscles or glands).</li>  
<li>Effectors produce a response, like moving a muscle or secreting a hormone.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system sends fast electrical signals along specialised cells called neurones to coordinate rapid responses.</p>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect specific stimuli such as light, heat, or touch because they are specialised cells adapted to sense those changes.</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites to carry impulses from receptors towards the CNS, allowing fast transmission of information to the brain or spinal cord.</li>  
<li><strong>CNS (brain and spinal cord)</strong> acts as the control centre, processing information and making decisions about how to respond.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors, triggering a response; their long axons effectively link CNS to the rest of the body.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) respond by contracting or secreting chemicals, producing the necessary response to the stimulus.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus (e.g., heat) activates receptors.</li>  
<li>This causes receptors to generate an electrical impulse transmitted along sensory neurones.</li>  
<li>The CNS receives and processes impulses rapidly.</li>  
<li>Impulses travel along motor neurones to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors act (muscle contraction or gland secretion), producing a quick, coordinated response.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Adaptation → advantage:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Myelin sheath around axons insulates and speeds up impulses by allowing them to jump between gaps (nodes of Ranvier) — increasing reaction speed.</li>  
<li>Dendrites increase the area to receive signals from other neurones, enhancing communication efficiency.</li>  
<li>The branching of neurones lets the nervous system handle many stimuli and coordinate complex responses quickly.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, say “Sensory neurone carries impulses <em>to</em> the CNS” and “Motor neurone carries impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors.”</p>  
<p>👉 Always explain how the structure suits the function and what happens next, showing the cause → effect chain clearly.</p>  
<p>👉 Don’t just say “nerves send messages” — be specific about electrical impulses, neurone types, and effectors involved.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit electrical impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon and prevents loss of electrical impulses.  
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the sheath called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This reduces the time taken for the impulse to travel along the neurone.  
4. As a result, effectors respond faster to stimuli which helps the organism react quickly to danger.  

</details>

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>The nervous system controls rapid responses using electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord; the PNS includes all neurones outside the CNS.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect stimuli; sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS processes information; motor neurones carry impulses to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce the response by contracting or secreting.</li>  
<li>Neurones have dendrites (receive signals), an axon (carries impulses away), and often a myelin sheath (speeds transmission).</li>  
<li>Synapses between neurones allow impulses to cross chemically.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: Describe not just what happens but how the structure enables the function — linking each step carefully.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, explain direction of impulses and name neurone types for full marks.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or group of cells that detects stimuli.<br />  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.<br />  
<strong>Response</strong> – A reaction by the body to a stimulus.<br />  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses within the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.<br />  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – A small gap between neurones where chemicals carry impulses.<br />  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central Nervous System; includes brain and spinal cord.<br />  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral Nervous System; all neurones outside the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to nervous impulses.<br />
