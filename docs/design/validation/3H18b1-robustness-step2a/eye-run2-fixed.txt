5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → passes through the lens which adjusts focus → light is focused sharply onto the retina where photoreceptors detect it.</strong></p>
<p>Let's break down the full cause → effect chain:</p>
<ol>
<li>Light enters the eye through the transparent <strong>cornea</strong>.</li>
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays, helping to direct them.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> controls how much light enters by adjusting the size of the <strong>pupil</strong>.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> fine-tunes the focus by changing its shape via <strong>ciliary muscles</strong> and <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, a process called <strong>accommodation</strong>.</li>
<li>Light rays converge precisely on the <strong>retina</strong>, which contains photoreceptor cells.</li>
<li>The photoreceptors detect the light and send electrical impulses to the brain to create an image.</li>
</ol>

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key thing to remember is how different parts of the eye work together to focus light correctly.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye works by bending light via the cornea and lens so it focuses on the retina where light-sensitive cells detect the image.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea, which bends (refracts) the light to help focus it.</li>
<li>The iris controls pupil size to regulate light intensity entering the eye.</li>
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focus by changing shape — thicker for near objects, thinner for distant ones. This is called accommodation.</li>
<li>The photoreceptors in the retina detect the focused light and send signals to the brain to create an image.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent, adapted to refract light efficiently to start focusing.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> alters pupil diameter to control light entry, protecting the retina from damage and improving image clarity.</li>
<li>The <strong>ciliary muscles</strong> contract or relax to change the lens's thickness via the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, adapting focus for different distances.</li>
<li>A focused image forms on the <strong>retina</strong>, whose photoreceptors convert light into electrical impulses.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In your answer, do not just say “the lens focuses light. ” Instead, say “light is refracted by the cornea and lens; accommodation happens when ciliary muscles change lens shape to focus images onto the retina.</p>
<p>” This shows cause → effect and uses correct terms.</p>

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say "the eye zooms in" or "the lens focuses light" without explaining how.</p>
<p>The better way is to say "the ciliary muscles contract, causing suspensory ligaments to slacken, making the lens thicker to refract light more for near focus."</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent, curved front part of the eye that refracts light entering the eye.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part of the eye controlling pupil size to regulate light entry.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening controlled by the iris where light enters the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – Transparent structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptor cells detecting light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape during accommodation.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres connecting ciliary muscles to the lens, adjusting lens tension and shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes through different substances, essential for focusing in the eye.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cells in the retina that convert light into electrical signals.
