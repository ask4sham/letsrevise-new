3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions in the body to maintain optimum conditions for cells and enzymes despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Maintaining stable internal conditions is vital because enzymes, which control all chemical reactions in cells, only work efficiently within narrow conditions such as temperature and pH.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works by a cycle: Receptors detect a change or stimulus → Coordination centres process this information → Effectors carry out a response to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>  

<p><strong>Look at this chain carefully:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus causes a change in an internal condition.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect the stimulus.</li>  
<li>The coordination centre receives and processes this information.</li>  
<li>Effectors produce a response to reverse the change.</li>  
<li>The internal condition returns towards its optimum level.</li>  
<li>The response stops once optimum is restored (negative feedback).</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions constant so enzymes and cells work best.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Internal conditions can change due to external factors (like temperature) or internal activities (like exercise).</li>  
<li>A stimulus, such as a rise in temperature or blood sugar, is detected by specialized receptors.</li>  
<li>The information is sent to a coordination centre, often part of the brain or pancreas, which processes it.</li>  
<li>The coordination centre triggers effectors to produce the correct response, restoring conditions to optimum.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are cells sensitive to specific stimuli, so they detect changes accurately.</li>  
<li>Coordination centres receive and interpret signals, deciding what action is needed.</li>  
<li>Effectors are muscles or glands; muscles can contract to generate heat, and glands can secrete hormones or sweat to adjust conditions.</li>  
<li>This system works continuously using negative feedback to maintain stable conditions.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering exam questions, use the full chain: name the receptor detecting the stimulus, name the coordination centre processing it, identify the effector responding, explain the response mechanism, and show how the change restores the optimum condition.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to write answers like "The brain makes you sweat" without specifying the hypothalamus as the coordination centre, or simply saying "Sweating cools you down" without explaining how evaporation removes heat.</p>  

<p>👉 A better way to say it is: "Thermoreceptors detect a rise in body temperature; the hypothalamus in the brain acts as the coordination centre and activates sweat glands. Sweat evaporates from the skin, removing heat energy and cooling the body."</p>  

<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Marks are lost when students name parts vaguely or miss explaining the process that restores optimum conditions.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Homeostasis keeps the body’s internal environment stable so enzymes can work efficiently. Without this, chemical reactions would slow down or stop, damaging cells and tissues.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering homeostasis questions, always use the key terms: receptor, coordination centre, effector, stimulus, and response. Explain clearly how each part causes the next step, and always connect your answer to how the internal condition returns to optimum.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The body sweats when hot.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Receptors detect the temperature rise, so the body sweats to cool down.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Thermoreceptors detect the rise in core temperature and send information to the hypothalamus (coordination centre). The hypothalamus activates sweat glands (effectors) which produce sweat. Sweat evaporates from the skin surface, removing heat energy and returning core temperature to the optimum.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It names specific structures (thermoreceptors, hypothalamus, sweat glands).</li>  
<li>It describes the exact process (evaporation removing heat energy).</li>  
<li>It explains the cause → effect → final outcome chain required for top marks.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how the body responds to a rise in blood glucose concentration after a meal. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Receptors in the pancreas detect the rise in blood glucose concentration (stimulus).  
2. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin (response).  
3. Insulin causes body cells to take in glucose and the liver to convert glucose to glycogen (effectors respond).  
4. Blood glucose concentration falls back towards the optimum level.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal body conditions steady and stable.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change detected inside or outside the body.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects a stimulus.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Part of the body (brain, spinal cord, pancreas) that processes information from receptors.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to restore balance.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken to reverse a change.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal level or condition for cells and enzymes.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – The mechanism that reverses a change to restore optimum conditions.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – A chemical messenger often released by glands to control body processes.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – A protein that speeds up chemical reactions in cells, sensitive to conditions like temperature and pH.
