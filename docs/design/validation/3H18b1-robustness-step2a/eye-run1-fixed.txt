9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p><strong>👉 The key idea is the eye’s structures work as a team to focus light onto the retina for clear vision.</strong></p>
<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cornea:</strong> A curved, transparent layer at the front of the eye that refracts (bends) incoming light to start focusing it.</li>
<li><strong>Iris and pupil:</strong> The iris is a muscle controlling the pupil size—dilates or contracts to regulate light entering.</li>
<li><strong>Lens:</strong> A flexible, transparent structure behind the pupil that changes shape to fine-tune light focus.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments:</strong> These control the lens shape during accommodation by contracting or relaxing.</li>
<li><strong>Retina:</strong> Contains photoreceptor cells that detect focused light and convert it into nerve impulses.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light passes through the cornea → light rays bend to start focusing → iris adjusts pupil size based on light intensity → ciliary muscles change tension in suspensory ligaments → lens changes shape (thicker for near objects, thinner for distant) → light focuses sharply on retina → photoreceptor cells detect the image → electrical signals are sent to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Explain how accommodation works (near vision):</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract.</li>
<li>Suspensory ligaments slacken.</li>
<li>Lens becomes thicker and more curved.</li>
<li>Light refracts more strongly.</li>
<li>Near object forms a focused image on the retina.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Explain how accommodation works (distant vision):</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>Ciliary muscles relax.</li>
<li>Suspensory ligaments pull tight.</li>
<li>Lens becomes thinner and less curved.</li>
<li>Light refracts less.</li>
<li>Distant object forms a focused image on the retina.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "The lens focuses light" — explain that the ciliary muscles contract or relax to change suspensory ligament tension, causing the lens shape to change, which adjusts refraction and focuses the image.</p>
<p>👉 Use terms like <strong>accommodation</strong>, <strong>ciliary muscles</strong>, and <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> consistently.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say <em>"The lens just focuses light."</em></p>
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> Explain that the lens changes shape due to the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments, which changes how much the lens bends (refracts) light.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Without this explanation, you miss marks for showing understanding of <strong>accommodation</strong> and the mechanism behind focusing.</p>
<p>👉 Another error is to say <em>"The retina sees the image."</em></p>
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> The retina <strong>detects light</strong> and sends signals; the brain interprets the image.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> This distinction is important for full marks because it shows you know the retina’s specific role.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent front layer of the eye that refracts light.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Muscle that controls pupil size and light intensity entering the eye.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The hole in the iris through which light enters the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – A flexible, transparent structure that changes shape to focus light.
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer at the back of the eye with photoreceptor cells that detect light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that control the tension of suspensory ligaments to change lens shape.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Ligaments that hold the lens and change its tension during accommodation.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing the lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light rays when they pass through different materials.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-detecting cells in the retina that convert light into electrical signals.
