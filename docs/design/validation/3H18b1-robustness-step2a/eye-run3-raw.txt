9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 The eye focuses light onto the retina using several specialized structures working together.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light enters through the cornea, which refracts the light to help focus it.</li>  
<li>The iris adjusts pupil size to control how much light enters according to brightness.</li>  
<li>The lens fine-tunes focus by changing shape through accommodation.</li>  
<li>Light rays then converge onto the retina, where photoreceptors detect them.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent → it refracts (bends) incoming light rays → causing them to start converging towards the retina.</li>  
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> is a muscle controlling the pupil size → it adjusts light intake → protecting the retina and improving image clarity in bright or dim light.</li>  
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> control <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> → when ciliary muscles contract, suspensory ligaments slacken → the <strong>lens</strong> becomes thicker → it refracts light more for near vision (accommodation).</li>  
<li>When ciliary muscles relax, suspensory ligaments pull tight → lens becomes thinner → light is refracted less for distant vision.</li>  
<li>Focused light hits <strong>photoreceptors</strong> in retina → they create electrical signals → signals travel to brain → brain forms the image.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say 'light is focused.' Say how the cornea and lens refract light, and explain accommodation using ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments changing lens shape.</p>  
<p>👉 Use a clear cause → effect → outcome chain: e.g., "Ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker → light bends more → image focuses on retina."</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying "The lens focuses light" without explaining how it changes.</p>  
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> The lens focuses light by changing its shape through the action of ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</p>  
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Marks are lost if you name parts without describing the process of accommodation or how the lens adapts.</p>  

<p>👉 Students also often say "The retina sees the image."</p>  
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> The retina contains photoreceptors that detect light and convert it into electrical signals sent to the brain; the brain actually forms the image.</p>  
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Using “detects light” rather than “sees image” shows better understanding and meets AQA expected wording.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Focusing light precisely on the retina is essential for sharp vision; mistakes cause blurred images and vision problems.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>In exams, when asked to explain how the eye focuses light or how accommodation works, always use the correct sequence of events using these terms:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>Light is refracted by the <strong>cornea</strong> and <strong>lens</strong>.</li>  
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> control tension in the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, changing the lens shape.</li>  
<li><strong>Accommodation</strong> means changing lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>Use the phrase "light is refracted" rather than "light is focused" alone. Avoid vague words like "zoom" or "eye zooms in."</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light by changing the lens.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The lens focuses light because it changes shape when muscles contract.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ciliary muscles contract, causing the suspensory ligaments to slacken, so the lens becomes thicker and more curved, increasing refraction of light rays to focus a near object image clearly on the retina.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses specific terms (ciliary muscles, suspensory ligaments, lens shape).</li>  
<li>It explains the cause → process → effect clearly.</li>  
<li>It directly answers how accommodation focuses on near objects.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent front part of the eye that bends incoming light rays.  
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured muscle that controls the size of the pupil.  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening controlled by the iris that allows light into the eye.  
<strong>Lens</strong> – Transparent flexible structure that changes shape to focus light.  
<strong>Retina</strong> – Light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors.  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that change tension on the lens via suspensory ligaments.  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that hold the lens and change its shape during accommodation.  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes through different substances.  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – A cell in the retina that detects light and converts it into electrical signals.
