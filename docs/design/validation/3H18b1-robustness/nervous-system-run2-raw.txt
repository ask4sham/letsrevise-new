5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS (brain and spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>This chain explains how information flows from detecting a change to producing a response in the body.</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Stimulus is detected by a receptor</li>  
<li>Impulses travel along sensory neurones to the CNS</li>  
<li>Relay neurones in the CNS process information</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from CNS to effectors</li>  
<li>Effectors produce a response (muscle contracts or gland secretes)</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let’s break down how the nervous system is built and works.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The nervous system is adapted to detect changes quickly and send fast electrical messages to produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect a specific stimulus (like light, heat, or pressure).</li>  
<li>Electrical impulses then travel along neurones to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS acts as a coordination centre, processing these signals.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors such as muscles or glands.</li>  
<li>The effectors respond by contracting muscles or releasing chemicals.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are specialised cells with structures sensitive to specific stimuli → This allows them to detect small changes accurately.</li>  
<li>Sensory neurones have long dendrites to carry impulses towards the CNS → This ensures signals reach the processing centre quickly.</li>  
<li>Relay neurones in the CNS connect sensory and motor neurones → They integrate and process information.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones have long axons insulated by myelin sheath → This speeds up the transmission of electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>Effectors respond quickly to motor neurone signals → Muscles contract or glands secrete as appropriate.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always use the full pathway in answers: <em>stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response</em> and explain how structure helps function.</p>  
<p>👉 Use terms like “sensory neurone carries impulses to CNS” and “motor neurone carries impulses away from CNS” to be precise.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like “messages travel” without explaining the electrical nature and pathway.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system’s pathway and structure is essential because exam questions test your ability to explain the flow of information in a precise sequence.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always follow the exact pathway: <em>stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response</em>. Use correct terms like 'sensory neurone carries impulses to CNS' and 'motor neurone carries impulses away from CNS'. Avoid vague words like "messages."</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>

<ul>  
<li>It uses specific terms: sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, effector.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects stimuli.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that can be detected.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The reaction of the body to a stimulus.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone within the CNS connecting sensory and motor neurones.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone carrying impulses from CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – Gap between neurones where impulses are transmitted chemically.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, made of brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, all neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that respond to impulses.
