<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions to maintain optimum conditions for cells and enzymes, despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>The homeostasis control pathway always follows this sequence:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>A <strong>stimulus</strong> changes an internal condition.</li>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this stimulus.</li>  
<li>The information is sent to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> (like the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) that processes it.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) produce a response to counteract the change.</li>  
<li>The internal condition returns towards the <strong>optimum</strong> level.</li>  
</ol>  
<p>For example: <br />  
Stimulus → Receptor → Coordination centre → Effector → Response → Return to optimum</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis involves detecting changes in the body's internal environment and correcting them to keep conditions steady.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A <strong>stimulus</strong> causes an internal condition to deviate from its <strong>optimum</strong>.</li>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this stimulus and send signals to a <strong>coordination centre</strong>.</li>  
<li>The <strong>coordination centre</strong> processes the information and sends instructions to <strong>effectors</strong>.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> respond to reverse the stimulus and bring conditions back to the optimum.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised cells that detect specific stimuli, such as temperature receptors in the skin that respond to heat.</li>  
<li>The <strong>brain</strong> or pancreas serve as <strong>coordination centres</strong> because they can process incoming information and make decisions.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> include muscles that contract (like shivering muscles to generate heat) or glands that secrete hormones or sweat.</li>  
<li>This cycle creates a <strong>negative feedback loop</strong>, meaning the response reduces the original stimulus to restore balance.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don't just say "The body cools down." Instead, say “Thermoreceptors detect the temperature rise, the hypothalamus (coordination centre) sends signals to sweat glands (effectors), sweat evaporates removing heat, so the core temperature returns to the optimum.”</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)  

Question:  
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose levels rise after eating a meal. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Blood glucose (stimulus) rises above the optimum level.  
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this change.  
3. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases insulin (effector hormone) into the blood.  
4. Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose and the liver to store it as glycogen, lowering blood glucose back to the optimum.  

This is a negative feedback response because the effect reduces the original stimulus.  
</details>

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the name of the process that maintains stable internal conditions?</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Name two organs that can act as coordination centres in homeostasis.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain how negative feedback works to regulate blood glucose concentration.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Negative feedback detects when blood glucose is too high or low (stimulus). Receptors in the pancreas detect this change and send information to the pancreas (coordination centre). The pancreas releases insulin or glucagon (effectors) to lower or raise blood glucose, returning it to optimum.  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Describe how the body responds when core temperature falls below optimum.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
When core temperature falls below optimum, thermoreceptors in the skin detect the drop and send signals to the hypothalamus (coordination centre). The hypothalamus activates muscles to shiver (effectors), generating heat through muscle contractions. Blood vessels near the skin constrict to reduce heat loss by less blood flow. This response raises core temperature back towards optimum.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal conditions stable despite external or internal changes.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or group of cells that detect a stimulus.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Organ that processes information and sends instructions (e.g., brain, pancreas).  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that responds to restore optimum conditions.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to reverse the change.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or ideal conditions for enzyme and cell function.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – Process where a change causes responses that reverse that change and restore balance.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released by glands to change body processes (e.g., insulin).  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological catalysts that speed up reactions and work best under optimum conditions.
