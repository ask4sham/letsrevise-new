9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key thing to remember about the eye is that it’s all about focusing light onto the retina so your brain can create clear images.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye uses different structures to control and focus light precisely.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first passes through the cornea, which is transparent and curved to start bending the light rays — this is refraction.</li>
<li>The iris adjusts the size of the pupil to control how much light enters, protecting the eye in bright conditions or allowing more light in the dark.</li>
<li>The lens then fine-tunes the focus — it changes thickness through a process called accommodation, controlled by ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</li>
<li>Finally, light is focused onto the retina, which contains photoreceptors that detect light and send information via the optic nerve to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The cornea’s curved shape causes light rays to refract → this bends the light toward the pupil → ensuring they head through the central opening;</li>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract or relax → suspensory ligaments either loosen or tighten → lens shape changes (thicker or thinner) → light is refracted more or less → images from near or far objects focus sharply on the retina;</li>
<li>Photoreceptors in the retina detect light intensity and colour → convert light into electrical signals → sent to the brain for image processing.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In your exam answers, explain the full cause → process → effect chain and use terms like refraction , accommodation , and name the structures involved (cornea, lens, ciliary muscles).</p>
<p>Don’t just say "the lens focuses the light"—explain how accommodation changes lens shape.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistakes about the eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say "the lens focuses the light" without explaining how it changes shape or mentioning accommodation.</p>
<p>Students often confuse the pupil and iris. The pupil is the hole light passes through, while the iris is the coloured part that controls pupil size.</p>
<p>Another error is saying "the retina sees the image". The retina only detects light; the brain interprets the image.</p>
<h3><strong>Exam link:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 If you omit how the lens changes shape or misuse terms, you lose marks for explanation and accuracy. Use precise terms and link structure → function clearly.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the eye focuses on a near object. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The ciliary muscles contract to reduce tension on the suspensory ligaments.
2. The suspensory ligaments slacken, allowing the lens to become thicker and more curved.
3. A thicker lens refracts light rays more strongly.
4. This stronger refraction focuses the light rays from the near object onto the retina, forming a clear image.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent outer layer that refracts (bends) incoming light.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part of the eye that controls the size of the pupil.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Hole in the iris that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure that changes shape to focus light onto the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – Light-sensitive layer containing photoreceptor cells that detect light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape during accommodation.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Ligaments that pull on the lens to change its tension and shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different materials, like the cornea and lens.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Cells in the retina that detect light and convert it into electrical signals.
