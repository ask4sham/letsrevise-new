5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → the cornea refracts (bends) the light → then the lens fine-tunes the focus → the focused light then falls onto the retina → photoreceptors detect the light and send signals to the brain.</strong></p>  
<p>Look at this cause → effect chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the transparent cornea</li>  
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays towards the lens</li>  
<li>The iris controls how much light enters by adjusting the pupil size</li>  
<li>The lens changes shape to focus light correctly (accommodation)</li>  
<li>Light rays are focused to form a sharp image on the retina</li>  
<li>Photoreceptor cells in the retina detect light and send impulses to the brain</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the eye focuses light to form a clear image on the retina so the brain can process what we see.</p>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is the clear, curved front surface of the eye and is adapted to refract light as it enters.</li>  
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> controls the size of the <strong>pupil</strong>, adjusting how much light passes into the eye.</li>  
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> is flexible and changes shape to focus light on the retina — this process is called <strong>accommodation</strong>.</li>  
<li>The <strong>retina</strong> contains photoreceptor cells that detect light and send electrical signals to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light passes through the cornea and is refracted → this begins focusing the light rays toward the retina.</li>  
<li>The iris adjusts pupil size → controlling light intensity and improving vision in different light levels.</li>  
<li>For near objects, ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker and more curved → light refracts more strongly → focused light hits the retina sharply.</li>  
<li>For distant objects, ciliary muscles relax → suspensory ligaments tighten → lens flattens → less refraction of light → distant object image is focused on the retina.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the lens focuses light.” Say “light is refracted by the cornea and lens, and accommodation occurs when ciliary muscles change the lens shape so light is correctly focused on the retina.”</p>  
<p>👉 Use the correct terms like “ciliary muscles,” “suspensory ligaments,” and “accommodation” to show deeper understanding.</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exams, understanding how the eye focuses light and the role of accommodation helps you answer questions on vision clearly and fully.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When a question asks to “explain” or “describe” the eye’s focusing process, always include the sequence: cornea refraction → pupil size control by iris → lens accommodation → image on retina.</p>  
<p>👉 Use accurate terms like “ciliary muscles contract causing suspensory ligaments to slacken,” not vague phrases like “lens zooms in.”</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid saying “the retina sees the image.” Instead say “the retina detects light and sends signals to the brain.”</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>

<p>The lens focuses light because it changes shape.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Light enters the eye and is refracted by the cornea and lens. The ciliary muscles contract, causing the suspensory ligaments to slacken, which makes the lens thicker. This increases refraction so the near object is focused sharply onto the retina.</p>

2. The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays to start focusing.

This chain shows how the eye forms a clear, focused image for vision.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent front part of the eye that refracts incoming light.  
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part that controls pupil size to regulate light entry.  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening that allows light into the eye.  
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure that changes shape to focus light.  
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer of photoreceptors detecting light at the back of the eye.  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape.  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Connect ciliary muscles to lens to adjust tension.  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes through different materials.  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cells in the retina that detect light stimuli.
