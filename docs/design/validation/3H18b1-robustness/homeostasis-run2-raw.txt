3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the process by which living organisms regulate their internal conditions to keep them stable and at optimum levels despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stable internal conditions are vital because enzymes and cells only work efficiently within narrow temperature, pH, and chemical ranges needed for survival.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works through a clear sequence: <em>Receptors detect a stimulus → Coordination centres process the information → Effectors produce a response to restore optimum conditions.</em></strong></p>  
<p><strong>Step-by-step cause → effect chain:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus (change in internal condition) occurs.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this change.</li>  
<li>Coordination centres such as the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas receive and interpret signals from receptors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) are activated to bring conditions back to the set point.</li>  
<li>The response from effectors reverses the change, restoring the optimum internal environment.</li>  
</ol>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Body temperature regulation:</strong> Thermoreceptors detect changes, hypothalamus coordinates, sweat glands or muscles respond.</li>  
<li><strong>Blood glucose control:</strong> Pancreas detects glucose levels, releases insulin or glucagon, effectors cause cells or liver to absorb or release glucose.</li>  
<li><strong>Water balance control:</strong> Osmoreceptors detect water levels, brain coordinates, kidneys adjust water reabsorption.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal environments stable so enzymes and cells function properly.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A change, or stimulus, moves a condition (like temperature) away from its optimum.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this change and send a message to a coordination centre.</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes this information and sends signals to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors act to reverse the change, restoring the optimum level.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect temperature changes → the hypothalamus receives and processes this in the brain → sweat glands (effectors) produce sweat → evaporation cools the body → body temperature returns to optimum.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining homeostasis, always use the key terms: receptor, coordination centre, effector, stimulus, and response.</p>  
<p>👉 Describe the cause → effect flow, for example, "Thermoreceptors detect increased temperature → hypothalamus coordinates response → sweat glands produce sweat → evaporation lowers temperature → temperature returns to optimum."</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague terms like "the brain makes you sweat"—name the hypothalamus instead.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE

Question:  
Explain how the body controls blood glucose levels when they rise after a meal. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. A rise in blood glucose acts as the stimulus →  
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this change →  
3. The pancreas acts as coordination centre by releasing insulin →  
4. Insulin causes body cells to absorb glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen →  
5. Blood glucose levels decrease back to the optimum level.  
</details>

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions to maintain optimum function.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect stimuli (changes inside the body).</li>  
<li>Coordination centres process the information and send instructions.</li>  
<li>Effectors carry out responses that restore balance.</li>  
<li>Examples include temperature, blood glucose, and water balance control.</li>  
<li>This system protects enzymes and cells, keeping the body functioning efficiently.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: The body uses a continuous feedback loop to maintain stability despite external or internal changes.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always link your explanation with the full sequence: stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → optimum.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal conditions stable and balanced.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Detects changes or stimuli.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes receptor signals and directs effectors.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Produces a response to restore balance.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken to correct a change.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal condition for cells and enzymes.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process where the response reverses the change.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger used in coordination.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that catalyses reactions, sensitive to internal conditions.
