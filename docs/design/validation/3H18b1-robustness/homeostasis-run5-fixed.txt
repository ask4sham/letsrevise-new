5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works by a clear, step-by-step process: Receptors detect a change (stimulus) → Coordination centres process this information → Effectors bring about a response to return conditions to the optimum.</strong></p>
<p>Look at the full chain: </p>
<ol>
<li>A stimulus causes a change from the set point.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this change.</li>
<li>The coordination centre (e.g., brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) processes the information.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) respond to reverse the change.</li>
<li>Conditions return towards the optimum set point.</li>
<li>The response stops once conditions are back to optimum (negative feedback).</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis controls important internal conditions to keep them stable.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus causes an internal condition to move away from the optimum level, such as temperature rising or blood glucose increasing.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this change and send information to a coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes the information and instructs effectors to produce a response.</li>
<li>The effectors act to reverse the change, bringing the condition back towards the optimum.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised cells sensitive to specific stimuli; for example, thermoreceptors detect temperature changes because they have proteins sensitive to heat.</li>
<li>The <strong>coordination centres</strong> like the brain or pancreas integrate signals, ensuring the response is appropriate.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> such as muscles or glands produce a response: muscles can generate heat by shivering, glands produce hormones or sweat.</li>
<li>This process causes the restoration of internal conditions → cells and enzymes work efficiently again → survival is maintained.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, say “Receptors detect the stimulus and send information to the coordination centre, which activates effectors that produce a response to return conditions to the optimum.”</p>
<p>Avoid vague phrases like “the body fixes the problem,” or “the brain makes you sweat” without naming structures.</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Homeostasis keeps your internal environment stable, which is crucial because enzymes, which control chemical reactions, only work within narrow conditions like specific temperatures and pH. Disturbances can stop enzymes working, which harms cells and survival.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When the exam question says “Explain” or “Describe,” always include the full homeostasis pathway: stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to optimum.</p>
<p>👉 Use specific terms, e.g., “thermoreceptors detect temperature change,” not just “the body feels hot.”</p>
<p>👉 Avoid vague statements like “the brain tells muscles,” you must name the actual centre, e.g., hypothalamus.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>
<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The body gets too hot so it sweats.</p>

<p>Thermoreceptors detect the temperature rise, then the brain tells sweat glands to produce sweat.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Thermoreceptors detect the rise in core temperature and send a signal to the hypothalamus, which activates sweat glands. The sweat evaporates, removing heat and returning the temperature to the optimum.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose levels rise after eating. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. When blood glucose levels rise, receptors in the pancreas detect this change.
2. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin as a hormone.
3. Insulin causes liver and muscle cells (effectors) to absorb glucose and store it as glycogen.
4. This response lowers blood glucose back towards the optimum level.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The process of keeping internal conditions stable.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that can be detected.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or group of cells that detects a stimulus.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – A part of the body that processes information (e.g., brain, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action that restores conditions to optimum.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best condition for cells and enzymes to work.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control mechanism where the response reduces the original change.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – A chemical messenger released by glands affecting target cells.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – A protein that controls chemical reactions, sensitive to conditions.
