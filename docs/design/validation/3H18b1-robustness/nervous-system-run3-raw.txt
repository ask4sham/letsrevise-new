5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Central nervous system (CNS) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>👉 A clear cause → effect chain illustrating how information is detected, processed, and acted upon.</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus (change in environment) is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>Receptors send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the information and sends impulses along motor neurones.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones stimulate effectors (muscles or glands).</li>  
<li>Effectors produce a response, such as muscle contraction or gland secretion.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The nervous system is like a fast messaging network that helps the body respond rapidly to changes.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Neurones are specialised cells adapted to carry electrical impulses quickly across the body.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Stimuli like light, heat, or pain are detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>Receptors send impulses along sensory neurones into the CNS.</li>  
<li>Within the CNS, relay neurones connect sensory input to motor output.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses to effectors, causing muscles to contract or glands to secrete.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> have sensitive membranes that respond only to specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors respond to light).</li>  
<li><strong>Neurones</strong> have long axons insulated by myelin sheath which speeds up electrical impulse transmission by saltatory conduction (impulses jump between nodes).</li>  
<li>Dendrites receive signals from other neurones or receptors, enabling effective communication.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) coordinates information processing and decision making.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> are muscles that contract to produce movement or glands that secrete hormones or enzymes as responses.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always say impulses travel along <strong>neurones</strong> as electrical signals and specify the direction: sensory neurone carries impulses <em>to</em> CNS; motor neurone carries impulses <em>away</em> from CNS to effectors.</p>  
<p>👉 Don't just say "messages" or "signals" without naming neurones and CNS parts.</p>  
<p>👉 Link the function of myelin sheath to the advantage of faster transmission.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<h2><strong>Common mistakes</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying "nerves send messages" without explaining that these are electrical impulses traveling along specific neurones like sensory or motor neurones.</p>  
<p>A better way to say it is: "Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS."</p>  
<p>👉 Another common error is confusing the direction: students might say motor neurones carry impulses to the CNS instead of away from it.</p>  
<p>Exam link: This loses marks because the specific neurone pathways and their direction are crucial for describing nervous system function clearly.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system’s structure and function is essential because exam questions require precise knowledge of pathways, specific brain regions, and how different parts work together to maintain homeostasis and react to stimuli.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>Use command words like "describe," "explain," or "compare" by making cause → effect chains. For example, when explaining nerve impulse transmission, always include the direction along neurones and link structure to function.</p>  
<p>Avoid vague phrases like "messages travel" without naming neurones or explaining how impulses speed up with myelin.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The nerves send messages from the receptors to the muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone carries impulses from the receptors to the CNS, then the motor neurone carries impulses to the muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone carries an electrical impulse from the receptor to the CNS, where relay neurones process the signal, then the motor neurone carries the impulse away from the CNS to the effector muscle, which contracts to produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses precise terminology: sensory, relay, and motor neurones.</li>  
<li>It explains direction of impulses and the roles of each neurone.</li>  
<li>It includes the cause → process → effect sequence expected by examiners.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)  

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. Myelin sheath insulates the axon to prevent electrical signal loss.  
2. The electrical impulses jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This jumping speeds up the transmission compared to impulses traveling continuously.  
4. As a result, impulses reach effectors faster, allowing quicker responses.  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Name the part of the nervous system that processes information from sensory neurones.</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Describe the role of the motor neurone in the nervous system.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain how the myelin sheath increases the speed of nerve impulses.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
The myelin sheath insulates the axon so electrical impulses do not get lost. The impulses jump between nodes of Ranvier (gaps) rather than traveling continuously, which speeds up transmission of the impulse. This allows faster responses from effectors.  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Describe the pathway of a reflex action when a person touches something sharp and explain why reflexes are important.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
When the skin touches a sharp object, receptors detect the stimulus and send impulses along sensory neurones to the spinal cord (CNS). Relay neurones in the spinal cord process this and send impulses along motor neurones to muscles (effectors), which contract to pull the hand away. Reflexes are important because they provide a rapid, automatic response to danger without waiting for conscious brain processing, protecting the body from injury.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects changes in the environment (stimuli).  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that can be detected by a receptor.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by effectors due to a stimulus.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – The neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone within the CNS that connects sensory and motor neurones.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – The neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where chemical signals pass impulses.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system made up of the brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system; neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to a motor neurone impulse.
