9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing to remember is how each part of the eye contributes to focusing light and forming a clear image.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light accurately so that the retina receives a sharp image for the brain to interpret.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light first enters the eye through the **cornea**, which is curved and transparent.</li>  
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) light rays towards the lens.</li>  
<li>The **iris** adjusts the size of the **pupil** to control how much light enters, protecting the retina from damage when it's too bright.</li>  
<li>The **ciliary muscles** and **suspensory ligaments** then change the shape of the **lens** in a process called **accommodation**.</li>  
<li>The lens focuses light rays precisely on the **retina**, where photoreceptor cells detect the image.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The **cornea** is curved and transparent, allowing it to refract light efficiently on entry.</li>  
<li>The **lens** can change thickness: when ciliary muscles contract, suspensory ligaments slacken, making the lens thicker for near objects—this increases refraction to focus light exactly on the retina.</li>  
<li>When looking at distant objects, ciliary muscles relax, suspensory ligaments tighten, and the lens becomes thinner, bending light less strongly to focus on the retina.</li>  
<li>The **retina** contains photoreceptors (rods and cones) that detect the light and send impulses along the optic nerve to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "the lens focuses light." Instead say: "Light is refracted by the cornea and the lens. The ciliary muscles change the lens shape to focus light accurately onto the retina, a process called accommodation."</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying “The lens zooms in or out to focus.”</p>  
<p>This is incorrect because the lens does not zoom like a camera—it changes shape due to the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments altering tension to bend light properly. Saying "zoom" loses marks because it’s imprecise.</p>  
<p>Instead, say: “The ciliary muscles contract or relax to change the lens's thickness for accommodation, which focuses light sharply on the retina.”</p>  

<p>👉 Another common error is stating “The retina sees the image.”</p>  
<p>This is wrong because the retina does not see; it contains photoreceptors that detect light and then send electrical signals to the brain, which actually interprets the image.</p>  
<p>Explanation is needed to show understanding of the retina's role in detection, not vision perception.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – transparent front part that refracts light entering the eye  
<strong>Iris</strong> – coloured part controlling pupil size and light entry  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – hole in the iris allowing light into the eye  
<strong>Lens</strong> – changes shape to focus light on the retina  
<strong>Retina</strong> – layer at the back with cells that detect light  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – muscles that control lens shape for accommodation  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – connect ciliary muscles to lens and adjust its tension  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – process of changing lens shape to focus on objects at different distances  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – bending of light rays when they pass between materials  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – light-detecting cell in the retina (rods and cones)
