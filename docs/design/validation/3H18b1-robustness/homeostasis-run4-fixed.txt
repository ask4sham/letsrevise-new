SHORT SUMMARY FIELD:
Homeostasis regulates internal body conditions through a negative feedback system involving receptors, coordination centres like the brain, and effectors such as muscles and glands.

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works through a sequence:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Receptors detect a stimulus (change in internal conditions).</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes this information (e.g., brain, spinal cord, pancreas).</li>
<li>Effectors respond to restore conditions to optimum (muscles or glands).</li>
<li>This restores the condition and switches off the response (negative feedback).</li>
</ol>

Stimulus moves condition away from optimum → receptors detect change → coordination centre processes → effectors act → condition returns to optimum → response switched off.</p>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps your internal environment stable, vital for survival.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A change called a <strong>stimulus</strong> moves a condition away from its ideal level.</li>
<li>Special cells called <strong>receptors</strong> detect this change.</li>
<li>The information is sent to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> like the brain.</li>
<li>The coordination centre triggers <strong>effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) to respond.</li>
<li>The <strong>effectors</strong> act to reverse the change and bring the condition back to the <strong>optimum</strong>.</li>
<li>This stops the response once balance is restored — this is called <strong>negative feedback</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are adapted to detect specific changes (e.g., temperature receptors in the skin).</li>
<li>The brain is a coordination centre because it can process diverse information.</li>
<li>Effectors like sweat glands produce sweat which evaporates, removing heat and cooling the body.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Explain how negative feedback works:</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>A condition moves away from the optimum.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus and send signals.</li>
<li>The coordination centre decides the action needed.</li>
<li>Effectors respond to reverse the change.</li>
<li>The condition returns to the optimum level.</li>
<li>The response is turned off to prevent overcorrection.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always use the precise terms <strong>receptor</strong>, <strong>coordination centre</strong>, <strong>effector</strong>, <strong>stimulus</strong>, and <strong>response</strong>. Explain the effect of the effector’s action on returning conditions to optimum.</p>
<p>👉 Don’t just say “the body does this”. Name structures and the outcome clearly.</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Understanding homeostasis is crucial for biology exams because many questions ask you to explain how the body keeps conditions stable through negative feedback.</p>
<p>Recognising the roles of receptors, coordination centres, and effectors helps you write clear, detailed answers that examiners reward.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>When a question asks “Explain how…” or “Describe the role of…”, use the sequence:</p>
<ul>
<li>State the stimulus moves the condition from the optimum.</li>
<li>Mention specific receptors detect the change.</li>
<li>Name the coordination centre involved (e.g., hypothalamus, pancreas).</li>
<li>Describe effector actions clearly (e.g., muscles contract/shivering, glands secrete hormones/sweat).</li>
<li>Explain how these actions restore optimum conditions.</li>
</ul>
<p>Always say <strong>negative feedback</strong> to show the response stops when balance is restored.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>
<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The body sweats when hot to cool down.</p>
<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The body sweats because sweat glands produce sweat which cools the skin by evaporation.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The thermoreceptors in the skin detect the rise in core temperature as a stimulus. The hypothalamus in the brain acts as the coordination centre and activates sweat glands, which act as effectors. Sweat evaporates from the skin surface, removing heat energy and returning the temperature to its optimum. This negative feedback stops further sweating once balance is restored.</p>
<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>It uses precise terminology—receptors, coordination centre, effectors, stimulus, response.</li>
<li>Clearly explains the cause → effect chain.</li>
<li>Mentions the feedback system stopping the response, crucial for full marks.</li>
</ul>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body controls blood glucose when you eat a meal high in sugar. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. After eating, blood glucose rises above its optimum level (stimulus).
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this increase in blood glucose concentration.
3. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases the hormone insulin (effector).
4. Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen, lowering blood glucose back to optimum. Negative feedback stops insulin release once balanced.
</details>

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>
<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the term for the ideal conditions that homeostasis maintains?</p>
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Name the structures involved in homeostasis when body temperature rises.</p>
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain why it is important for the body to keep blood glucose concentration constant.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
Keeping blood glucose constant is important because enzymes work best at optimum concentrations, and very high or low glucose affects cell function.
</details>
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Describe the process of negative feedback in homeostasis using the example of body temperature regulation when it gets too cold.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
When body temperature drops below optimum (stimulus), thermoreceptors detect the decrease and send this information to the hypothalamus (coordination centre). The hypothalamus activates effectors such as muscles to contract and shiver. Shivering generates heat, warming the body to restore temperature to optimum. Once the temperature returns to normal, the response is switched off by negative feedback.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal conditions stable despite changes.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal environment.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Detects changes (stimuli) in the body.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information and sends out instructions (e.g., brain, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response.
<strong>Response</strong> – Action taken to restore balance.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or ideal conditions for enzyme and cell function.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A system that stops a response once conditions return to normal.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger released by glands in homeostasis.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological catalyst that works best at steady conditions.
