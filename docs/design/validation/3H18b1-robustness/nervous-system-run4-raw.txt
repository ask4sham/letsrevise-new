5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS (brain and spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector (muscle or gland) → Response</strong></p>  
<p>Step-by-step explanation:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus is detected by receptors in the body.</li>  
<li>The receptor sends an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone.</li>  
<li>The impulse reaches the CNS where it is processed.</li>  
<li>A motor neurone carries the impulse away from the CNS.</li>  
<li>The impulse reaches an effector which produces the response (muscle contracts or gland secretes).</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system is organised to detect information, process it quickly, and coordinate actions using special cells called neurones.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect a specific stimulus (like light, heat, sound, or pressure).</li>  
<li>They convert this into an electrical impulse that travels along sensory neurones.</li>  
<li>The impulse reaches the CNS — either the brain or spinal cord — where the message is processed.</li>  
<li>The CNS sends impulses along motor neurones to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce the response — for example, contracting a muscle or releasing a hormone.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors for vision), allowing accurate sensing.</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites and axons to transmit impulses rapidly to the CNS.</li>  
<li><strong>Relay neurones</strong> in the CNS connect sensory neurones to motor neurones, allowing quick processing.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> carry impulses from the CNS to effectors, causing responses.</li>  
<li>The <strong>myelin sheath</strong> around axons speeds up electrical impulse transmission by insulating the neurone.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining nervous system responses, always use the pathway: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response.</p>  
<p>👉 Name the type of neurone and describe what each part does — examiners expect clear cause → effect chains, not just lists.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague terms like “messages” without stating electrical impulses or direction.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is writing “nerves send messages to the brain” without explaining that these are electrical impulses along sensory neurones.</p>  
<p>👉 <strong>Why this loses marks:</strong> Saying “messages” is too vague; examiners want electrical impulse transmission named explicitly, including direction from receptor to CNS.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way is: “The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses from the receptor to the CNS.”</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system is vital for explaining how organisms detect and respond to changes, a key topic in the AQA GCSE Biology exams.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always describe the full pathway using correct terminology: “stimulus,” “receptor,” “sensory neurone,” “CNS,” “motor neurone,” “effector,” and “response.”</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague terms like “messages” or “signals” without reference to electrical impulses and directions.</p>

<p>Receptors send electrical impulses to the brain, which sends messages to muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Receptors detect a stimulus and send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS, where relay neurones process the information and send impulses through motor neurones to effectors, causing muscles to contract and produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It correctly names the pathway using key terms.</li>

</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how impulses are transmitted faster in a myelinated neurone than an unmyelinated neurone (4 marks).

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing loss of electrical signal.  
2. Impulses jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This reduces the length of membrane the impulse needs to travel along.  
4. Therefore, impulses travel faster, allowing quicker responses from effectors.  

</details>

<h2><strong>Synthesis: Brain regions and their functions</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The brain is a key part of the CNS with specialised areas that control different body functions.</p>  

<ul>  
<li><strong>Cerebral cortex:</strong> Controls consciousness, intelligence, memory, and language.</li>  
<li><strong>Cerebellum:</strong> Coordinates muscle activity and balance.</li>  
<li><strong>Medulla:</strong> Controls unconscious activities like heartbeat and breathing.</li>  
</ul>  

<p>This organisation allows the brain to manage both voluntary actions such as thinking and involuntary actions like breathing without conscious effort.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialised cell that detects changes in the environment (stimuli).  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by effectors after nervous system processing.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Connects sensory neurones to motor neurones within the CNS.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where impulses cross using chemicals.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system consisting of the brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system made up of neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that responds to impulses from motor neurones.
