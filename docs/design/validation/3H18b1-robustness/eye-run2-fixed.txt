<p>👉 <strong>The pathway of light focusing in the eye:</strong></p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> contracting or relaxing.</li>
<li><strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> tightening or slackening.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens changing shape</strong> to focus light correctly.</li>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key thing to understand is how the structures of the eye work together to focus light sharply onto the retina.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye focuses light by bending it first at the cornea, then fine-tuning focus with the lens, so the retina receives a clear image.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light rays enter through the cornea, which is curved to bend (refract) light sharply.</li>
<li>The iris controls pupil size to regulate how much light gets in, protecting the retina from damage.</li>
<li>The lens sits just behind the pupil and changes shape to focus on near or far objects — this is accommodation.</li>
<li>The retina receives the focused light; its photoreceptors convert light into electrical signals for the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Cornea is transparent and curved → it refracts light rays towards the centre of the eye → begins focusing the image.</li>
<li>Iris muscles contract or relax → pupil size changes → controls the amount of light entering the eye → protects retina and improves vision clarity.</li>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract or relax → suspensory ligaments tighten or slacken → lens changes shape → changes the focal length → light is focused on the retina regardless of object distance.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "the lens focuses light." Instead, explain how the ciliary muscles contract, suspensory ligaments slacken, and the lens becomes thicker to focus on near objects.</p>
<p>👉 Use the phrase: "Light is refracted by the cornea and lens, and accommodation changes the lens shape to keep images sharp on the retina."</p>

Explain how the eye focuses light on the retina when looking at a near object. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Ciliary muscles contract to reduce tension on suspensory ligaments.
2. Suspensory ligaments slacken, so they no longer pull the lens tight.
3. The lens becomes thicker and more curved.
4. This increases refraction of light, allowing near objects to be focused on the retina.
This causes a sharp image to form on the retina for clear vision of near objects.
</details>

<p>👉 The eye uses a two-step focusing system: the cornea provides the main refraction to bend light, and the lens fine-tunes the focus through accommodation.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent front layer that bends light entering the eye.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part that controls pupil size to regulate light intake.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening controlled by the iris that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – Transparent structure behind the pupil that changes shape to focus light.
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors that detect light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles controlling the tension on the lens to adjust its shape.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that connect ciliary muscles to the lens and adjust lens shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus light on the retina.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different substances.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cells in the retina that detect light intensity and colour.
