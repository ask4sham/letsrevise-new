3 — DEFINITION

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>The nervous system is a network that detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses by transmitting electrical impulses between receptors, the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord), and effectors such as muscles and glands.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS (brain and spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>Step-by-step chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Stimulus detected by receptors</li>  
<li>Sensory neurone carries electrical impulses to the CNS</li>  
<li>Relay neurones in the CNS process the information</li>  
<li>Motor neurone carries impulses to effectors</li>  
<li>Effector (muscle or gland) produces a response – movement or secretion</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong> The nervous system detects stimuli quickly and coordinates an immediate response using specialised cells called neurones.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect a change (stimulus) in the environment (e.g., light, temperature, pressure).</li>  
<li>The stimulus triggers an electrical impulse in the sensory neurone.</li>  
<li>Impulse travels via sensory neurone to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>  
<li>Relay neurones in the CNS process the information and connect to motor neurones.</li>  
<li>The motor neurone carries the impulse to an effector — a muscle contracts or a gland secretes.</li>  
<li>The response protects the body or adjusts conditions to restore balance.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are adapted to detect specific stimuli, e.g. light receptors contain pigments that respond to light.</li>  
<li>Neurones have long axons to carry impulses quickly over distances.</li>  
<li>Myelin sheath insulates axons, increasing impulse speed via saltatory conduction.</li>  
<li>Dendrites connect to other neurones, receiving impulses.</li>  
<li>Synapses between neurones ensure impulses travel in one direction using neurotransmitters.</li>  
<li>The CNS integrates information and sends coordinated commands.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Use full chains in your answers: Stimulus detected by receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS processes → Motor neurone → Effector → Response.</p>  
<p>👉 Name neurone types and CNS parts clearly.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague terms like “messages” or “signals” without saying “electrical impulses.”</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Being able to explain the nervous system’s organisation helps you understand how organisms detect and respond to environmental changes quickly—a key survival skill and a common exam topic.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always describe the pathway from stimulus to response in order and use the correct terms: receptor, sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, effector.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague terms like “message” or confusing motor and sensory neurones. Be clear about the direction impulses travel.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The receptor sends a message to the brain and the muscle moves.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone carries impulses to the brain, which sends impulses to the muscle to move because it detects a stimulus.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>A stimulus is detected by a receptor, which generates an electrical impulse. The sensory neurone carries this impulse to the CNS (brain or spinal cord), where relay neurones pass the impulse to motor neurones. The motor neurone carries the impulse to the muscle (effector), causing it to contract and produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses precise terms like sensory neurone, CNS, relay neurone, and motor neurone.</li>  
<li>It explains the direction and sequence of impulse transmission.</li>  
<li>It links stimulus → impulse generation → response in a clear cause → effect chain.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)  

Question:  
Explain how the myelin sheath affects the speed of electrical impulse transmission along neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon of the neurone, preventing electrical impulses from leaking out.  
2. This insulation allows impulses to jump across gaps called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. Because impulses jump instead of moving continuously, transmission is much faster.  
4. This means the effector responds more quickly to stimuli, which is important for survival, such as quickly withdrawing from danger.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – detects changes (stimuli) inside or outside the body  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – a change in the environment that is detected by receptors  
<strong>Response</strong> – a reaction by muscles or glands to a stimulus  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – neurones inside the CNS that connect sensory to motor neurones  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – carries impulses from the CNS to effectors  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – the gap between neurones where impulses are transmitted chemically  
<strong>CNS</strong> – central nervous system; brain and spinal cord  
<strong>PNS</strong> – peripheral nervous system; all other nerves outside the CNS  
<strong>Effector</strong> – muscle or gland that produces a response to an impulse
