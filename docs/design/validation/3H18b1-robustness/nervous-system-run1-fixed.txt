9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system uses specialised cells called neurones to send electrical messages rapidly.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Stimuli are detected by receptors, which are cells adapted to respond to specific changes like light, temperature, or pressure.</li>
<li>These receptors convert the stimulus into electrical impulses.</li>
<li>The impulses travel along sensory neurones to the CNS — brain and spinal cord — where the information is processed.</li>
<li>The CNS sends signals via motor neurones to effectors, like muscles or glands.</li>
<li>Effectors then produce the response: muscles contract, glands secrete.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Sensory neurones have long dendrites to carry impulses from receptors to the cell body and long axons to transmit signals to the CNS.</li>
<li>Motor neurones have long axons to reach effectors quickly, enabling a fast response.</li>
<li>Myelin sheath surrounds axons to insulate and speed up electrical impulses by saltatory conduction, jumping between Nodes of Ranvier.</li>
<li>Synapses between neurones allow chemical transmission of impulses with neurotransmitters, ensuring directional control.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, say 'sensory neurone carries impulses to the CNS' and 'motor neurone carries impulses away to effectors'. Always describe the pathway fully and explain why myelination increases speed if asked.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath acts as an insulator around the axon.
2. Electrical impulses jump between gaps in the sheath called Nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).
3. This means less electrical signal is lost during transmission.
4. As a result, impulses travel much faster along myelinated neurones, allowing quicker responses.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects changes in the environment (stimuli).
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action produced by an effector following stimulus detection.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses within the CNS, between sensory and motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where chemical neurotransmitters pass impulses on.
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system; brain and spinal cord where processing occurs.
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system; neurones outside the CNS connecting receptors and effectors.
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to a stimulus.
