5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → The cornea refracts (bends) light to help focus it → The lens fine-tunes this focus by changing shape → The light is focused onto the retina → The retina contains photoreceptors that detect the light and send signals to the brain.</strong></p>  
<p>To break it down as a chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the <strong>cornea</strong></li>  
<li>Cornea <strong>refracts</strong> (bends) light rays</li>  
<li><strong>Iris</strong> adjusts the size of the <strong>pupil</strong> controlling light intensity</li>  
<li><strong>Lens</strong> changes shape (accommodation) to focus light accurately</li>  
<li>Light rays converge on the <strong>retina</strong></li>  
<li><strong>Photoreceptors</strong> in the retina detect light and send impulses to the brain</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the eye focuses light precisely onto the retina to form a clear image that the brain can interpret.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light first enters the eye through the transparent <strong>cornea</strong>.</li>  
<li>The cornea bends (refracts) the light rays, beginning the focusing process.</li>  
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> adjusts the size of the <strong>pupil</strong> to control the amount of light entering, depending on brightness.</li>  
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> then fine-tunes the focus. It changes shape by contracting or relaxing the <strong>ciliary muscles</strong>, which slacken or tighten the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>.</li>  
<li>This change in lens shape is called <strong>accommodation</strong>, allowing focus on objects at different distances.</li>  
<li>Finally, light rays converge on the <strong>retina</strong>, where photoreceptor cells detect the light and send signals via the optic nerve to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The <strong>curved cornea</strong> is adapted to efficiently refract light rays as they enter, helping to focus them toward the retina.</li>  
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> is flexible and changes shape for accommodation, allowing sharp images from objects both near and far.</li>  
<li>The <strong>retina’s photoreceptors</strong> convert light energy into electrical signals, essential for vision.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the eye focuses light” — say <em>light is refracted by the cornea and the lens changes shape by accommodation using ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments to focus the image on the retina where photoreceptors detect it</em>.</p>  

<p>👉 Use clear cause → effect chains, e.g. “Ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker → light refracted more strongly → near object focused on retina.”</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying <em>“the lens just focuses light”</em> without explaining how.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is that <strong>the lens changes shape by the action of the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments</strong> to focus light on the retina precisely.</p>  
<p>👉 Exam link: Failing to explain accommodation causes loss of marks because the examiner wants the <em>cause → process → effect</em> chain, not just naming function.</p>  

<p>👉 Students also sometimes say <em>“the retina sees the image”</em>. Actually, <strong>the retina detects light and the brain interprets the image</strong>, which is a better phrasing for full credit.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Clear vision depends on how the eye focuses light onto the retina. If focusing fails, images are blurred, which impacts survival activities like finding food or avoiding danger.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When a question asks to explain focusing or accommodation, always use key terms like <strong>cornea refracts light</strong>, <strong>lens changes shape</strong>, <strong>ciliary muscles contract or relax</strong>, <strong>suspensory ligaments slacken or tighten</strong>, and <strong>retina detects light</strong>.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like "the eye zooms in" or "the lens just focuses". Explain <em>how</em> focusing happens with cause and effect.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The lens focuses light onto the retina.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The lens focuses light because it changes shape.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The lens changes shape by the contraction or relaxation of the ciliary muscles that slacken or tighten the suspensory ligaments, allowing the lens to become thicker or thinner. This changes the amount light is refracted so that near or distant images are focused sharply onto the retina.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Includes clear cause → process → effect reasoning.</li>  
<li>Uses precise terms: ciliary muscles, suspensory ligaments, accommodation.</li>  
<li>Shows detailed understanding beyond simple naming.</li>  
</ul>

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The eye uses a combination of structures working together: the cornea bends incoming light, the iris controls light entry through the pupil, and the lens uses accommodation to fine-focus images on the retina. This coordinated system ensures clear vision under different conditions.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – the transparent, curved front part of the eye that refracts light as it enters.  
<strong>Iris</strong> – the coloured part of the eye that controls pupil size to regulate light intensity.  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – the opening in the iris through which light passes into the eye.  
<strong>Lens</strong> – a flexible, transparent structure that changes shape to focus light.  
<strong>Retina</strong> – the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors.  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – muscles that change the shape of the lens by tightening or relaxing suspensory ligaments.  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – fibres that connect ciliary muscles to the lens, adjusting lens shape.  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – the process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – bending of light as it passes from one medium to another.  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – specialised cells in the retina that detect light and trigger electrical signals.
