5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The homeostasis process follows this pathway:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A <strong>stimulus</strong> causes a change that moves conditions away from the optimum.</li>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this change (the stimulus).</li>
<li>The <strong>coordination centre</strong> (e.g., brain, spinal cord, pancreas) receives and processes the information.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) carry out a response to counteract the change.</li>
<li>The response restores conditions to the <strong>optimum</strong>, turning off the corrective action when balance is achieved.</li>
</ol>
<p>1 stimulus → 2 receptor → 3 coordination centre → 4 effector → 5 response → 6 return to optimum</p>
<p>👉 <strong>This negative feedback loop keeps internal conditions stable.</strong></p>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The key idea here is that homeostasis keeps conditions inside your body stable—even when the outside world changes a lot.</strong></p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus causes conditions to change from the optimum point that enzymes and cells like.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this specific stimulus—like temperature receptors sensing cold or glucose receptors sensing blood sugar changes.</li>
<li>The coordination centre, for example the brain or pancreas, receives this info and decides what needs to be done.</li>
<li>Effectors—muscles or glands—produce a response to reverse the change.</li>
<li>This response brings the condition back to its optimum level, and the effectors stop working when balance is restored.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect temperature changes → send signals to the hypothalamus (coordination centre) → which activates sweat glands (effectors) to produce sweat → evaporation cools the skin → core temperature returns to optimum.</li>
<li>Special receptors in the pancreas detect high blood glucose (stimulus) → pancreas (coordination centre) releases insulin (effector action via hormone) → insulin causes glucose to be absorbed by cells for storage → blood glucose level falls → homeostasis achieved.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 A strong exam answer names the receptor, coordination centre, and effector, explains the mechanism clearly, and shows the response returning conditions to the optimum level via negative feedback.</p>
<p>👉 Avoid just saying "the body changes back" without naming parts or showing how.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose concentration rises after a meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The stimulus is a rise in blood glucose concentration after eating.
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this increase in glucose.
3. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin (hormone) into the bloodstream.
4. Insulin causes body cells to take in glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen, which lowers blood glucose back to the optimum.
This negative feedback loop restores blood glucose to normal levels.
</details>

<ul>
<li>Homeostasis maintains stable internal conditions despite external and internal changes.</li>
<li>It is essential because enzymes and cells only work well under optimum conditions.</li>
<li>Homeostasis uses a simple but effective process: stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to optimum.</li>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal conditions stable for healthy cell function.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by the body.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or organ that detects a stimulus.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Part of the body that processes information and sends out instructions.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response to restore balance.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action done by the effector to counteract the stimulus.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best condition for enzyme and cell function.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process that reverses a change to return conditions to optimum.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released into the blood to coordinate responses.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that speeds up reactions but works best within certain conditions.
