9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the eye focuses light so the brain receives a clear image.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea.</li>
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light to start focusing it.</li>
<li>The iris controls how much light passes through the pupil to protect the eye and improve vision.</li>
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focusing by changing its shape — this is accommodation.</li>
<li>The light converges on the retina, where photoreceptors detect it.</li>
<li>Electrical signals then travel via the optic nerve to the brain for image interpretation.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The cornea is curved and transparent, so it can bend light effectively to start focusing the image.</li>
<li>The lens changes shape due to the action of ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments, which alter lens curvature for near or distant focus.</li>
<li>The retina contains photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) that detect light intensity and colour, sending impulses to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Accommodation for near objects (example):</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>The ciliary muscles contract</li>
<li>Suspensory ligaments slacken</li>
<li>The lens becomes thicker and more curved</li>
<li>This causes light to refract more strongly</li>
<li>The image of the near object is focused sharply on the retina</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Never just say “The lens focuses light” — always explain the mechanism: “The lens changes shape due to ciliary muscles contracting or relaxing, which alters how much light bends, enabling accommodation.”</p>
<p>👉 Use cause → effect wording: “Ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens thickens → light refracted more → image focused on retina.”</p>

<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

</details>

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The eye uses several structures working together to produce clear vision:</p>
<ul>
<li>The cornea bends light as it enters, starting the focusing process.</li>
<li>The iris and pupil control the amount of light entering the eye.</li>
<li>The lens changes shape via accommodation to focus images sharply on the retina.</li>
<li>The retina converts light into electrical signals sent to the brain for interpretation.</li>
</ul>
<p>👉 Together, these processes allow the eye to adjust quickly to different lighting and distances, a vital function for survival and daily activity.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – The transparent front layer of the eye that bends light rays as they enter.
<strong>Iris</strong> – The coloured part of the eye that adjusts pupil size to regulate light entering.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening in the iris through which light passes into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – A flexible structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – The light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptor cells.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change the lens shape for accommodation.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that attach to the lens and pull on it to alter its shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different materials.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Specialized cells in the retina that detect light and colour.
