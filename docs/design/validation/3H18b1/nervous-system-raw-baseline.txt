<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Central Nervous System (brain/spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector (muscle or gland) → Response</strong></p>
<p>Watch the cause → effect chain here:</p>
<ol>
<li>A stimulus is detected by a specific receptor cell.</li>
<li>The receptor sends an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone.</li>
<li>The impulse reaches the CNS where it’s processed (in the brain or spinal cord).</li>
<li>The CNS sends an impulse via a motor neurone to an effector.</li>
<li>The effector (muscle or gland) carries out a response (like contraction or secretion).</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Let’s break down the overall structure and how it helps you respond.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The nervous system is designed to detect changes and trigger fast, coordinated responses.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are specialized cells sensitive to specific stimuli like light, temperature, or pressure.</li>
<li>When stimulated, receptors convert the signal into electrical impulses that travel along neurones.</li>
<li>The impulses are carried by sensory neurones to the CNS—the brain and spinal cord—where they are processed.</li>
<li>Relay neurones inside the CNS pass the impulse to motor neurones.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors (muscles/glands).</li>
<li>The effectors produce a response, such as muscle contraction or hormone secretion.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> have nerve endings to detect specific stimuli → produces impulses in sensory neurones → allowing rapid communication.</li>
<li><strong>Myelin sheath</strong> on neurones insulates and speeds up impulses → impulses jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier → faster responses.</li>
<li><strong>The CNS</strong> integrates incoming impulses → sends out precise signals → coordinating complex responses.</li>
<li><strong>Effector muscles</strong> contract → movement occurs (e.g., pulling away from harmful stimuli) → survival advantage.</li>
<li><strong>Glands</strong> secrete chemicals or hormones → help regulate body functioning and maintain homeostasis.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 When explaining nervous responses in exams, say the stimulus is detected by receptors which send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS, and the motor neurones carry impulses away to effectors that respond.</p>
<p>Avoid vague terms like 'messages' or just 'signals. '</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Describe how myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon of the neurone.
2. Impulses jump between nodes of Ranvier in a process called saltatory conduction.
3. This reduces the loss of electrical signal down the neurone.
4. As a result, impulses travel faster along the neurone, allowing quicker responses.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialized cell that detects a stimulus in the environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by an effector as a result of a nervous impulse.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – A nerve cell that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – A nerve cell in the CNS that connects sensory and motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – A nerve cell that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap where impulses pass between neurones using chemicals.
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system consisting of the brain and spinal cord.
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system linking the detectors and effectors to the CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to a stimulus.
