9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 The nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates quick responses by transmitting electrical impulses through specialised cells called neurones.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus, such as heat or pressure, is detected by receptors in the sensory organs.</li>  
<li>These receptors convert the stimulus into an electrical impulse.</li>  
<li>The impulse travels along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord) where it is processed.</li>  
<li>The CNS sends impulses along motor neurones to effectors (muscles or glands).</li>  
<li>Effectors produce responses, like muscle contraction or secretion of hormones by glands.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect specific stimuli and generate electrical impulses → allows the nervous system to start responses.</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites and axons → efficiently carry impulses to CNS.</li>  
<li>The <strong>CNS</strong> processes impulses and coordinates action → central control centre.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> have axons and are myelinated → transmit impulses quickly to effectors.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) receive impulses → muscles contract or glands secrete → organism responds quickly.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining, say “sensory neurone carries impulse <em>to</em> CNS” and “motor neurone carries impulse <em>away</em> to effector”. Avoid vague phrases like “messages travel in nerves”.</p>  
<p>👉 Always link structure (e.g., myelin sheath) to function (speed of impulse conduction) and then to effect (rapid response).</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system’s structure and function helps you accurately describe how organisms detect changes and respond quickly, a common exam topic.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When an exam question asks “Describe how impulses travel in the nervous system,” focus on the direction: show impulses go <em>to</em> the CNS along sensory neurones and <em>away</em> from the CNS along motor neurones, and mention synapses if relevant.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Impulses travel along nerves to the brain and then to muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Electrical impulses travel along sensory neurones to the CNS, then motor neurones carry impulses to muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Receptors detect the stimulus and generate electrical impulses which travel along sensory neurones to the CNS. The CNS processes the information, sending impulses along motor neurones to effectors which cause muscles to contract.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses correct terminology like “sensory neurone” and “motor neurone.”</li>  
<li>It clearly describes the direction of impulses and links structure to function.</li>  
<li>It explains the full cause → effect chain, earning maximum marks.</li>  
</ul>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing the electrical impulse from leaking out.  
2. This causes impulses to jump from one node of Ranvier (gap in the myelin) to the next — called saltatory conduction.  
3. Jumping impulses reduce transmission time along the neurone.  
4. Therefore, impulses reach effectors faster, enabling rapid responses to stimuli.  
</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>The nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates fast responses using electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect changes and send impulses via sensory neurones to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information and sends impulses through motor neurones.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) carry out the response by contracting or secreting.</li>  
<li>Myelin sheaths speed up impulse transmission along neurones.</li>  
<li>The pathway is stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember, each part’s structure is adapted to its role — for example, myelin increases speed, and dendrites increase connections.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always explain the cause → effect chain with specific terms for full marks.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A structure that detects stimuli and starts an electrical impulse.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.  
<strong>Response</strong> – An action by the organism following a stimulus.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone located in the CNS that transmits impulses between sensory and motor neurones.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The small gap between neurones where impulses are passed chemically.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, consisting of the brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, all neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response when stimulated by a motor neurone.
