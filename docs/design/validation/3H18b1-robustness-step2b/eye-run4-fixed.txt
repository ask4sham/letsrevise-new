9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is understanding how the eye focuses light to create a clear image.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first passes through the cornea, which starts bending (refracting) the light because it is curved and transparent.</li>
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil to regulate how much light enters — smaller in bright light, larger in dim light.</li>
<li>Then light passes through the lens, which fine-tunes the focus by changing shape — this is called accommodation.</li>
<li>The focused light converges on the retina, which contains photoreceptor cells that detect light intensity and colour.</li>
<li>These photoreceptors send electrical signals via the optic nerve to the brain, allowing you to see.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent → so it can refract light rays → to begin focusing them onto the retina.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> changes pupil size → to control the amount of light entering → protecting the retina and improving vision in different light levels.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> contract or relax → changing tension on the suspensory ligaments → which alters the lens shape → changing how strongly it refracts light (accommodation) → enabling focus on objects at different distances.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Accommodation for near objects:</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract →</li>
<li>Suspensory ligaments slacken →</li>
<li>Lens becomes thicker and more curved →</li>
<li>Light is refracted more strongly →</li>
<li>Near object is focused on the retina.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Accommodation for distant objects:</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>Ciliary muscles relax →</li>
<li>Suspensory ligaments tighten →</li>
<li>Lens becomes thinner and less curved →</li>
<li>Light is refracted less strongly →</li>
<li>Distant object is focused on the retina.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In your exam answers, always explain using cause → effect steps.</p>
<p>For example, say “The ciliary muscles contract, so suspensory ligaments slacken, allowing the lens to become thicker, which refracts light more strongly to focus near objects on the retina. ”</p>
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like “the lens just focuses light” without explaining how the shape changes or how muscles control this.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistakes</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say <em>“The lens just focuses light”</em> without explaining how it changes shape.</p>
<p>👉 The correct explanation is that <strong>ciliary muscles change tension on suspensory ligaments</strong>, which alters the lens’s curvature, adjusting how strongly it refracts light.</p>
<p>👉 <strong>Exam link:</strong> Answers missing this chain lose marks because they omit the key process of accommodation, which is how the lens actually focuses.</p>
<p>👉 Another frequent error is saying <em>"The retina sees the image."</em> The retina doesn't see but <strong>detects light using photoreceptors</strong>, then sends impulses for the brain to interpret the image.</p>
<p>👉 <strong>Exam link:</strong> Using “detects light” is more accurate and gains marks; vague wording loses precision.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – The clear, curved front layer of the eye that bends light.
<strong>Iris</strong> – The coloured part of the eye that controls pupil size.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening in the iris that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – A flexible structure behind the pupil that changes shape to focus light.
<strong>Retina</strong> – The light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that control the shape of the lens for accommodation.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that connect the ciliary muscles to the lens and change its shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on near or distant objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light when it passes through materials like the cornea and lens.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – A type of cell in the retina that detects light and sends signals to the brain.
