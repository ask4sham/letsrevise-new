5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Central Nervous System (CNS) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>This shows the pathway of electrical impulses from detecting a change to producing a reaction.</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus (like touching something hot) is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>Receptors send impulses along sensory neurones towards the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain or spinal cord) processes the information.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) create a response, like pulling the hand away.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea here is that the nervous system detects and responds rapidly by transmitting electrical signals.</p>  

<h3><strong>How the nervous system is organised</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The CNS consists of the brain and spinal cord, acting as the main processing centres.</li>  
<li>The Peripheral Nervous System (PNS) includes all the nerves branching from the CNS to the rest of the body.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised cells adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light, temperature, touch).</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> carry electrical impulses toward the CNS.</li>  
<li><strong>Relay neurones</strong> inside the CNS connect sensory inputs to motor outputs.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> transmit impulses away from the CNS to effectors.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) produce a response like contraction or secretion.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Process → effect</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus activates receptors → impulses travel via sensory neurones → CNS processes → impulses travel via motor neurones → effectors respond → organism reacts.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>How neurones are adapted for their function</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Dendrites</strong> receive impulses from other cells.</li>  
<li>A long <strong>axon</strong> carries impulses rapidly over distance.</li>  
<li><strong>Myelin sheath</strong> insulates axons, increasing impulse speed by saltatory conduction.</li>  
<li><strong>Synapses</strong> allow chemical transmission between neurones.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always link: “Impulses travel along sensory neurones <em>to</em> the CNS, then along motor neurones <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors.” Avoid vague wording like “messages pass through nerves” without mentioning electrical impulses or direction.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying “nerves send messages” without specifying that the messages are electrical impulses carried along neurones.</p>  
<p><strong>Why this matters:</strong> The examiners expect you to explain how impulses travel as electrical signals along specific neurones, not just vague ‘messages.’</p>  
<p>👉 Another frequent error is mixing up sensory and motor neurones, saying motor neurones carry impulses to the CNS, which is incorrect.</p>  
<p><strong>Correct it by saying:</strong> Sensory neurones carry impulses <em>to</em> the CNS; motor neurones carry impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the nervous system pathway is crucial for explaining how the body coordinates rapid responses, which is a common exam topic.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>When the question asks “explain how impulses pass…” use precise terms: mention electrical impulses, name the neurones (sensory, relay, motor), and clearly state the direction impulses travel relative to the CNS.</p>  
<p>Avoid vague terms like “messages” or “signals” without explanation.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Impulses travel through nerves to the brain and muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Electrical impulses travel along sensory neurones to the brain, then motor neurones take the signal to muscles.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Electrical impulses travel from receptors along sensory neurones <em>to</em> the CNS where relay neurones connect to motor neurones, which carry impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors that produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It includes specific neurone types and impulse directions, matching exam expectations.</li>  
<li>Links cause (stimulus) → process (impulse transmission) → effect (response).</li>  
<li>Uses correct scientific vocabulary precisely.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing electrical current loss.  
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This reduces the distance impulses travel along the neurone’s membrane.  
4. Therefore, impulses travel faster, allowing rapid responses to stimuli.  
</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>The nervous system coordinates immediate responses using electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information, while the PNS includes nerves throughout the body.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects stimuli.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that causes a response.  
<strong>Response</strong> – An organism’s reaction to a stimulus.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Connects sensory neurones to motor neurones in the CNS.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where chemical signals pass impulses.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system made of brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system made of nerves outside CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response.
