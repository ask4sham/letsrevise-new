5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Light passes through the cornea → then through the lens → then focuses on the retina for image detection.</strong></p>
<p>Watch the cause → effect chain here:</p>
<ol>
<li>Light enters the eye through the <strong>cornea</strong>.</li>
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays to begin focusing.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> adjusts the size of the pupil to control how much light enters.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> fine-tunes the focus by changing shape (a process called accommodation).</li>
<li>Light rays converge and form an image on the <strong>retina</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Photoreceptors</strong> in the retina detect light and send signals to the brain.</li>
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the eye focuses light using several parts working together to produce a clear image on the retina.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first passes through the <strong>cornea</strong> where refraction, or bending of light rays, begins.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> controls the size of the <strong>pupil</strong>, adjusting how much light enters depending on brightness.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> fine-tunes the focus by changing its shape to make sure the light converges precisely on the retina.</li>
<li>The <strong>retina</strong> contains photoreceptor cells that detect light and convert it into electrical signals sent to the brain.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent, perfect for bending light rays as they enter the eye, starting the focusing process.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> has muscles that contract or relax to control the pupil’s diameter, which regulates light intensity to protect the retina.</li>
<li>The <strong>ciliary muscles</strong> adjust the tension on the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, changing the <strong>lens</strong>’s shape for clear focus, a process called <strong>accommodation</strong>.</li>
<li>Around eye focusing: Contracting ciliary muscles slacken suspensory ligaments, allowing the lens to become thicker for focusing on close objects.</li>
<li>Relaxing ciliary muscles tighten suspensory ligaments, making the lens thinner to focus on distant objects.</li>
<li>This causes the light rays to converge exactly on the retina, forming a clear image.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the lens focuses light”. Instead, use phrases like “light is refracted by the cornea and lens,” and explain “ciliary muscles control suspensory ligaments to change lens shape during accommodation”.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say <em>"The lens simply focuses light"</em> without explaining how.</p>
<p>A better way to say it is: “The lens changes shape controlled by the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments to focus light on the retina.”</p>
<p>Exam link: <strong>Without the accommodation explanation, you miss key marks because the examiner expects process detail.</strong></p>

<p>👉 Another error is to say <em>"The retina sees the image."</em></p>
<p>A clearer answer is: “The retina detects light with photoreceptors and sends electrical impulses to the brain which interprets the image.”</p>
<p>Exam link: <strong>“Seeing” is not a cell function; marks are given for understanding detection and signal transmission.</strong></p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent, curved outer layer that bends light entering the eye.  
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part with muscles that adjust pupil size to control light entry.  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The hole in the iris that lets light into the eye.  
<strong>Lens</strong> – Transparent structure that changes shape to focus light.  
<strong>Retina</strong> – Light-sensitive layer with photoreceptors that detect light.  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape for focusing.  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that pull on the lens to change its shape during accommodation.  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes through different materials.  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cell in the retina that detects light and sends signals to the brain.
