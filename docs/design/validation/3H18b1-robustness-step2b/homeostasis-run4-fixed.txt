5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis involves a simple but crucial pathway:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A <strong>stimulus</strong> causes a change in internal conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this change (stimulus).</li>
<li>Information is sent to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> (like the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) which processes the information.</li>
<li>The coordination centre activates <strong>effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) to respond.</li>
<li>The <strong>effectors</strong> carry out actions that restore conditions to the <strong>optimum</strong> level.</li>
</ol>
<p>This is often called a <strong>negative feedback loop</strong>, where the body reverses the change to return to normal.</p>
<p>👉 <strong>Cause → effect chain:</strong> Stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to optimum.</p>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea here is that homeostasis keeps conditions inside your body steady and optimal for cells to function.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>When something in the body changes (stimulus), receptors sense that change.</li>
<li>The receptors send this information to coordination centres, such as the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas, which process it.</li>
<li>The coordination centres then activate effectors — muscles or glands — that produce a response to counteract the change.</li>
<li>This response brings the condition back to the optimum level.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are specialised cells adapted to detect specific stimuli, such as temperature receptors in the skin sensing heat.</li>
<li>Coordination centres have the role of processing the receptor signals and deciding on the correct response (e.g., the hypothalamus in the brain regulates temperature).</li>
<li>Effectors carry out the response: muscles may contract (shivering), or glands may secrete hormones or sweat.</li>
<li>The response counteracts the original stimulus, restoring balance (negative feedback).</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In exam answers, name all three key parts: receptor, coordination centre, and effector, then explain what each does and end with how the response restores optimum conditions.</p>
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like “the brain makes you sweat” — instead say “thermoreceptors detect the rise in temperature, the hypothalamus processes this, and sweat glands produce sweat to cool the body.”</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake in homeostasis explanations</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say "The brain makes you sweat" without naming the hypothalamus as the coordination centre.</p>
<p>👉 Students often write “The body cools itself by sweating” without explaining how evaporation removes heat energy.</p>
<p>👉 <strong>Exam link:</strong> Answers lose marks if they miss naming the specific receptor, coordination centre, and effector, or if they do not explain how the response corrects the change.</p>
<p>👉 A better way is to say: "Thermoreceptors detect increased temperature; the hypothalamus processes this information and activates sweat glands; sweat evaporates and removes heat, returning temperature to optimum."</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the pancreas helps maintain blood glucose concentration after a sugary meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The rise in blood glucose is detected by receptors in the pancreas.
2. The pancreas acts as a coordination centre and releases insulin (a hormone).
3. Insulin stimulates liver cells to absorb glucose and convert it to glycogen.
4. Blood glucose concentration decreases back to the optimum level.
This is negative feedback restoring glucose balance after a meal.
</details>

<p>👉 Homeostasis is a control system with three main players — receptors detect a stimulus, coordination centres process the information, and effectors produce responses.</p>
<p>👉 This system uses negative feedback to reverse changes and keep internal conditions stable, which is vital because enzymes and cells only work well in narrow conditions.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions despite external changes.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects a stimulus.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information and sends instructions (e.g., brain, spinal cord, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action by effectors to restore optimum conditions.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal condition for cells and enzymes to function best.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process that reverses a change to bring conditions back to normal.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger released by glands in homeostasis.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that speeds up chemical reactions, needing stable conditions to work.
