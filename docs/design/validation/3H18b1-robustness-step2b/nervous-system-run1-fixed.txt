9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The nervous system is designed to detect changes quickly and coordinate rapid responses.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>Specialised cells called receptors detect stimuli and send electrical impulses through neurones to the CNS where they are processed, and then impulses are sent to effectors to produce a response.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Stimuli such as light, heat, or pressure trigger receptors.</li>
<li>Receptors generate electrical impulses that travel along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord).</li>
<li>The CNS processes this information and sends impulses via motor neurones to muscles or glands (effectors).</li>
<li>The effectors carry out a response like contracting a muscle or secreting a hormone.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are adapted to detect specific stimuli (light receptors respond to light).</li>
<li>Sensory neurones have dendrites and long axons to transmit impulses quickly to the CNS.</li>
<li>The CNS acts as a processing centre, deciding how to respond.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS to the effector.</li>
<li>Effectors respond by contracting muscles or releasing chemicals, allowing movement or secretion.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 When explaining, say precisely how impulses move along named neurones, specify direction (to or from the CNS), identify effectors, and connect how structure helps function for full marks.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say “Nerves send messages to the brain” without explaining that these are electrical impulses travelling along sensory neurones toward the CNS.</p>
<p>Students sometimes confuse sensory and motor neurones or miss the direction of impulse travel.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> This loses marks because AQA expects precise use of terms ‘impulses’ and correct neurone roles, especially naming sensory neurones carrying impulses <em>to</em> the CNS and motor neurones carrying impulses <em>away</em>.</p>
<p>A better way is to say, “The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses from the receptor to the CNS, and the motor neurone carries impulses from the CNS to the effector.”</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Understanding the structure and function of the nervous system is key for explaining how organisms sense their environment and react quickly, a fundamental survival mechanism tested in exams.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When describing the nervous system, always explain the direction of impulses clearly: sensory neurones carry impulses <em>to</em> the CNS, motor neurones carry impulses <em>away</em>, and name the effectors and responses.</p>
<p>👉 Use cause → effect chains; avoid vague descriptions like “messages” without specifying electrical impulses or synapses.</p>

<p>The nervous system sends electrical impulses from receptors to muscles to cause movement.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing loss of electrical signal.
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).
3. This allows impulses to travel faster along the neurone than continuously along the whole axon.
4. Faster transmission means effectors respond more quickly to stimuli, improving survival.
</details>

24— SUMMARY
Paste into: Text (concept)
<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>The nervous system coordinates rapid responses by sending electrical impulses between receptors, CNS, and effectors.</li>
<li>The CNS consists of the brain and spinal cord, organising and processing information.</li>
<li>Receptors detect stimuli, and sensory neurones carry impulses <em>to</em> the CNS.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors like muscles or glands.</li>
<li>Neurones have dendrites to receive signals, an axon to carry impulses, and myelin sheath to speed transmission.</li>
<li>Synapses are junctions between neurones where impulses cross chemically.</li>
<li>Effectors produce responses such as muscle contraction or secretion from glands.</li>
</ul>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – detects changes (stimuli) in the environment
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – a change that organisms detect and respond to
<strong>Response</strong> – the action produced by an effector to a stimulus
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – carries electrical impulses to the CNS
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – connects sensory neurones to motor neurones within the CNS
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – carries impulses from the CNS to effectors
<strong>Synapse</strong> – a small gap between neurones where chemicals transmit impulses
<strong>CNS</strong> – central nervous system made up of brain and spinal cord
<strong>PNS</strong> – peripheral nervous system, all neurones outside the CNS
<strong>Effector</strong> – muscles or glands that respond to impulses to produce an effect
