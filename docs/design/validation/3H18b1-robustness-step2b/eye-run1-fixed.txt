9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 At its simplest, the eye works by bending light to form a focused image on the retina, enabling us to see clearly.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye focuses light accurately using the cornea and lens so the retina detects sharp images.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first hits the <strong>cornea</strong>, a transparent curved layer that refracts (bends) incoming light rays.</li>
<li>The amount the light bends depends on the curvature of the cornea, ensuring rays begin to focus.</li>
<li>Next, light passes through the <strong>pupil</strong>, the opening controlled by the <strong>iris</strong>, which adjusts size to regulate light entering the eye.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> then fine-focuses light, changing its shape for shallow or deep focusing via accommodation.</li>
<li>Focused light converges on the <strong>retina</strong>, where photoreceptor cells detect light intensity and colour.</li>
<li>Signals from photoreceptors are sent to the brain through the optic nerve, forming the image we see.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent → so it can refract light efficiently as it enters the eye producing initial focus.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> control the tension on <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> → changing the <strong>lens</strong> shape for accommodation → which alters how strongly light is refracted to focus near or distant objects.</li>
<li>A change in lens shape → causes light rays to converge precisely on the retina → so a sharp image forms for photoreceptors to detect.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "the lens focuses light" — say how "ciliary muscles change the tension on suspensory ligaments causing the lens to thicken or thin, altering refraction to focus light on the retina."</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistakes in explaining the eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is just writing "the lens focuses light" without explaining how it changes shape.</p>
<p>Students often confuse "focusing" with just "looking" and don’t mention <strong>accommodation</strong>, which lose marks.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Examiners expect cause → effect chains showing ciliary muscles → suspensory ligaments → lens shape change → refraction → image focus.</p>
<p>👉 Another error is saying "the retina sees the image." The retina <em>detects light</em>, but the brain interprets the image.</p>
<p><strong>Better way:</strong> The retina contains photoreceptors that detect light and send signals to the brain for image formation.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent outer layer that refracts light entering the eye
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured muscle controlling pupil size and light entry
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Opening controlled by iris that lets light into the eye
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure that changes shape to focus light
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer of photoreceptor cells detecting focused light and sending signals
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Connect lens to ciliary muscles, adjusting tension
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on objects at different distances
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes through different materials
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cells in the retina detecting brightness and colour
