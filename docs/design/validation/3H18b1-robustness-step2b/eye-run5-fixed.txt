9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key thing to understand is how the eye focuses light to form clear images on the retina.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye’s main structures work together to bend (refract) light and focus it precisely on the retina.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first passes through the <strong>cornea</strong>, which is curved and transparent, so it refracts light toward the eye’s interior.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> adjusts the size of the <strong>pupil</strong> to control how much light enters, protecting the retina from too much light.</li>
<li>The <strong>lens</strong> changes shape to fine-tune the focus by thin or thickening, a process controlled by the <strong>ciliary muscles</strong> and <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>.</li>
<li>The <strong>retina</strong> at the back receives the focused light and contains photoreceptors (rods and cones) that detect images and send signals to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>curved cornea</strong> refracts incoming light rays → bending them to direct toward the centre of the eye.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris controls pupil size</strong> → regulating light intensity entering → protecting the retina.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles contract or relax</strong> → changing tension in <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> → lens becomes more or less rounded → altering its refractive power.</li>
<li>This process, called <strong>accommodation</strong>, changes how light is bent to focus on the retina → sharp images for near or distant objects.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Use the sequence: light enters cornea → light refracted → pupil size adjusted by iris → lens shape changed by ciliary muscles → light focused on retina → photoreceptors detect light.</p>
<p>Don’t just say “the lens focuses light”; say how the ciliary muscles and ligaments change lens shape to focus.</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Understanding the eye’s structure and focusing mechanism is vital for explaining how we see sharply and for recognising what goes wrong in vision problems like short-sightedness.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When exam questions ask you to "explain" or "describe" how the eye focuses light, always include the steps: light refraction by the cornea → pupil controls light intensity → lens changes shape (accommodation) via ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments → light focuses on the retina.</p>
<p>👉 Avoid vague answers like “the lens focuses light” without detail on how it changes shape or how light is bent.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>
<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The eye focuses light on the retina.</p>
<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The lens changes shape to focus light on the retina because of the ciliary muscles.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Light is refracted by the cornea and lens; the ciliary muscles contract or relax to change the tension in suspensory ligaments, altering the lens shape so light is focused precisely on the retina for a clear image.</p>
<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>It includes the main structures involved (cornea, lens, ciliary muscles, suspensory ligaments, retina).</li>
<li>It shows cause → effect connections between muscles contracting → ligaments slackening → lens shape changing → image formed on retina.</li>
<li>It uses correct terms and avoids vague descriptions.</li>
</ul>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent curved front layer that bends incoming light.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part of eye that changes pupil size.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The opening that controls light entering the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – Transparent structure that changes shape to focus light.
<strong>Retina</strong> – Light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye with photoreceptors.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that connect ciliary muscles to the lens.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different materials.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Cells in the retina that detect light and send signals to the brain.
