<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → the cornea refracts (bends) the light → the iris adjusts the pupil size controlling light intensity → the lens changes shape to focus light (accommodation) → light is focused on the retina → photoreceptors in the retina detect light and create nerve impulses sent to the brain.</strong></p>  

<ol>  
<li>Light enters through cornea (outer transparent layer)</li>  
<li>Cornea refracts light rays to begin focusing</li>  
<li>Iris controls pupil size, regulating light entry</li>  
<li>Lens fine-tunes focus by changing shape (accommodation)</li>  
<li>Focused light reaches retina</li>  
<li>Photoreceptors on retina detect light and send signals to brain</li>  
</ol>  

<p>👉 Notice this is a clear cause → effect chain explaining how light is controlled and focused.</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing to remember is that the eye's job is to focus light sharply on the retina so the brain can interpret what we see.</p>

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light by bending it to form a clear image on the retina.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea, which is curved and transparent.</li>  
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays towards the center of the eye.</li>  
<li>The iris controls how much light enters by adjusting pupil size (gets bigger in dim light, smaller in bright light).</li>  
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focus by changing its thickness depending on object distance — this process is called accommodation.</li>  
<li>After focusing, light reaches the retina, where photoreceptors detect the light and send electrical signals to the brain.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The cornea's curved shape allows it to refract light effectively → this starts focusing the light onto the retina.</li>  
<li>The iris controls pupil size → this regulates light intensity entering → protecting the retina and improving image clarity.</li>  
<li>The ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments change the lens shape (accommodation) → this adjusts the focal length → light is focused sharply on the retina regardless of object distance.</li>  
<li>Photoreceptors in the retina convert light into electrical impulses → these impulses travel to the brain → allowing us to interpret the visual scene.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the lens focuses light.” Instead, say “light is refracted by the cornea and lens, and the ciliary muscles change lens shape for accommodation to focus light on the retina.”</p>  
<p>👉 Connect cause → effect clearly; for example: “Ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker → light refracts more → near objects focused on retina.”</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying “The lens zooms in to focus light.”</p>  
<p>This is incorrect because the lens doesn't zoom; it changes shape through accommodation.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say this is: “The ciliary muscles contract or relax, changing the lens shape to focus light on the retina.”</p>  
<p>👉 Exam link: Using correct terminology like 'accommodation' and explaining the mechanism earns marks, while vague phrases lose them.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how the eye focuses light on the retina when looking at a nearby object. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. Ciliary muscles contract, which reduces tension on the suspensory ligaments.  
2. Suspensory ligaments slacken, allowing the lens to become thicker and more curved.  
3. The thicker lens refracts light rays more strongly.  
4. This stronger refraction focuses light precisely on the retina, forming a clear image of the near object.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent outer layer that refracts light entering the eye  
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part controlling pupil size to regulate light intensity  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Opening in the iris that allows light into the eye  
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure altering shape to focus light (accommodation)  
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer at the back containing photoreceptors to detect light  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that change tension on the lens to adjust shape  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Connect lens to ciliary muscles and control lens shape  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – Process of changing lens shape to focus on objects at different distances  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes between materials of different density  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cell in the retina that converts light into electrical signals
