9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system detects stimuli and rapidly coordinates responses by sending electrical impulses through specialised cells.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus, such as light, heat, or pressure, triggers receptors in the body.</li>
<li>These receptors send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the central nervous system (CNS).</li>
<li>The CNS processes the information; the brain and spinal cord are key parts of the CNS.</li>
<li>From the CNS, impulses travel along motor neurones to effectors—muscles or glands that respond.</li>
<li>The effector produces a response, like muscle contraction or gland secretion.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors detect light intensity).</li>
<li>Sensory neurones have dendrites to receive impulses and long axons to transmit electrical signals rapidly to the CNS.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors, enabling a response.</li>
<li>Myelin sheath around axons acts as insulation and speeds up transmission by saltatory conduction.</li>
<li>Synapses are tiny gaps where chemical neurotransmitters allow impulses to transfer between neurones.</li>
<li>Effectors are muscles that contract or glands that secrete hormones or enzymes to cause the response.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Explain how electrical impulses travel:</strong></h3>
<p>A stimulus activates receptors → receptors trigger electrical impulses in sensory neurones → impulses reach CNS → relay neurones pass impulses within CNS → motor neurones carry impulses to effectors → effectors respond.</p>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “messages. ” Use “electrical impulses” along named neurones, state direction: “sensory neurone carries impulse to CNS” and “motor neurone carries impulse to effectors.</p>
<p>” Include how effectors respond.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon from electrical loss.
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).
3. This reduces the time the impulse takes to travel along the neurone.
4. Faster transmission allows effectors to respond quickly, improving survival chances.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – a cell that detects a stimulus such as light, heat, or pressure.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – a change in the environment detected by receptors.
<strong>Response</strong> – a reaction produced by effectors after receiving signals.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – neurone in the CNS that connects sensory neurones to motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – a gap between neurones where chemicals transmit impulses.
<strong>CNS</strong> – central nervous system, made up of the brain and spinal cord.
<strong>PNS</strong> – peripheral nervous system; all nerves outside the CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – a muscle or gland that produces the response to a stimulus.
