<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Central Nervous System (CNS) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>This is the main pathway showing how information travels rapidly from detection to action:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Stimulus is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>Receptors send electrical impulses along sensory neurones to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the information.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses from the CNS to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce a response.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system enables fast electrical communication through specialised cells called neurones, connecting detection to action.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors in the skin or sense organs detect a specific stimulus (such as light, temperature, or pressure).</li>  
<li>These receptors convert the stimulus into electrical impulses that travel along neurones.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are adapted to detect specific stimuli; for example, temperature receptors have proteins sensitive to heat changes.</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites to carry impulses from receptors to the CNS quickly.</li>  
<li><strong>The CNS</strong>, composed of the brain and spinal cord, processes information and coordinates the response.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors; their axons are often myelinated to speed conduction.</li>  
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) respond by contracting or secreting substances, producing the appropriate response.</li>  
</ul>  

<p>👉 Explain how myelin sheaths increase transmission speed: They insulate axons, allowing impulses to jump between nodes of Ranvier, speeding up communication. This rapid response is vital for survival.</p>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “nerves send messages”; say “sensory neurones carry electrical impulses to the CNS, and motor neurones carry impulses away to effectors.”</p>  
<p>👉 Use terms like synapse to explain how impulses cross gaps chemically between neurones.</p>

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying “Nerves send messages to the brain” without mentioning the direction of impulses or the types of neurones involved.</p>  
<p>✅ The correct explanation must mention that “sensory neurones carry electrical impulses to the CNS (which includes the brain and spinal cord), and motor neurones carry impulses away to effectors” to gain full credit.</p>  
<p>👉 Students often forget that synapses are gaps where impulses cross chemically, not electrical wires, which makes their answers lose marks.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects stimuli and starts electrical impulses.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that causes a response.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The effect or action caused by a nervous impulse.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Connects sensory neurones to motor neurones in the CNS.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – Gap between neurones where chemicals transfer impulses.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central Nervous System, includes brain and spinal cord.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral Nervous System, all nerves outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to impulses to cause an action.
