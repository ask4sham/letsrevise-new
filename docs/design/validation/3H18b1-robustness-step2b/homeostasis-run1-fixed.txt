<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works through receptors detecting a stimulus → signals sent to a coordination centre that processes the information → signals to effectors (muscles or glands) which respond to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Step-by-step cause → effect chain:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A change (stimulus) moves conditions away from the set optimum value.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this change and send information to the coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes the signals and decides the response.</li>
<li>Effectors respond to correct the change.</li>
<li>The internal conditions return to the optimum level.</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the body continuously monitors its internal environment and makes adjustments to keep conditions stable.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus is any change that moves a condition away from its optimum (for example, core temperature rising).</li>
<li>Receptors detect these stimuli. For temperature, these are thermoreceptors in the skin and brain.</li>
<li>The coordination centre, such as the hypothalamus in the brain, receives and processes this information.</li>
<li>Effectors, like sweat glands or muscles controlling blood vessel diameter, produce a response to reverse the change.</li>
<li>The response restores the condition to the optimum level, ensuring enzymes and cells work efficiently.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Thermoreceptors have nerve endings sensitive to temperature changes → they detect when temperature rises above optimum.</li>
<li>The hypothalamus acts as a coordination centre because it processes signals and coordinates the response.</li>
<li>Sweat glands secrete sweat, which evaporates → evaporation removes heat energy → body temperature falls back to optimum.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In your answers, always name the receptor, coordination centre, and effector, then explain exactly how the effector’s action helps return conditions to the optimum.</p>
<p>👉 For example, say “Thermoreceptors detect the rise in temperature → hypothalamus activates sweat glands → sweat evaporates removing heat → temperature returns to optimum.”</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the blood glucose level is controlled when it becomes too high in the blood. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Receptors in the pancreas detect the high blood glucose level.
2. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin.
3. Insulin causes liver and muscle cells (effectors) to absorb glucose and convert it to glycogen.
4. Blood glucose levels fall back to the optimum, restoring homeostasis.
This clear cause → effect chain shows each step leading to the return to a stable blood glucose level.
</details>

Negative feedback means when a condition moves away from the optimum, receptors detect the change and send information to the coordination centre. </details>

24— SUMMARY
Paste into: Text (concept)
<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li>Homeostasis is the process that keeps internal body conditions steady at optimum levels.</li>
<li>Receptors detect stimuli — changes in conditions.</li>
<li>Coordination centres (brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) process information and decide responses.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) act to reverse the change and restore optimal conditions.</li>
<li>Key examples include regulating body temperature, blood glucose, and water balance.</li>
<li>Negative feedback loops switch off responses once conditions return to optimum.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Remember:</strong> Use precise terms in exams and always explain the cause → effect sequence.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions essential for life.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change that moves a condition away from the optimum.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Detects a stimulus and sends information.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information and coordinates the response.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Produces a response to restore balance.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken to return conditions to optimum.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best condition for enzymes and cells to work.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process that reverses a change to return to optimum.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers used in homeostasis (e.g., insulin).
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein catalysts sensitive to conditions controlled by homeostasis.
