5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The basic model of homeostasis involves: receptors detecting changes (stimuli) → coordination centres processing this information → effectors responding to restore conditions to optimum.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Structure → Function chain:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A stimulus causes a change in an internal condition, moving it away from the optimum.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this specific change (e.g., temperature receptors in the skin).</li>
<li>The receptors send signals to a coordination centre (e.g., brain or pancreas) that processes the information.</li>
<li>The coordination centre sends commands to effectors (muscles or glands).</li>
<li>Effectors produce a response that counteracts the change, returning conditions to the optimum.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></p>
<p>👉 Always mention specific receptor, coordination centre, and effector in your answers, linked by a clear cause → effect chain ending with returning to optimum.</p>

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is homeostasis keeps internal conditions steady so the body functions properly.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Your body continuously monitors internal conditions like temperature, blood glucose, and water balance.</li>
<li>When a condition moves away from optimum, receptors detect this stimulus and send a signal to a coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes the change and activates effectors to bring conditions back.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect temperature changes → send nerve impulses to the hypothalamus (brain).</li>
<li>Hypothalamus acts as coordination centre → sends signals to sweat glands (effectors) to increase sweat production.</li>
<li>Sweat evaporates → removes heat → lowers body temperature to optimum.</li>
</ul>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The regulation of internal conditions to maintain a stable internal environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in internal or external conditions that is detected by receptors.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or group of cells that detect stimuli and send information to the coordination centre.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information from receptors and sends signals to effectors (examples: brain, spinal cord, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to restore optimum conditions.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to counteract the change detected by receptors.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal condition for cells and enzymes to function effectively.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control system where a change away from optimum initiates responses that reverse the change.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers released into the blood to coordinate responses over longer periods (example insulin).
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological catalysts that work best under specific internal conditions maintained by homeostasis.
