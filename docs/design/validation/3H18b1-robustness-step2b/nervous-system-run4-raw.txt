9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong> The nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses by electrical signals.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect changes (stimuli) inside or outside the body.</li>  
<li>Electrical impulses travel along neurones, connecting receptors to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes the information and sends impulses to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce the physical response.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are specialised to detect specific stimuli to convert into electrical impulses.</li>  
<li>Sensory neurones have dendrites that receive impulses from receptors and a long axon to send impulses to the CNS quickly.</li>  
<li>Relay neurones in the CNS connect sensory input to motor output.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones have long axons to carry impulses to effectors efficiently.</li>  
<li>Myelin sheath covers axons, insulating them to speed up impulse transmission via saltatory conduction.</li>  
<li>Synapses between neurones allow impulses to cross chemically, ensuring controlled communication.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “nerves send messages.” Instead say “Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses to the CNS, and motor neurones carry impulses away to effectors causing a response.”</p>  
<p>👉 Use the full pathway: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response for full marks.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how myelinated neurones transmit electrical impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon preventing ion loss.  
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the sheath called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).  
3. This reduces the loss of electrical signal along the neurone.  
4. Therefore, impulses travel faster, allowing quicker responses to stimuli.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects changes in the environment (stimuli).</p>  
<p><strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that can be detected by receptors.</p>  
<p><strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by the body after processing a stimulus.</p>  
<p><strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.</p>  
<p><strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Connects sensory and motor neurones within the CNS.</p>  
<p><strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.</p>  
<p><strong>Synapse</strong> – The small gap where impulses pass chemically between neurones.</p>  
<p><strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, consisting of the brain and spinal cord.</p>  
<p><strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, the network of nerves outside the CNS.</p>  
<p><strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response after receiving impulses.</p>
