<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The key pathway in homeostasis is:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A <strong>stimulus</strong> changes an internal condition.</li>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this change.</li>
<li>The information goes to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> (like the brain or pancreas).</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles or glands) produce a response.</li>
<li>The response restores the condition to its <strong>optimum</strong> level.</li>
</ol>
<p>For example: <strong>Receptors → Coordination centre → Effectors → Response → Optimum restored</strong></p>

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions steady for cells to work properly.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>If an internal condition changes (like temperature or blood sugar), this is a <strong>stimulus</strong> detected by specialised <strong>receptors</strong>.</li>
<li>The receptors send signals to a <strong>coordination centre</strong>, which processes the information and decides how to fix the problem.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> then produce a response, such as muscles contracting or glands releasing hormones, to restore optimum conditions.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> (e.g. thermoreceptors in skin) are adapted to detect specific stimuli, allowing precise sensing of changes.</li>
<li>The <strong>brain</strong> coordinates and communicates signals quickly to effectors, enabling fast responses.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> like sweat glands or muscles respond to reverse the change, restoring the optimum internal state.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In exam answers, say exactly which <strong>receptors</strong> detect the stimulus, name the <strong>coordination centre</strong>, and describe the <strong>effector</strong> response clearly, including how the response restores optimum conditions by negative feedback.</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Understanding homeostasis explains how organisms stay alive and function in changing environments, which is a key biological concept often tested in GCSE exams.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>For command words like "explain" or "describe," always include the pathway: name the <strong>receptors</strong>, then the <strong>coordination centre</strong>, and the <strong>effectors</strong>, followed by a clear cause → effect explanation of how the condition returns to the optimum.</p>
<p>Avoid vague statements such as "the body reacts" or "it just happens," as these do not earn marks.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>
<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The body sweats to cool down when hot.</p>
<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The body sweats because it is too hot, so sweat cools the skin.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Thermoreceptors in the skin detect an increase in core temperature. The hypothalamus (coordination centre) receives this information and activates sweat glands (effectors). Sweat evaporates from the skin surface, removing heat energy, which cools the body and returns temperature to the optimum. This is a negative feedback mechanism that stops sweating once temperature is normal.</p>
<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>It names the receptor, coordination centre, and effector.</li>
<li>It explains the process (evaporation removes heat).</li>
<li>It includes the concept of negative feedback and stopping the response.</li>
</ul>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose concentration is too high. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Receptors in the pancreas detect high blood glucose levels.
2. The pancreas (coordination centre) responds by releasing insulin into the blood.
3. Insulin causes liver and muscle cells (effectors) to take up glucose and store it as glycogen.
4. As glucose is removed from the blood, blood glucose concentration returns to the optimum level.
This is a negative feedback mechanism that stops insulin release when levels are normal.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Keeping internal conditions stable despite changes.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell or organ that detects changes (stimuli).
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes receptor information and sends signals (e.g., brain, pancreas).
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to restore balance.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action that reverses the change and restores optimum.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best level or conditions for enzymes and cells to work.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process where the response stops once the optimum is reached.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – A chemical messenger released by glands to regulate body functions.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein molecules that speed up biochemical reactions and need specific conditions.
