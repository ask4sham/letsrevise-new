5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>Energy is stored → transferred through pathways → turned into useful output energy and wasted energy; total energy is conserved.</strong></p>  
<p>In more detail:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Energy starts in a store (e.g., chemical energy in fuel).</li>  
<li>It moves via a transfer pathway (e.g., electrical wires, mechanical forces, heating).</li>  
<li>It ends partly as useful energy (e.g., kinetic energy moving a machine) and partly as wasted energy (usually thermal energy lost to the surroundings).</li>  
<li>The total energy before and after transfer stays the same (law of conservation of energy).</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Energy</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let’s break down how energy works in systems using clear physics.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is never used up; it just moves from one store to another or transfers to different forms.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens physically:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy stored in objects can be in many forms—like chemical energy inside fuels or batteries, gravitational energy when an object is high up, or kinetic energy when it’s moving.</li>  
<li>This stored energy moves through paths—such as electrical currents in wires, mechanical force through moving parts, or heating through conduction/convection.</li>  
<li>During transfer, not all energy does useful work; some is wasted as thermal energy due to friction or resistance.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Chemical store in fuel → burned → transfers energy → kinetic energy moves car → friction causes thermal energy waste → total energy conserved.</li>  
<li>Energy input (work done) by person pushing a box → mechanical energy transfer → box moves (kinetic energy) → some thermal energy lost in friction with floor.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain what the energy starts as (store), how it moves (transfer pathway), and what the useful vs wasted outputs are, using clear terms like “transfers” and “conserved” to earn full marks.</p>

Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say energy is "used up" or "lost" without explanation.</p>

<p>👉 Explaining that energy is “wasted” rather than “used up” shows deeper understanding and is rewarded in exams.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Energy transfer and conservation principles explain how all machines, engines, and natural processes work — this is fundamental to engineering, environmental science, and everyday life.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exams, don’t just list energy forms or say energy is 'lost'; always explain how energy transfers from one store to another, specifying which transfers are useful and which produce wasted energy, and mention total energy remains constant.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is used up in a machine, so it stops working.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is transferred to useful work, but some energy is wasted as heat because of friction.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is transferred from chemical stores to kinetic energy to move the machine, but some energy is wasted as thermal energy due to friction; the total energy is conserved.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the direction of energy transfer and specifies the stores involved.</li>  
<li>It identifies the cause of wasted energy (friction) and the form wasted energy takes (thermal).</li>  
<li>It uses correct scientific terms and confirms energy conservation, which examiners reward.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A kettle transfers 2000 J of electrical energy to heat water, but 400 J is lost as thermal energy to the surroundings. Calculate the efficiency of the kettle.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Calculate useful energy output:  
Useful energy = Total input energy - Wasted energy = 2000 J - 400 J = 1600 J  

Step 2: Write efficiency formula:  
Efficiency = (Useful energy output / Total energy input) × 100%  

Step 3: Substitute values:  
Efficiency = (1600 J / 2000 J) × 100% = 0.8 × 100% = 80%  

Step 4: Final answer:  
The kettle is 80% efficient.  
</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Describe the energy transfer steps when a cyclist pedals uphill and then coast downhill.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

When pedaling uphill, chemical energy in muscles is transferred to kinetic energy of the bike and gravitational potential energy as the cyclist gains height. Some energy is wasted as thermal energy due to friction and air resistance. When coasting downhill, gravitational potential energy transfers into kinetic energy, and again some energy is wasted as heat due to friction and air resistance. Total energy remains conserved.  
</details>

23 — SUMMARY

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy is the ability to do work and is stored in different forms like kinetic and chemical energy.</li>  
<li>Energy transfers happen through pathways such as mechanical movement, electrical circuits, or heating.</li>  
<li>According to conservation of energy, total energy remains constant in a closed system.</li>  
<li>Energy efficiency is the ratio of useful output to total input energy, always less than 100% due to wasted energy.</li>  
<li>Wasted energy usually takes the form of thermal energy caused by friction or resistance.</li>  
<li>Explaining energy in terms of stores, transfer, and outputs is critical for exam success.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: energy is never lost, only transferred and conserved.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always explain cause → effect for energy transfers and specify both useful and wasted energy forms.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Energy</strong> – The ability to do work or cause change, measured in joules (J).  
<strong>Energy store</strong> – Where energy is kept in an object or system (e.g., chemical, kinetic).  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – The movement of energy from one store or object to another.  
<strong>Useful energy</strong> – Energy transferred in a form that does the intended work.  
<strong>Wasted energy</strong> – Energy transferred that does not do useful work, often lost as heat.  
<strong>Conservation of energy</strong> – The principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred.  
<strong>Efficiency</strong> – A measure of how much input energy is usefully transferred (usually expressed as a percentage).  
<strong>Joule (J)</strong> – The unit of energy.  
<strong>Mechanical transfer</strong> – Energy transferred by a force moving an object.  
<strong>Thermal energy</strong> – Energy associated with the temperature of an object, often wasted due to friction or resistance.
