3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A quadratic is an algebraic expression or equation involving the square of an unknown variable, usually written in the form <strong>ax² + bx + c = 0</strong>, where a ≠ 0.</p>  
<p>👉 The graph of a quadratic equation is a curve called a <strong>parabola</strong>, which is symmetrical and shaped like a "U" (or an upside down "U").</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratics are everywhere: in physics for projectile motion, in economics for profit maximisation, and in pure maths for understanding graphs that are not straight lines.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, understanding quadratics means solving a wide range of problems from graph sketching, equation solving, to interpreting real-world contexts.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 The key GCSE model for quadratics is: </p>  
<ol>  
<li>Start with the quadratic expression in standard form: <strong>ax² + bx + c</strong></li>  
<li>Plot or sketch the graph on coordinate axes using known points or vertex.</li>  
<li>Identify the <strong>vertex</strong> (turning point) of the parabola, its <strong>axis of symmetry</strong>, and intercepts with axes.</li>  
<li>Solve quadratic equations by <strong>factorising</strong>, <strong>completing the square</strong>, or using the <strong>quadratic formula</strong>.</li>  
</ol>  
<p>👉 This chain connects the algebraic form → geometric graph → solution points.</p>

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>Factorising: Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0 by factorising to (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0 so x = -2 or -3.</li>  
<li>Graph sketch: Plot y = x² - 4x + 3, find vertex at x = 2, y = -1, and x-intercepts at x = 1 and 3.</li>  
<li>Quadratic formula: Use formula to solve 2x² + 3x - 2 = 0 when factorising is hard.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Quadratics</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that quadratics always create a U-shaped curve called a parabola, which can open upwards or downwards depending on the sign of <strong>a</strong>.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The coefficient <strong>a</strong> controls the shape and direction of the parabola — if <em>a</em> is positive, the parabola opens upwards; if negative, downwards.</li>  
<li>The terms <strong>b</strong> and <strong>c</strong> shift the parabola left/right and up/down respectively, changing where it crosses the axes and where its vertex lies.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Quadratic formula: <em>x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a</em> lets you find the exact roots even when factorising isn’t easy.</li>  
<li>The vertex’s x-coordinate is at <em>-b/2a</em>, and plugging this into the equation gives the vertex’s y-coordinate.</li>  
<li>The discriminant <em>b² - 4ac</em> tells you the number of real solutions: if positive, two roots; zero, one root; negative, no real roots (complex roots).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When asked to solve or graph a quadratic, always start by identifying <em>a, b, c</em>, then explain your method clearly step-by-step and state all final answers precisely.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing the signs in the quadratic formula, especially missing the minus before <em>b</em> or mixing up when to use plus or minus.</p>  
<p>👉 Students often forget that <em>a ≠ 0</em>, which is essential for it to be a quadratic.</p>  
<p>👉 Do NOT just write the solutions without showing substitution — always write the full formula with numbers before calculating.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is: "Substitute <em>a, b, c</em> into the formula exactly, then calculate the discriminant carefully." This earns method marks and avoids sign errors.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)  

Process:  
Solving a quadratic using the quadratic formula

Step 1:  
Identify coefficients a, b, and c from the equation ax² + bx + c = 0.

↓

Step 2:  
Calculate the discriminant, D = b² - 4ac.

↓

Step 3:  
Use the formula x = (-b ± √D) / 2a to find the two possible roots.

↓

Step 4:  
Simplify the values to get exact or decimal solutions.

Exam link:  
Explaining every substitution step and showing the discriminant determines root types is essential for full marks in exams.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratics often form the bigger problem you need to solve in an exam, from graph sketching to calculating exact roots. Understanding how to interpret and use features like the vertex and roots can boost your marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down the full quadratic formula with the substituted values for <em>a, b</em>, and <em>c</em>. Show your working clearly, and state the discriminant before finding the roots. This approach guarantees method marks even if the arithmetic slips.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>It just gives the root answers without showing any formula or discriminant calculation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Shows the formula and some numbers substituted but makes minor calculation or sign errors.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Clearly writes the quadratic formula with all values substituted; calculates the discriminant correctly; solves both roots with ± signs shown; gives answers to the required accuracy.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Full clarity of method.</li>  
<li>Correct use of formula and discriminant.</li>  
<li>Shows understanding, not just results.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — FINAL MEMORY RULE  
Paste into: Final memory rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>💡 Key Insight</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When dealing with quadratics, always remember this four-step chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li><strong>Identify a, b, and c</strong> in the standard form ax² + bx + c.</li>  
<li><strong>Calculate the discriminant</strong> (b² - 4ac) to know the roots' nature.</li>  
<li><strong>Find the vertex</strong> at x = -b/(2a) and plug in to get y.</li>  
<li><strong>Use factorising or quadratic formula</strong> to solve for exact roots.</li>  
</ol>  
<p>👉 Holding this chain in your head helps you work logically, avoid mistakes, and get full marks efficiently.</p>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write down the standard form of a quadratic expression.</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve the quadratic equation x² - 7x + 10 = 0 by factorising.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Use the quadratic formula to solve 2x² + 3x - 2 = 0. Show all your working, including the discriminant.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> The height (in metres) of a ball thrown upwards is modelled by y = -5x² + 20x + 15, where x is time in seconds. Find:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>The time when the ball reaches its maximum height.</li>  
<li>The maximum height of the ball.</li>  
<li>The time(s) when the ball is at height 15 metres.</li>  
</ul>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

<strong>Q1:</strong> ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0.<br /><br />  

<strong>Q2:</strong>  
Factorise: x² - 7x + 10 = (x - 5)(x - 2) = 0.  
Set each bracket to zero: x - 5 = 0 → x = 5, x - 2 = 0 → x = 2.<br /><br />  

<strong>Q3:</strong>  
a = 2, b = 3, c = -2.  
Discriminant = b² - 4ac = 3² - 4×2×(-2) = 9 + 16 = 25.  
x = (-3 ± √25) / (2×2) = (-3 ± 5)/4.  
Roots: x = (2)/4 = 0.5, x = (-8)/4 = -2.<br /><br />  

<strong>Q4:</strong>  
a = -5, b = 20, c = 15.  
Time of max height (vertex x) = -b / (2a) = -20 / (2×-5) = -20/-10 = 2 seconds.  
Height at x=2: y = -5(2)² + 20(2) + 15 = -20 + 40 + 15 = 35 metres maximum height.  
Solve for y=15: -5x² + 20x + 15 = 15 → -5x² + 20x = 0 → -5x(x - 4) = 0.  
Roots: x = 0 or x = 4 seconds (times at height 15 metres).  

</details>  

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Quadratic equations are in the form ax² + bx + c, with a ≠ 0.</li>  
<li>Their graph is a parabola with key features: vertex, axis of symmetry, roots, and y-intercept.</li>  
<li>Factorising is the quickest way to solve if possible; otherwise, use the quadratic formula.</li>  
<li>The discriminant b² - 4ac tells you how many real roots there are.</li>  
<li>Plotting or sketching graphs helps interpret solutions and application problems.</li>  
<li>Avoid sign errors in formulas and always show your working clearly for exam method marks.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: “Determine a, b, c → find discriminant → locate vertex → solve roots” as your problem-solving chain.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always state final answers clearly and check units or context where needed.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Quadratic equation</strong> – An equation involving x² as the highest power.  
<strong>Parabola</strong> – The U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.  
<strong>Vertex</strong> – The highest or lowest point on a parabola.  
<strong>Axis of symmetry</strong> – The vertical line that divides the parabola into two equal halves.  
<strong>Roots</strong> – The values of x where the quadratic equals zero (x-intercepts).  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number multiplied by a variable term.  
<strong>Discriminant</strong> – The part under the square root in the quadratic formula (b² - 4ac).  
<strong>Factorising</strong> – Expressing a quadratic as a product of two binomials.  
<strong>Quadratic formula</strong> – The formula x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / (2a) used to solve any quadratic.  
<strong>Intercept</strong> – The points where the graph meets an axis (x or y-axis).
