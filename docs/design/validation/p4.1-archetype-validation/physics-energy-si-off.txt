9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Energy</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that energy exists in different stores and moves through systems but is always conserved overall. This helps us explain many physical phenomena.</p>  

<h3><strong>Energy stores & transfers</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The main energy stores are kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, and nuclear.</li>  
<li>Energy transfers happen when forces do work, causing changes in motion, temperature, or shape.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Energy transfer examples and formulae</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Kinetic energy (moving objects): <em>KE = ½ × mass × speed²</em></li>  
<li>Gravitational potential energy (any object at height): <em>GPE = mass × gravitational field strength × height</em></li>  
<li>Elastic potential energy (in stretched or compressed springs): <em>EPE = ½ × spring constant × extension²</em></li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Efficiency and energy loss</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Not all input energy transfers usefully; some is wasted, usually as thermal energy due to friction or resistance.</li>  
<li>Efficiency = (useful energy output ÷ total energy input) × 100%</li>  
<li>Devices with higher efficiency waste less energy.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Don’t just name energy stores or say ‘energy is lost’. Explain <em>what</em> causes the transfer (like friction) and <em>where</em> that energy goes (to thermal store). Use formulae to calculate exact changes when numbers are given.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying energy is “lost” or “used up”. This is wrong because energy is always conserved. It’s better to say energy is “transferred to a less useful form” like thermal energy, often wasted due to friction.</p>  
<p>👉 Another common error is mixing up kinetic and potential energy formulas or units — remember kinetic depends on speed squared, potential depends on height.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Energy transfer in a falling object  

Step 1:  
The object has gravitational potential energy because of its height above ground.  

↓  

Step 2:  
As it falls, gravitational potential energy decreases because height decreases.  

↓  

Step 3:  
The lost potential energy transfers to kinetic energy, increasing the object’s speed.  

↓  

Step 4:  
On hitting the ground, some kinetic energy transfers to thermal energy and sound energy (waste).

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding energy transfers and conservation is central to physics, explaining everyday events and engineering problems, from powering cars to heating homes.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exams, don’t just list energy stores or say energy is lost. Always explain the cause (e.g., friction, electrical resistance), what happens to the energy, and use correct terminology like 'energy transferred to thermal store'. Show the flow clearly.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is lost when the object moves.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is lost because it changes into heat when the object moves.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy is conserved but transferred from kinetic to thermal energy due to friction between the object and air, causing energy to be wasted as heat.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It states the cause (friction).</li>  
<li>It explains what type of energy it transfers to (thermal).</li>  
<li>It explicitly states energy conservation, avoiding saying energy is ‘lost’.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A 2 kg ball is thrown straight up to a height of 5 m. Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained. (g = 9.8 N/kg)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

GPE = mass × g × height = 2 × 9.8 × 5 = 98 J  

The ball gains 98 joules of gravitational potential energy when it reaches the height.  
</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Explain why a moving car eventually stops even if the driver doesn’t apply the brakes.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

The car’s kinetic energy is transferred to thermal energy in the brakes and surrounding air due to friction and air resistance. This energy transfer causes the car to slow down and eventually stop, as energy is conserved but spread into less useful forms.  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the unit of energy?</p>  
<p><strong>A1:</strong> Joule (J)</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> A 3 kg object is lifted 4 m. Calculate the increase in gravitational potential energy. (g = 10 N/kg)</p>  
<p><strong>A2:</strong> GPE = 3 × 10 × 4 = 120 J</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain why energy efficiency can never be 100% in real machines.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Energy input is always partly transferred to waste energy stores, mostly thermal due to friction or resistance.<br/>  
This means not all energy is converted into useful output.<br/>  
Therefore, efficiency is less than 100%.  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A compressed spring has an elastic potential energy of 50 J. When released, it transfers this energy into kinetic energy and thermal energy due to friction. Explain the energy transfers that take place and why energy conservation still applies.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
The elastic potential energy stored in the compressed spring transfers to kinetic energy as the spring moves a mass.<br/>  
Some energy is also transferred to thermal energy because friction converts part of the energy to heat.<br/>  
Energy conservation means total energy before release equals total energy after transfer, just split into different stores.<br/>  
Energy is not lost, but some is less useful thermal energy.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Energy</strong> – The ability to do work or cause change, measured in joules (J).  
<strong>Energy store</strong> – Where energy is held in a system (e.g., kinetic, potential).  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – Movement of energy from one store to another.  
<strong>Conservation of energy</strong> – Energy can’t be created or destroyed, only transferred.  
<strong>Kinetic energy</strong> – Energy stored in moving objects.  
<strong>Gravitational potential energy</strong> – Energy stored due to an object’s height.  
<strong>Elastic potential energy</strong> – Energy stored in stretched or compressed objects.  
<strong>Efficiency</strong> – Ratio of useful energy output to total energy input (%).  
<strong>Wasted energy</strong> – Energy transferred to less useful forms, often thermal.  
<strong>Work done</strong> – The energy transferred when a force moves an object (joules).
