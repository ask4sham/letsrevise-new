9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Ratio</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When working with ratio, the key thing is to see ratio as a comparison, not just numbers written with a colon.</p>  
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Ratios tell us how many parts of each quantity there are relative to each other.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>You start with quantities, write their ratio, then simplify it to simplest form.</li>  
<li>To divide amounts in a given ratio, add the parts to find total parts, then split the quantity accordingly.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Write original ratio → Simplify the ratio → Calculate total parts → Divide amount by total parts → Multiply parts to find shares.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When you answer ratio questions, always show the simplifying step and calculation for each part – this chain is what examiners look for: reason → calculation → answer.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is treating the ratio numbers as quantities to add or subtract directly without working out total parts first.</p>  
<p>For example, dividing £50 in ratio 2:3 means total parts are 2+3=5, so each part is £10; ignoring this leads to wrong answers.</p>  
<p>Students often confuse simplifying a ratio with dividing quantities; remember to simplify ratios first, then divide amounts accordingly.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
How to divide an amount in a given ratio

Step 1: Write the ratio and add the parts to get the total number of parts.  
↓  
Step 2: Divide the total amount by the total parts to find the value of one part.  
↓  
Step 3: Multiply one part value by each part of the ratio for each share.  
↓  
Step 4: Check your parts add up to the total amount as a verification step.  

Exam link:  
This method is exactly how you show clear working for ratio division questions in the exam.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Ratio questions come up often on the Higher Tier. Understanding and showing every step clearly – simplifying ratio, finding one part value, then calculating each share – gets you straightforward marks.</strong></p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write the total parts and show division by total parts explicitly. This proves you understand how ratio works and helps avoid careless mistakes. If asked, check your answer adds up to the total given.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio is 2:3, so one person gets £2 and the other £3.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The total ratio parts are 5, so dividing £50 by 5 gives £10 per part. Then multiply £10 by 2 and £10 by 3 to get the shares.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio 2:3 has total parts 5. Dividing £50 ÷ 5 gives £10 per part. Therefore, the first share is 2 × £10 = £20 and the second is 3 × £10 = £30, which add to £50.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It shows the total parts and division step explicitly.</li>  
<li>It uses clear multiplication to find shares.</li>  
<li>It checks the final sums add to the total amount, showing comprehension.</li>  
</ul>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Calculate how to divide £84 in the ratio 5:7. Show full working.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Add ratio parts: 5 + 7 = 12 parts total.  
Step 2: Divide total amount by total parts: £84 ÷ 12 = £7 per part.  
Step 3: Multiply each part:  
5 parts × £7 = £35  
7 parts × £7 = £49  
Step 4: Check: £35 + £49 = £84, correct!  

Answer: £35 and £49.  

</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
If two numbers are in the ratio 7:9 and their total is 128, what is the smaller number?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Total parts = 7 + 9 = 16  
One part = 128 ÷ 16 = 8  
Smaller number = 7 × 8 = 56  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Simplify the ratio 10:15.</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Divide 42 into the ratio 3:4.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A map scale uses ratio 1:250,000. What real distance does 4 cm on the map represent? Show your working.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Two ingredients are mixed in ratio 2:5. You have 28 g of the first ingredient. Calculate the total mass of the mixture and how much of the second ingredient is needed.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: Simplify 10:15 by dividing both by 5 → 2:3.  
Q2: Total parts 3 + 4 = 7; 42 ÷ 7 = 6; shares: 3 × 6 = 18 and 4 × 6 = 24.  
Q3: Scale 1:250,000 means 1 cm = 250,000 cm; 4 cm = 4 × 250,000 = 1,000,000 cm = 10,000 m = 10 km.  
Q4: Ratio 2:5 means total parts 7. One part = 28 ÷ 2 = 14 g. Total mass = 7 × 14 = 98 g. Amount of second ingredient = 5 × 14 = 70 g.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Ratio</strong> – A comparison showing the relative size of two or more values.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – Reducing a ratio to its smallest whole numbers by dividing both parts by their greatest common divisor.  
<strong>Proportion</strong> – The equal relationship between ratios or amounts.  
<strong>Share</strong> – To divide a total amount into parts according to a ratio.  
<strong>Divide</strong> – To split an amount into parts based on a ratio.  
<strong>Part</strong> – One component or segment in a ratio.  
<strong>Equivalent ratio</strong> – A ratio that has the same proportion but different numbers.  
<strong>Greatest common divisor (GCD)</strong> – The largest number that divides two numbers without remainder.  
<strong>Total parts</strong> – The sum of all parts in a ratio, used as the divisor to split quantities.  
<strong>Scale</strong> – A proportional relationship used to resize or convert measures.
