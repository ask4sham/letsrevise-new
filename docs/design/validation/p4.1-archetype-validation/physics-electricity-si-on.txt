5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>Electric circuit organisation model:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>Electric current flows continuously through conductors.</li>  
<li>In series circuits, current is the same through all components; voltage divides among components.</li>  
<li>In parallel circuits, voltage is the same across each branch; current divides among branches.</li>  
<li>Total resistance in series adds up; in parallel, total resistance decreases.</li>  
<li>Power in a circuit depends on current and voltage: P = IV.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Electricity Organisation</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong></p>  
<p>Current, voltage, and resistance behave differently in series and parallel circuits, controlling how energy is supplied and used.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens in series circuits:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The current is the same through every component because there is only one path for charge to flow.</li>  
<li>The voltage across components adds up to the total voltage supplied by the battery.</li>  
<li>Total resistance increases because the resistors add up: <strong>Rtotal = R1 + R2 + ...</strong>.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>What happens in parallel circuits:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Voltage across each branch is the same as the battery voltage because each branch connects directly across the power supply.</li>  
<li>The total current supplied divides between the branches; the sum of branch currents equals total current.</li>  
<li>Total resistance decreases because the current has multiple paths: <strong>1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ...</strong>.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Formula relationships and electricity flow:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Ohm’s law relates voltage, current, and resistance: <strong>V = IR</strong>.</li>  
<li>Electrical power, the rate of energy transfer, is <strong>P = IV</strong>.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Use clear cause → effect chains: for example, “Because the current is the same in series, the voltage divides, so…” This proves understanding beyond just quoting facts.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say current is 'used up' or decreases along the circuit in series. Actually, current is the same everywhere in a series circuit because charge flows continuously.</p>  
<p>👉 Students often confuse voltage and current in parallel circuits by thinking current is the same in all branches; it is not — current splits depending on resistance.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is “Current is the same in series because there’s one path, and voltage divides; in parallel, voltage is the same, and current divides between branches.” This earns marks in explanations.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Calculating total resistance in series and parallel

Step 1:  
Identify whether resistors are connected in series or parallel.

↓

Step 2:  
For series, add resistances directly: Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3.

↓

Step 3:  
For parallel, calculate reciprocal sum: 1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3.

↓

Step 4:  
Use Ohm’s law (V = IR) to find current or voltage as needed.

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A circuit has three resistors in series: 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 5 Ω. The battery provides 10 V. Calculate:  
a) The total resistance  
b) The current flowing in the circuit  
c) The voltage across the 3 Ω resistor

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
a) Total resistance in series = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 Ω.  

b) Using Ohm's law I = V / R_total = 10 V / 10 Ω = 1 A.  

c) Voltage across 3 Ω resistor = I × R = 1 A × 3 Ω = 3 V.  
</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<p>👉 The organisation of electricity in circuits depends critically on the arrangement of components, which dictates how current, voltage, and resistance interact. Understanding these relationships lets us design and predict circuit behaviour.</p>  
<p>👉 Series circuits have one path—current stays constant, voltage splits, resistance adds up.</p>  
<p>👉 Parallel circuits have multiple paths—voltage stays constant, current splits, resistance decreases.</p>  
<p>👉 These patterns explain device operation and help prevent common errors in circuit analysis.</p>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What quantity is measured in amperes?</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> State two differences between series and parallel circuits in terms of current and voltage.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A 6 V battery powers two 3 Ω resistors connected in parallel. Calculate the total resistance and total current supplied by the battery.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Explain why the brightness of bulbs changes when one bulb is removed in a series circuit compared to a parallel circuit.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: Current is measured in amperes.  

Q2: In series circuits, current is the same through all components but voltage divides; in parallel circuits, voltage is the same across each branch but current divides between branches.  

Q3:  
- Total resistance in parallel: 1/R_total = 1/3 + 1/3 = 2/3 → R_total = 3/2 = 1.5 Ω.  
- Current: I = V / R_total = 6 V / 1.5 Ω = 4 A.  

Q4:  
- In series, removing one bulb breaks the circuit, stopping current and bulbs go out.  
- In parallel, bulbs are on separate branches; removing one does not break other branches, so remaining bulbs stay bright.  
- In series, current is the same and depends on the whole loop; in parallel, current divides and bulbs operate independently.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Current</strong> – the flow of electric charge measured in amperes (A).</p>  
<p><strong>Voltage</strong> – potential difference; energy per unit charge supplied measured in volts (V).</p>  
<p><strong>Resistance</strong> – property opposing current flow measured in ohms (Ω).</p>  
<p><strong>Series circuit</strong> – circuit with components connected end-to-end in one path.</p>  
<p><strong>Parallel circuit</strong> – circuit with branches providing multiple paths for current.</p>  
<p><strong>Ohm’s law</strong> – formula relating voltage, current, and resistance (V = IR).</p>  
<p><strong>Power</strong> – rate of energy transfer in a circuit (P = IV).</p>  
<p><strong>Total resistance</strong> – combined resistance of all components in a circuit.</p>  
<p><strong>Branch</strong> – one path in a parallel circuit.</p>  
<p><strong>Potential difference</strong> – synonymous with voltage, energy difference per charge.</p>
