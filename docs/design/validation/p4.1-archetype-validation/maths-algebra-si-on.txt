9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Algebra</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that algebra is a methodical process to find unknowns or express relationships clearly.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Start by identifying what you are asked to find (the unknown variable).</li>  
<li>Use inverse operations like subtracting, adding, multiplying, or dividing to isolate the variable.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Expression → simplify by combining like terms → easier to interpret and solve problems.</li>  
<li>Equation → rearrange using inverse operations → variable isolated → solution found.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Show every step explicitly: state your operation, write the new equation, and confirm your final answer is checked and consistent.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up simplifying an expression with solving an equation. Simplifying means reducing expressions without solving for a value.</p>  
<p>Students often confuse "+" and "−" signs when rearranging formulae, leading to wrong answers.</p>  
<p>👉 Always perform inverse operations carefully and check each step to avoid sign errors, which can lose marks in exams.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra is fundamental in GCSE Maths because it appears not just by itself but woven through geometry, sequences, graphs, and problem-solving questions. Understanding algebra gives you a tool to solve many question types confidently.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exams, don’t just write answers — show every step clearly. Use inverse operations carefully and write checks by substitution. This shows your working and can gain method marks even if the final answer slips a bit.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Says <strong>"x=3"</strong> without showing any working or explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Says <strong>"Subtract 4 then divide by 3 so x=4"</strong>, showing some steps but has a calculation slip.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Shows <strong>"3x + 4 = 16; subtract 4 both sides → 3x = 12; divide both sides by 3 → x = 4; substitute back 3(4)+4=16, correct."</strong></p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It includes all algebraic steps with proper reasoning.</li>  
<li>It uses precise inverse operations and checks the answer.</li>  
<li>It avoids vague statements and demonstrates full understanding.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Simplify the expression: 5x + 3x – 2</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve the equation 7x – 4 = 24 for x.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Rearrange the formula A = 3l + 2w to make l the subject.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Start with A = 3l + 2w  
Subtract 2w from both sides: A – 2w = 3l  
Divide both sides by 3: l = (A – 2w)/3  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> The total cost, C pounds, for x items is given by C = 5x + 20. Rearrange this formula to find x in terms of C, then calculate x when C = 65.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Start with C = 5x + 20  
Subtract 20 from both sides: C – 20 = 5x  
Divide both sides by 5: x = (C – 20)/5  
Substitute C = 65: x = (65 – 20)/5 = 45/5 = 9  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Expression</strong> – A combination of numbers, variables, and operations without an equals sign  
<strong>Equation</strong> – A mathematical statement with an equals sign showing two expressions are equal  
<strong>Formula</strong> – An equation that shows the relationship between different variables  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To reduce an expression to its simplest form by combining like terms  
<strong>Rearrange</strong> – To change a formula to make a different variable the subject  
<strong>Substitute</strong> – To replace a variable with a known number in an expression or formula  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find the value of the variable that makes an equation true  
<strong>Inverse operations</strong> – Operations that undo each other, such as addition and subtraction  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number multiplying a variable in an algebraic term  
<strong>Variable</strong> – A letter or symbol used to represent an unknown number in algebra
