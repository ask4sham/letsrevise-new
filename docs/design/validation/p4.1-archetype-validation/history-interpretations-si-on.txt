6 — KEY EXAMPLES

<h2><strong>Teaching: Historians' interpretations of the Cold War</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that historians disagree because they focus on different causes and evidence.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Some say the Cold War was caused by aggressive Soviet leaders trying to spread communism.</li>

<li>Both sides may have made mistakes that increased tension, leading to a long Cold War.</li>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding different historians’ interpretations helps you write balanced, analytical answers rather than just listing facts.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 For an “evaluate” or “how far do you agree” question, always include both supporting evidence and counter-evidence for each interpretation you discuss.</p>  
<p>👉 Use connectives like ‘however’, ‘nevertheless’, and ‘on the other hand’ to show balance and depth.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Gaddis blames the Soviet Union for causing the Cold War.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Gaddis blames the Soviet Union because Stalin wanted to spread communism, which made the USA feel threatened.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Gaddis argues the Cold War was caused by Stalin’s aggressive expansionist policy, shown by Soviet actions in Eastern Europe in 1948–49, which threatened US security and led to tensions. However, Revisionists argue that US economic policies also caused conflict, so the cause isn't solely the USSR.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Includes specific evidence (Soviet actions in Eastern Europe).</li>  
<li>Shows understanding of different views, not just stating one side.</li>  
<li>Uses clear causal language explaining the chain of cause → effect.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)  

Question:  
Evaluate the view that the Cold War was caused mainly by the actions of the Soviet Union.  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The orthodox historian John Lewis Gaddis claims the USSR caused the Cold War because Stalin wanted to spread communism into Eastern Europe. Evidence includes Soviet control of countries like Poland and East Germany after 1945.  

2. However, this view is challenged by revisionists who argue that US policies, like the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan, were aggressive and caused the USSR to react defensively.  

3. Post-revisionists suggest both superpowers made mistakes, and neither side alone caused the Cold War. They point to misunderstandings and fear on both sides.  

4. Therefore, while the USSR’s expansion was a key factor, it’s too simplistic to blame it alone; evidence shows the Cold War was caused by a complex mix of actions and reactions.  

</details>

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p>Q1 (1 mark): Name one historian who blamed the Soviet Union for the Cold War.</p>  

<p>Q2 (2 marks): Describe one way the USA’s actions could be seen as causing the Cold War according to revisionist historians.</p>  

<p>Q3 (3 marks): Explain why post-revisionist historians disagree with both orthodox and revisionist views.</p>  

<p>Q4 (4 marks): How far do you agree that the Cold War was caused mainly by the Soviet Union? Use evidence and explain your answer.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers for Q3 and Q4</summary>  

Q3 Model answer:  
Post-revisionist historians argue that the Cold War was not caused by one side alone. They believe both the USA and the USSR made mistakes and had misunderstandings that increased tensions. This challenges the orthodox view of sole Soviet blame and the revisionist focus on the USA.  

Q4 Model answer:  
On one hand, the Soviet Union was responsible for Cold War tensions because Stalin expanded control over Eastern Europe, breaking agreements made at Yalta, which worried the USA. On the other hand, the USA’s policies like the Truman Doctrine showed a desire to contain communism aggressively, provoking the USSR. Therefore, while the Soviet Union played a major part, the Cold War was caused by actions from both countries, so I partly agree with the view.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<strong>Interpretation</strong> – A historian’s explanation or view on a historical event.  
<strong>Orthodox</strong> – The traditional view blaming the Soviet Union for the Cold War.  
<strong>Revisionist</strong> – A view blaming the USA for causing Cold War tensions.  
<strong>Post-Revisionist</strong> – A view that blames both superpowers due to misunderstandings.  
<strong>Cold War</strong> – The period of tension between the USA and USSR from 1945 to 1991 without direct fighting.  
<strong>Evidence</strong> – Information that supports or challenges a historical interpretation.  
<strong>Cause</strong> – The factor or event that leads to another event happening.  
<strong>Bias</strong> – When a historian or source shows a one-sided preference or prejudice.  
<strong>Evaluate</strong> – To weigh up evidence and make a judgement about an interpretation’s usefulness.  
<strong>Claim</strong> – A statement made by a historian about what caused or happened in history.
