9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Forces</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Let’s break forces down with the key concept of resultant force.</p>

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>Resultant force determines motion changes—whether an object accelerates or stays steady.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Several forces can act on an object at once.</li>
<li>The single force causing motion change is their sum—the resultant force.</li>
<li>If forces are balanced (resultant = 0), no acceleration happens—the object stays still or moves steadily.</li>
<li>If forces are unbalanced (resultant ≠ 0), acceleration occurs in the direction of the resultant force.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Apply forces → calculate vector sum (resultant) → unbalanced leads to acceleration → balanced leads to equilibrium.</li>
<li>Use F=ma to relate force, mass, and acceleration. This allows quantitative prediction.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Use cause → effect → outcome: "Because the resultant force is [direction], the object accelerates [direction], so its velocity changes."</p>
<p>👉 In calculation, always state the formula, identify the variables, substitute values with units, and give the final answer with unit.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE

<p>👉 <strong>A common mistake is</strong> saying an object "moves because there is a force" without explaining the difference between balanced and unbalanced forces.</p>
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> Only an unbalanced resultant force causes acceleration or change in motion; balanced forces keep the motion the same.</p>
<p><strong>Why this correction earns marks:</strong> It shows understanding of how motion changes, not just that forces exist.</p>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

<p>👉 Explain why a car slows down when the driver takes their foot off the accelerator, using the ideas of forces and resultant force.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

When the accelerator is released, the driving force from the engine reduces, but friction and air resistance remain. These forces are no longer balanced by a forward force, so the resultant force acts opposite to the motion. Because the resultant force is unbalanced and acts backward, the car decelerates and slows down.

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>Q1 (1 mark): Define a force.</p>

<p>Q2 (2 marks): An object has a mass of 5 kg and experiences a force of 20 N. Calculate its acceleration. (Show your working.)</p>

<p>Q3 (3 marks): Explain what happens to a stationary object if an unbalanced force acts on it to the right.</p>

<p>Q4 (4 marks): A car of mass 1000 kg is accelerating at 2 m/s². The friction force acting against the motion is 500 N. Calculate the driving force of the car and explain how forces affect its motion.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answers</summary>  

Q1: A force is a push or pull that can change the motion of an object.

Q2:  
Use F = ma → a = F/m = 20 N / 5 kg = 4 m/s²

Q3:  
If an unbalanced force acts on a stationary object, it will accelerate in the direction of the force because the resultant force is not zero. This causes a change from rest to motion.

Q4:  
Calculate driving force (F_drive):

Net force for acceleration = mass × acceleration = 1000 × 2 = 2000 N.

Since friction acts opposite with 500 N, the driving force must overcome this plus provide net force:

F_drive = 2000 + 500 = 2500 N.

Explanation: The driving force is larger than friction, so the resultant force is forward, causing acceleration.

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Force</strong> – A push or pull that can change an object’s motion.</p>  
<p><strong>Resultant force</strong> – The single force that has the same effect as all forces acting together.</p>  
<p><strong>Balanced forces</strong> – Forces that cancel each other so the resultant force is zero.</p>  
<p><strong>Unbalanced forces</strong> – Forces that do not cancel, causing acceleration.</p>  
<p><strong>Acceleration</strong> – Rate of change of velocity (speed or direction).</p>  
<p><strong>Mass</strong> – The amount of matter in an object (kg).</p>  
<p><strong>Newton’s Second Law</strong> – The force on an object equals mass times acceleration (F = ma).</p>  
<p><strong>Friction</strong> – A force opposing motion between surfaces.</p>  
<p><strong>Terminal velocity</strong> – Constant speed when air resistance balances weight.</p>  
<p><strong>Equilibrium</strong> – When resultant force is zero and motion is steady or at rest.</p>
