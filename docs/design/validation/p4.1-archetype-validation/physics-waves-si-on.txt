9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Waves</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing to understand is how waves move energy without moving the particles themselves along the direction of travel.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Waves are patterns of oscillation that transfer energy from a source to a receiver without the net movement of matter.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Particles in the medium oscillate around a fixed point, passing energy onto neighbors.</li>  
<li>Because particles only vibrate in place, energy moves forward through the wave but matter does not travel with it.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A source (like a vibrating guitar string) produces oscillations in the medium (air).</li>  
<li>These produce waves with properties like wavelength (distance between crests), frequency (oscillations per second), speed (how fast energy moves), and amplitude (height, related to energy).</li>  
<li>Energy travels along the wave, allowing sound to reach your ear or light to illuminate a room.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining waves, always link the oscillation → energy transfer → particle movement carefully. Don’t just state a wave moves energy; explain how particles vibrate and transfer energy without moving permanently.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying waves carry matter along with them.</p>  
<p>Students often confuse waves with objects moving—like water carrying leaves downstream.</p>  
<p>Do NOT say that particles travel with the wave. Instead, particles oscillate in place, passing energy forward.</p>  
<p>This distinction is crucial for full marks because the mark schemes reward the explanation of energy transfer with particle vibration, not mass transport.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why sound waves cannot travel through a vacuum.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Sound waves are longitudinal waves that need particles in a medium to vibrate and transfer energy. In a vacuum, there are no particles to vibrate, so sound waves cannot travel because the wave has no medium to move through.  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam practice questions</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong></p>  
<p>State whether light is a transverse or longitudinal wave.</p>

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong></p>  
<p>Describe the difference between frequency and wavelength in a wave.</p>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong></p>  
<p>Explain how a wave transfers energy without transferring matter. Use a longitudinal wave as an example.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
A wave transfers energy by making particles oscillate about fixed points, passing energy to neighboring particles. In a longitudinal wave like sound, particles vibrate back and forth parallel to wave travel, transferring energy along the wave without particles moving away.  
</details>

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong></p>  
<p>A water wave has a wavelength of 2 m and travels at a speed of 4 m/s. Calculate its frequency and explain the relationship between these quantities.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Frequency = speed ÷ wavelength = 4 m/s ÷ 2 m = 2 Hz. Frequency is how many waves pass a point each second; wavelength is the length of one wave. If wavelength increases and speed stays the same, frequency decreases to keep the relationship speed = frequency × wavelength.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Wavelength</strong> – The distance between two points in phase on a wave (e.g., crest to crest).</p>  
<p><strong>Frequency</strong> – Number of wave oscillations passing a point per second (Hz).</p>  
<p><strong>Amplitude</strong> – The maximum displacement from the rest position; relates to wave energy.</p>  
<p><strong>Transverse wave</strong> – A wave where particles oscillate perpendicular to direction of energy transfer.</p>  
<p><strong>Longitudinal wave</strong> – A wave where particles oscillate parallel to direction of energy transfer.</p>  
<p><strong>Energy transfer</strong> – The movement of energy from one place to another, carried by waves.</p>  
<p><strong>Medium</strong> – The substance through which a mechanical wave travels.</p>  
<p><strong>Speed of wave</strong> – How fast a wave’s energy travels through a medium (m/s).</p>  
<p><strong>Oscillation</strong> – A repetitive movement around a fixed point.</p>  
<p><strong>Reflection</strong> – When a wave bounces back after hitting a boundary.</p>
