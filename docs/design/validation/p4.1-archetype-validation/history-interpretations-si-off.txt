9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Historians' interpretations of the Cold War</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing is that historians don’t all agree on why the Cold War started.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Different historical interpretations show how historians use evidence but reach different conclusions about causes.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Historians choose which events and evidence to focus on based on their perspective.</li>  
<li>This choice leads to different stories or explanations about the Cold War causes, such as blaming the USSR, the USA, or both.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Cause → event → judgement chain:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Cause: Soviet expansion in Eastern Europe after WWII is seen as aggressive by Traditionalists.</li>  
<li>Event: Tensions rise, leading to the Cold War’s start.</li>  
<li>Judgement: Some historians say the USSR is to blame; others say the USA's actions provoked the USSR.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain which historians you mention, what their argument is, and provide evidence for their view. Connect this clearly to the cause → event → judgement model to gain top marks.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding different historians’ interpretations shows you can analyse history critically, which is exactly what GCSE examiners want.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering ‘How far do you agree?’ or ‘Explain why...’ questions, name historians’ views, explain their reasons, and link to evidence using cause → effect → judgement chains.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The USA caused the Cold War.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The USA caused the Cold War because it blocked Soviet influence in Europe.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>According to Revisionist historians, the USA caused the Cold War because its policy of economic and military pressure threatened the USSR, leading the Soviets to respond aggressively and start tensions.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It names the interpretation (Revisionist).</li>  
<li>It explains the cause → effect clearly (policy → Soviet response → tension).</li>  
<li>It connects directly to Cold War origins, showing understanding of historians’ views.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Interpretation</strong> – A historian’s explanation or view about events.  
<strong>Cause</strong> – The reason why something happens.  
<strong>Bias</strong> – When a historian favours one side or viewpoint.  
<strong>Primary source</strong> – Original evidence from the time studied.  
<strong>Secondary source</strong> – An account written after the event, often analysing primary sources.  
<strong>Traditionalist</strong> – A view blaming the USSR for the Cold War.  
<strong>Revisionist</strong> – A view blaming the USA for causing tensions.  
<strong>Post-Revisionist</strong> – A view that blames both the USA and USSR.  
<strong>Evidence</strong> – Information or facts used to support an argument.  
<strong>Judgement</strong> – A historian’s conclusion about causes or responsibility.
