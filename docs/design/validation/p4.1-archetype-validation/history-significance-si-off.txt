<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The <strong>significance of the Holocaust</strong> is the importance and lasting impact of the systematic murder of six million Jews and millions of others by the Nazis during World War II.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The Holocaust matters because it was a unique and extreme example of human cruelty with lasting effects on laws, memory, and how societies understand genocide and racism.</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Significance of the Holocaust</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key thing to remember is that the Holocaust is significant because of its scale, the ideology behind it, and its effects that still shape the world today.</p>  
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was not just a tragic event; it changed global understanding of human rights and genocide forever.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Start: Nazi beliefs focus on racial purity and hatred of Jews (anti-Semitism).</li>  
<li>Cause → Event chain: This hatred causes laws against Jews, then mass deportations, and finally industrial-scale murder in camps.</li>  
<li>Consequence: Millions die; survivors face lifelong effects; world leaders respond after the war with trials and new laws.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Nazi ideology (structure) → government policies like the Nuremberg Laws (function) → made it legal to discriminate and dehumanise Jews (process) → this enabled the Holocaust (effect).</li>  
<li>Concentration camps → forced labour and extermination → mass deaths and trauma → led to international awareness of genocide.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 You want to explain significance by linking the cause (Nazi racism) to what happened (genocide) and the consequences (long-term effects on law and memory).</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common Mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say the Holocaust was just a war or battle.</p>  
<p>👉 Do NOT say it was only about fighting soldiers. It was a systematic genocide aimed at racial groups.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is: It was the planned murder of millions based on racist ideology.</p>  
<p>👉 This correction earns marks by linking cause with the unique nature of the Holocaust.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the Holocaust’s significance helps you explain how and why such a huge crime against humanity happened and why it still influences global attitudes today.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering an exam question on significance, always:  
<ol>  
<li>Identify a clear cause of the event.</li>  
<li>Explain what happened during the event.</li>  
<li>Describe one or more consequences or impacts.</li>  
</ol>  
Link these steps with “because” or “this means that” to show connections clearly.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was important because many people died.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was important because millions of Jews were killed, which was a terrible crime.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust is significant because Nazi racial ideology caused systematic genocide, resulting in the murder of six million Jews, which led to international laws and global awareness of human rights violations.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly links the cause (Nazi ideology) to the event (genocide).</li>  
<li>It states precise consequences (laws and awareness).</li>  
<li>It uses key terms relevant for GCSE marks.</li>  
</ul>

<ul>  
<li>The Holocaust was the genocide of six million Jews by the Nazis during World War II.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Genocide</strong> – The deliberate mass killing of a particular group of people.<br />  
<strong>Anti-Semitism</strong> – Hostility and discrimination against Jewish people.<br />  
<strong>Concentration camp</strong> – A place where large numbers of people were imprisoned and often killed by Nazis.<br />  
<strong>Extermination camp</strong> – A camp designed specifically for mass murder.<br />  
<strong>Nazi ideology</strong> – Nazi beliefs based on ideas of racial superiority.<br />  
<strong>Nuremberg Trials</strong> – War crime trials held after WWII to punish Nazi leaders.<br />  
<strong>Holocaust remembrance</strong> – Actions and events that remember and honour the victims of the Holocaust.<br />  
<strong>Human rights</strong> – Basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person.<br />  
<strong>Prejudice</strong> – Preconceived opinions or attitudes, often negative, about a group.<br />  
<strong>Racism</strong> – Belief in the superiority of one race over others leading to discrimination.<br />
