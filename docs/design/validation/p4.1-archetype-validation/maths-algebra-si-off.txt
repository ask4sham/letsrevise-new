<p>👉 The key idea is that algebra helps us work with unknown numbers using letters to stand in place of them.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is treating an expression like 2x + 5 as an equation that can be solved on its own.</p>
<p>In reality, <strong>expressions do not have an equals sign and cannot be 'solved' until they are part of an equation.</strong></p>
<p>👉 Do NOT say “solving 2x + 5” without an equals sign — you earn no marks if you confuse expression simplification with solving an equation.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Solving a simple linear equation

Step 1:  
Start with the equation, e.g., 3x + 2 = 11

↓

Step 2:  
Subtract 2 from both sides: 3x + 2 – 2 = 11 – 2 → 3x = 9

↓

Step 3:  
Divide both sides by 3: (3x)/3 = 9/3 → x = 3

↓

Step 4:  
Check by substituting x=3 back into original equation to verify balance

Exam link:  
This process shows how to isolate the variable and check your answer in GCSE algebra questions.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra questions are common in GCSE exams because they test your ability to think abstractly and solve different types of problems efficiently.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down each algebraic step clearly, showing how you rearrange the equation and simplify terms. Don’t skip steps, even if they seem obvious — clarity wins marks.</p>  
<p>👉 After solving, substitute your answer back into the original equation to check it balances. This helps catch mistakes and earns method marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The answer is x = 3 without showing any working.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Shows the steps 3x + 2 = 11 then 3x = 9 and x = 3 but no substitution check.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Starts with 3x + 2 = 11, subtracts 2 from both sides to get 3x = 9, divides both sides by 3 to find x = 3, then substitutes back (3×3 + 2 = 11) to verify the solution is correct.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly shows each algebraic manipulation step.</li>  
<li>Includes checking the solution, proving understanding.</li>  
<li>Uses correct terminology and logic that examiners reward.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  
<p>Q1 (1 mark): Simplify the expression 4a + 3a.</p>  

<p>Q2 (2 marks): Write an equation for the perimeter P of a square with side length s.</p>  

<p>Q3 (3 marks): Solve 2x + 5 = 15 and check your answer.</p>  

<p>Q4 (4 marks): A rectangle has length l = 3x + 2 and width w = x – 1. Write an expression for its perimeter and simplify. Then calculate the perimeter when x = 4.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

Q1: 4a + 3a = 7a  

Q2: P = 4s  

Q3:  
2x + 5 = 15  
2x = 10 (subtract 5)  
x = 5 (divide by 2)  
Check: 2×5 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15 ✓  

Q4:  
Perimeter = 2(length + width)  
= 2((3x + 2) + (x – 1))  
= 2(3x + 2 + x – 1)  
= 2(4x + 1)  
= 8x + 2  
When x =4: 8×4 + 2 = 32 + 2 = 34  

</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra uses variables to write expressions and set equations that model many situations.</p>  
<p>👉 Expressions combine terms but don’t have equals signs, while equations state equality and can be solved.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Variable</strong> – A symbol (usually a letter) representing an unknown number.  
<strong>Expression</strong> – A mathematical phrase with numbers, variables, and operations but no equals sign.  
<strong>Equation</strong> – A statement that two expressions are equal, shown with an equals sign.  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number multiplied by a variable in an expression.  
<strong>Constant</strong> – A fixed number without any variable attached.  
<strong>Term</strong> – A single number, variable, or product of numbers and variables in an expression.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To make an expression easier by combining like terms or performing operations.  
<strong>Substitute</strong> – Replacing a variable in an expression with a number.  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find the value of a variable that makes an equation true.  
<strong>Formula</strong> – An equation that shows the relationship between different quantities.
