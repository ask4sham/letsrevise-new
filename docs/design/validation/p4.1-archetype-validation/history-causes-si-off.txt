9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Causes of WW1</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that multiple causes built up over decades before a short event triggered a full-scale war.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happened in the long-term causes?</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Militarism:</strong> European powers expanded armies and navies, creating an arms race, especially between Britain and Germany.</li>  
<li><strong>Alliances:</strong> Countries formed defensive agreements like the Triple Entente (France, Russia, UK) and Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy).</li>  
<li><strong>Imperialism:</strong> Rivalries grew as countries competed for colonies, increasing distrust, especially between Britain, France, and Germany.</li>  
<li><strong>Nationalism:</strong> Strong patriotism and ethnic groups wanting independence increased tensions, particularly in the Balkans.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>What happened in the short-term cause?</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand by a Serbian nationalist was the immediate trigger.</li>  
<li>This led Austria-Hungary to issue an ultimatum to Serbia, escalating political tensions.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Process: chain to war</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Ultimatum refused → Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia → Russia mobilises to defend Serbia → Germany declares war on Russia → France and Britain join because of alliances → War begins.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always link your answer with cause → effect → consequence. Don’t just list causes; explain how one caused another.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the causes of WW1 is essential because it explains how a complex web of long-term tensions combined with a short-term spark to create the deadliest conflict of the early 20th century. It helps you answer “Explain why…” questions accurately. </p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In the exam, always explain cause and consequence using clear terminology. For example, say, “Because alliances meant countries promised to defend each other, a conflict between two countries quickly spread into a larger war.”</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>World War 1 started because Archduke Franz Ferdinand was killed.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The war started because Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated and this led Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia, which then involved other countries.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggered Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia, but long-term causes like alliances meant that Russia, Germany, France, and Britain were pulled into war quickly, escalating a regional conflict into a world war.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It links the trigger to wider underlying causes.</li>  
<li>It shows clear cause → effect chains and international consequences.</li>  
<li>It uses specific, accurate terminology expected in answers.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why alliances were a cause of World War 1. (3 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Alliances were agreements to defend each other if attacked. Because of the Triple Entente and Triple Alliance, when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, allied countries had to join — Russia helped Serbia; Germany helped Austria-Hungary; France and Britain joined because of their alliances with Russia. This made a small conflict into a large war.  

</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<h2><strong>Synthesis: How causes combined to start WW1</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that no single cause started World War 1 — instead, a mixture of long-term tensions and short-term events created a "perfect storm." </p>  

<p><strong>Cause → effect sequence:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li>Militarism increased fear and readiness for war.</li>  
<li>Alliances divided Europe, making it likely conflicts would involve many countries.</li>  
<li>Imperial rivalries increased distrust and competition.</li>  
<li>Nationalism caused ethnic tensions and demands for independence.</li>  
<li>The assassination triggered a chain reaction with alliances pulling all into war.</li>  
</ul>  

<p>👉 Together, these created a fragile peace that exploded into war in 1914.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Militarism</strong> – The belief that a country should maintain a strong military and be prepared to use it aggressively.  
<strong>Alliance</strong> – An agreement between countries to support each other, especially in case of war.  
<strong>Imperialism</strong> – The policy of extending a country's power through colonisation or military force.  
<strong>Nationalism</strong> – Devotion and pride in one’s nation or ethnic group; sometimes leads to competition and conflict.  
<strong>Assassination</strong> – The murder of an important person for political reasons.  
<strong>Arms race</strong> – Competition between countries to build more and better weapons.  
<strong>Triple Entente</strong> – The alliance of Britain, France, and Russia before WW1.  
<strong>Triple Alliance</strong> – The alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy before WW1.  
<strong>Balance of Power</strong> – A situation where no single country is dominant, to maintain stability.  
<strong>Mobilisation</strong> – The process of preparing and organizing troops for war.
