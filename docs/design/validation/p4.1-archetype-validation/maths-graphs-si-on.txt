3 — DEFINITION

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A graph is a visual representation of data or functions that shows the relationship between two variables plotted on perpendicular axes.</strong></p>  
<p>👉 The horizontal axis is called the <strong>x-axis</strong>, and the vertical axis is the <strong>y-axis</strong>.</p>

<ul>  
<li>The axes must be set with the right scale so plotting is accurate.</li>

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing the gradient as just the "rise" up, ignoring the "run" across.</p>  
<p>👉 Correction: The gradient = rise ÷ run, which means both vertical and horizontal changes matter.</p>  
<p>👉 This correction earns marks because examiners expect the full gradient calculation, showing understanding of rate of change.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Calculating Gradient from a Graph

Step 1:  
Identify two clear points on the straight line with coordinates (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).

↓

Step 2:  
Calculate the vertical change (rise) = y₂ - y₁.

↓

Step 3:  
Calculate the horizontal change (run) = x₂ - x₁.

↓

Step 4:  
Divide rise by run to get gradient = (y₂ - y₁) ÷ (x₂ - x₁).

Exam link:  
Showing this step-by-step method in exams demonstrates understanding of gradient calculation and gains method marks.

<p>The graph is a line with a gradient. It shows how y changes.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The graph of y = 3x + 2 is a straight line with gradient 3, meaning for every 1 unit increase in x, y increases by 3 units. The y-intercept is 2, showing that when x = 0, y = 2.</p>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
A straight line passes through points (1,4) and (3,10). What is the gradient of the line?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Gradient = (10 - 4) ÷ (3 - 1) = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong>  
What is the name of the horizontal axis on a graph?</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong>  
Calculate the gradient of a line passing through points (2,5) and (5,17).</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong>  
A quadratic graph has turning points at (3,4) and (7,10). Explain what the turning points represent.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong>  
The graph of y = -2x + 6 is drawn. Calculate the gradient and y-intercept. Then explain what the gradient tells you about the relationship between x and y.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1 Answer:  
The x-axis.  

Q2 Answer:  
Gradient = (17 - 5) ÷ (5 - 2) = 12 ÷ 3 = 4.  

Q3 Answer:  
Turning points are where the graph changes direction. At (3,4) and (7,10), these represent minimum or maximum values of y, showing where the curve goes from increasing to decreasing or vice versa.  

Q4 Answer:  
Gradient = -2, y-intercept = 6.  
The gradient of -2 means y decreases by 2 for every increase of 1 in x, showing a negative relationship between x and y. The y-intercept 6 shows that when x is 0, y is 6.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Axis</strong> – A reference line on a graph, usually horizontal (x-axis) or vertical (y-axis).  
<strong>Gradient</strong> – The steepness of a line; the rate at which y changes with x.  
<strong>Intercept</strong> – The point where the graph crosses an axis.  
<strong>Turning point</strong> – A maximum or minimum point where the graph changes direction.  
<strong>Plot</strong> – To mark points on a graph using coordinates.  
<strong>Scale</strong> – The units or spacing used on the axes.  
<strong>Coordinate</strong> – A pair of numbers (x, y) that locate a point on a graph.  
<strong>Curve</strong> – A line that is not straight, such as quadratic or cubic graphs.  
<strong>Linear</strong> – A graph that forms a straight line.  
<strong>Quadratic</strong> – A graph shaped like a parabola, described by an equation with an x² term.
