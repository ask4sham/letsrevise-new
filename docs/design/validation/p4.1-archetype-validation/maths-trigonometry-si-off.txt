5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>In any right-angled triangle, the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios link an angle to the ratio of two side lengths:</strong></p>  

<ul>  
<li><strong>Sine (sin) θ = Opposite side ÷ Hypotenuse</strong></li>  
<li><strong>Cosine (cos) θ = Adjacent side ÷ Hypotenuse</strong></li>  
<li><strong>Tangent (tan) θ = Opposite side ÷ Adjacent side</strong></li>  
</ul>  

<p>These ratios are constant for a given angle and are at the heart of solving trigonometry problems.</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Trigonometry</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that every angle in a right-angled triangle sets fixed ratios between the sides opposite, adjacent, and the hypotenuse.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>By knowing one angle (other than the right angle) and one side, you can find any missing side using sin, cos, or tan.</li>  
<li>If you know two sides, you can find an unknown angle using inverse trig functions (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Method → reason:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Identify the angle in question.</li>  
<li>Label the opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides.</li>  
<li>Choose the correct trig ratio based on which sides you know and want.</li>  
<li>Use the calculator for sine, cosine, or tangent; or their inverses.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always mention which sides you are using relative to the angle, state which trig ratio you apply, and write out the formula step clearly to get full marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing which side is opposite or adjacent relative to the angle given.</p>  
<p>This causes using the wrong trig ratio, leading to wrong answers.</p>  
<p>Always label the triangle with the angle in question, then clearly mark opposite and adjacent sides. The hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle.</p>  
<p>Think: “Opposite means directly across from the angle; adjacent means next to the angle.”</p>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question  

<p>Calculate the length of the side opposite a 40° angle in a right triangle if the hypotenuse is 10 cm.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
sin 40° = opposite ÷ 10  
opposite = 10 × sin 40° ≈ 10 × 0.6428 = 6.43 cm  
</details>  

21 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write down the formula for cosine of an angle θ.</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> In a right triangle, one angle is 30°, the hypotenuse is 12 cm. Find the length of the side opposite the 30° angle.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A ladder leans against a wall making a 60° angle with the ground. The foot is 4 m from the wall. Find the length of the ladder.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
The ladder is the hypotenuse, the foot to wall is adjacent, and angle is 60°.  
Use cos 60° = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse → cos 60° = 4 ÷ length  
cos 60° = 0.5  
So, 0.5 = 4 ÷ length → length = 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 m  
</details>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> From a point 20 m horizontally from a tower, the angle of elevation to the top is 35°. Calculate the height of the tower to the nearest metre.</p>  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Height is opposite side, distance is adjacent side, angle is 35°.  
Use tan 35° = opposite ÷ adjacent  
tan 35° ≈ 0.700  
Height = 20 × 0.700 = 14 m (to nearest metre)  
</details>

23 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<p><strong>Hypotenuse</strong> – The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.</p>  
<p><strong>Opposite side</strong> – The side opposite the angle you are working with.</p>  
<p><strong>Adjacent side</strong> – The side next to the angle you are working with, but not the hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Sine (sin)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Cosine (cos)</strong> – Ratio of adjacent side to hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Tangent (tan)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to adjacent side.</p>  
<p><strong>Inverse trig functions</strong> – Functions (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) used to find angles from side ratios.</p>  
<p><strong>Angle of elevation</strong> – The angle looking upwards from a horizontal line.</p>  
<p><strong>Angle of depression</strong> – The angle looking downwards from a horizontal line.</p>  
<p><strong>Right-angled triangle</strong> – A triangle with one 90° angle.</p>
