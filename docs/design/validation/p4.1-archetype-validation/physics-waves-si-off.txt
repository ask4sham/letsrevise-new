9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Waves</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>📘 The key idea is:</strong></p>  
<p>👉 Waves transfer energy by repeating disturbances called oscillations, which move through a medium or space.</p>  

<h3><strong>Types of waves:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Transverse waves:</strong> particles oscillate perpendicular (up and down) to the energy transfer direction.<br />Example: light waves, water waves.</li>  
<li><strong>Longitudinal waves:</strong> particles oscillate parallel (back and forth) to the energy transfer direction.<br />Example: sound waves, seismic P-waves.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Wave properties:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Amplitude:</strong> height of the wave from rest position; linked to energy transferred.</li>  
<li><strong>Wavelength:</strong> distance between two identical points on the wave (e.g., crest to crest).</li>  
<li><strong>Frequency:</strong> number of waves passing a point each second (Hz).</li>  
<li><strong>Period:</strong> the time for one wave to pass (seconds).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Energy transfer mechanism:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The oscillating particles pass energy to neighbouring particles by vibrations (for mechanical waves) or oscillations in electric and magnetic fields (for electromagnetic waves).</li>  
<li>Energy moves forward but particles oscillate about fixed points.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain waves as energy transferring without matter shifting locations. Describe oscillations (direction) and use correct terms.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Explaining wave motion accurately is vital because exam questions often ask for how energy moves, and clear differences between wave types.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering wave questions, use cause → effect: describe how oscillations cause neighbouring particles or fields to oscillate, transferring energy without particle transport. Use correct terms like <em>transverse</em> or <em>longitudinal</em>—this precision fetches marks.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Waves transfer energy by causing particles to oscillate around their fixed positions, passing energy to neighbouring particles without transferring matter in the direction of the wave.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Describe the difference between transverse and longitudinal waves.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

Transverse waves have particles oscillating perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer, like water waves or light. In longitudinal waves, particles oscillate parallel to the energy transfer direction, such as sound waves. Both transfer energy without the particles traveling along with the wave.

</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS

<p>👉 The type of oscillation (perpendicular vs parallel) defines transverse and longitudinal waves.</p>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Which part of a wave is the highest point called?</p>

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Explain why longitudinal waves can only travel through materials, not through a vacuum.</p>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Describe how transverse waves transfer energy through a medium, including particle movement.</p>

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A student says that sound waves move air particles from the speaker to the listener. Explain why this statement is incorrect. Use wave vocabulary.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

<strong>Q1:</strong> The highest point of a wave is called the crest.

<strong>Q2:</strong> Longitudinal waves require particles to oscillate back and forth, so they need a medium with particles. In a vacuum, there are no particles, so sound waves cannot travel.

<strong>Q3:</strong> Transverse waves transfer energy by particles oscillating perpendicular to the wave’s direction. The particles vibrate up and down while the energy moves forward through the medium.

<strong>Q4:</strong> Sound waves transfer energy by causing air particles to oscillate parallel to the direction of the wave. The particles vibrate about fixed positions and do not move from speaker to listener, so the statement is incorrect.

</details>  

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>👉 Waves transfer energy without moving matter.</p>  
<p>👉 Two main types: transverse (oscillate perpendicular) and longitudinal (oscillate parallel).</p>  
<p>👉 Key properties: amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period.</p>  
<p>👉 Mechanical waves need a medium; electromagnetic waves (like light) do not.</p>  
<p>👉 Accurate use of terminology boosts exam marks.</p>  
<p><strong>Remember:</strong> Energy flows with the wave; particles do NOT travel with it.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Wave</strong> – A disturbance transferring energy without transferring matter.  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – The movement of energy from one place to another.  
<strong>Transverse wave</strong> – Wave with oscillations perpendicular to energy transfer.  
<strong>Longitudinal wave</strong> – Wave with oscillations parallel to energy transfer.  
<strong>Oscillation</strong> – Repeated vibration or movement about a fixed point.  
<strong>Amplitude</strong> – Maximum height of a wave from its rest position.  
<strong>Wavelength</strong> – Distance between two identical points on a wave (e.g., crest to crest).  
<strong>Frequency</strong> – Number of waves passing per second (Hz).  
<strong>Period</strong> – Time taken for one complete wave to pass a point (seconds).  
<strong>Medium</strong> – The material through which a wave travels (solid, liquid, gas).
