<h2><strong>Teaching: Significance of the Holocaust</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong></p>  
<p>The Holocaust is significant because it caused immediate human suffering and changed how the world deals with human rights forever.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Holocaust led to the murder of six million Jews and millions of others — that scale of loss shocked the world.</li>

</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Event (Holocaust) → Immediate consequence (mass murder, trauma, liberation by Allies)</li>  
<li>Immediate consequence → Long-term legacy (new international human rights laws, Holocaust education)</li>  
<li>Legacy → Ongoing importance in international relations and history teaching</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain the cause → effect chain: why it happened, what immediate effects it had, and why those effects matter today.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The Holocaust’s significance is a frequent GCSE History exam topic because it connects history, ethics, and global politics.</p>  
<p>Understanding why it matters helps you write focused answers that explain changes in a clear cause → effect way.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When explaining significance, always: name the event, give a clear immediate impact, explain long-term consequences, and use evidence to support your point.</p>  
<p>Try to include criteria like “scale,” “lasting change,” or “historical influence” to show depth.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust is significant because many people died.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust is significant because six million Jews were killed, which caused shock worldwide and led to new laws.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust is highly significant because the genocide of six million Jews caused immediate global outrage, leading directly to the creation of the United Nations in 1945 and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948, establishing a lasting legacy of human rights protection and genocide prevention.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It includes specific evidence (six million Jews, UN creation, human rights declaration).</li>  
<li>It explains cause → effect clearly and uses historical terms.</li>  
<li>It demonstrates understanding of both immediate and long-term impacts.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Holocaust</strong> – The mass murder of six million Jews and millions of others during WWII.  
<strong>Significance</strong> – Why a historical event is important.  
<strong>Immediate impact</strong> – Effects that happened right after an event.  
<strong>Long-term legacy</strong> – Lasting effects or changes from an event.  
<strong>Genocide</strong> – The deliberate killing of a large group of people, especially those of a particular ethnic group.  
<strong>Prejudice</strong> – Unfair opinions against a group based on stereotypes.  
<strong>United Nations</strong> – An international organisation founded in 1945 to promote peace and human rights.  
<strong>Universal Declaration of Human Rights</strong> – A 1948 document setting out basic rights and freedoms for all people.  
<strong>Evidence</strong> – Information used to support a point in history.  
<strong>Judgement</strong> – A reasoned conclusion about the importance or meaning of a historical event.
