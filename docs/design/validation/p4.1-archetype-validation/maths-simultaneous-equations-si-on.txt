9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Simultaneous Equations</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let's break this down step-by-step so you feel confident tackling any pair of equations.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Simultaneous equations require two or more equations to be true at the same time, so the solution is the point(s) where their graphs meet.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Each equation restricts the possible values of variables.</li>  
<li>Only the values that satisfy all equations simultaneously are true solutions.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Method → process → result:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>First, choose substitution (solve one equation for a variable and plug it into the other) or elimination (add/subtract equations to remove a variable).</li>  
<li>Simplify the new equation, solve one variable, then plug back to find the other.</li>  
<li>Check your answers by putting them back into both original equations.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always show each step clearly, label what you are doing (e.g., "substituting y = ..."), and state your final answer with correct values listed—for full marks.</p>

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up the signs when adding or subtracting equations during elimination.</p>  
<p>This leads to wrong coefficients and incorrect answers.</p>  
<p>👉 Always line up variables carefully and check whether you should add or subtract to eliminate the variable.</p>  
<p>This correction will earn marks because it shows precise algebraic manipulation.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Solve these simultaneous equations using substitution:  
y = 3x − 2  
2x + y = 10  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Substitute y from equation 1 into equation 2:  

2x + (3x − 2) = 10  

Step 2: Simplify and solve for x:  

2x + 3x − 2 = 10  
5x − 2 = 10  
5x = 12  
x = 12 ÷ 5  
x = 2.4  

Step 3: Substitute x back to find y:  

y = 3(2.4) − 2 = 7.2 − 2 = 5.2  

Step 4: Check in both equations:  
Equation 1: y = 3x − 2 → 5.2 ≈ 3(2.4) − 2 → 5.2 = 5.2 ✔  
Equation 2: 2x + y = 10 → 2(2.4) + 5.2 = 4.8 + 5.2 = 10 ✔  

Final solution: x = 2.4, y = 5.2  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write down the number of variables in the system below.  
<br />3x + 4y = 10  
<br />2x − y = 3</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve for y in the equation 5x + y = 20.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Solve the simultaneous equations by elimination:  
<br />2x + 3y = 12  
<br />4x − 3y = 6</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Solve the nonlinear simultaneous equations:  
<br />y = x²  
<br />y = 2x + 3  
<br />Show your working.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers for Q3 & Q4</summary>  

<strong>Q3 Answer:</strong>  
Add the equations to eliminate y:  
(2x + 3y) + (4x − 3y) = 12 + 6 → 6x = 18 → x = 3  
Substitute x = 3 into 2x + 3y = 12:  
2(3) + 3y = 12 → 6 + 3y = 12 → 3y = 6 → y = 2  

Final solution: x = 3, y = 2  

<strong>Q4 Answer:</strong>  
Set equations equal:  
x² = 2x + 3  
Rearrange:  
x² − 2x − 3 = 0  
Factor:  
(x − 3)(x + 1) = 0  
x = 3 or x = −1  
Find y for each x:  
If x = 3, y = 3² = 9  
If x = −1, y = (−1)² = 1  

Solutions: (3,9) and (−1,1)  

</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  

<ul>  
<li>Simultaneous equations involve finding values for variables that satisfy two or more equations at once.</li>  
<li>Use substitution when one variable is already isolated or easy to rearrange.</li>  
<li>Use elimination to remove a variable by adding or subtracting equations carefully.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Simultaneous equations</strong> – Two or more equations solved together to find common solutions  
<strong>Substitution</strong> – Replacing one variable with an expression in terms of another  
<strong>Elimination</strong> – Adding or subtracting equations to remove one variable  
<strong>Variable</strong> – The unknown quantities represented by letters  
<strong>Solve</strong> – Find value(s) of variables that satisfy equations  
<strong>Back-substitution</strong> – Checking solution by substituting values back into original equations  
<strong>Linear</strong> – Equations where variables appear to the power of 1 only  
<strong>Nonlinear</strong> – Equations including powers, roots, or products of variables  
<strong>Check</strong> – Verifying that solutions satisfy all equations  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number multiplied by a variable in an equation
