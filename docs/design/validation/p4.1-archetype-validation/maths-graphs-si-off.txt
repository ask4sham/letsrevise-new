9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Graphs</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that a graph is a picture of a mathematical relationship.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>When a graph is a straight line, the rate of change between x and y is constant—this is called the gradient.</li>  
<li>If the graph curves, such as a parabola, the rate of change itself is changing—there may be turning points.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Plotting points (x,y) creates the graph shape → the gradient of lines shows constant change → this means you can predict y for any x on that line.</li>  
<li>Turning points show where the graph changes direction → this helps find maxima and minima important in problems.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining graphs, always refer to key features like intercepts or gradients and relate them to the question context. Use precise language like "the gradient shows the rate of change."</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing the gradient with the y-intercept.</p>  
<p>Students often say the steepness of the graph is where it crosses the y-axis, but actually, gradient is how steep the line is, and y-intercept is where it crosses the y-axis.</p>  
<p>This matters because examiners award marks for correctly identifying and explaining these separate features.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
How to find the gradient of a straight line graph

Step 1:  
Choose two points on the line with coordinates (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).

↓

Step 2:  
Calculate the change in y: Δy = y₂ − y₁.

↓

Step 3:  
Calculate the change in x: Δx = x₂ − x₁.

↓

Step 4:  
Find the gradient by dividing change in y by change in x: Gradient = Δy/Δx.

Exam link:  
Knowing the gradient helps answer questions about rates of change and equation of the line.

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The gradient is the slope of the line.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The gradient is the slope and shows how much y changes for each increase in x.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The gradient is the ratio of the change in y to the change in x between two points on the line, showing the constant rate of change throughout the graph.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It includes clear mathematical terminology.</li>  
<li>It correctly defines gradient as a ratio, not just a slope.</li>  
<li>It explains the meaning of the gradient in context.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A line passes through points (1, 3) and (4, 15). Find its gradient.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Calculate Δy = 15 − 3 = 12.  
Step 2: Calculate Δx = 4 − 1 = 3.  
Step 3: Gradient = Δy / Δx = 12 / 3 = 4.  

Answer: The gradient is 4.  
</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the y-intercept of the graph y = 6x + 2?</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
2
</details>

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Given a line passing through points (2, 7) and (5, 16), find the gradient.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
Gradient = (16 − 7) ÷ (5 − 2) = 9 ÷ 3 = 3
</details>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Sketch the graph of y = x² − 4, identifying roots and turning point.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
Plot points for x = −3 to 3.  
Roots at x = −2 and x = 2 where y=0.  
Turning point at (0, −4) lowest point of the parabola.  
Curve shaped upwards.
</details>

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> The graph of y = x³ − 3x has turning points. Find the coordinates of these turning points.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
Differentiate: dy/dx = 3x² − 3.  
Set dy/dx = 0: 3x² − 3 = 0 → x² = 1 → x = ±1.  
Find y:  
At x = 1, y = 1 − 3 = −2.  
At x = −1, y = −1 + 3 = 2.  
Turning points: (1, −2) and (−1, 2).
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Gradient</strong> – The rate of change of y with respect to x on a line.</p>  
<p><strong>Y-intercept</strong> – The point where a graph crosses the y-axis (x=0).</p>  
<p><strong>X-intercept / Root</strong> – The point where the graph crosses the x-axis (y=0).</p>  
<p><strong>Turning point</strong> – A point where the graph changes direction (maxima or minima).</p>  
<p><strong>Axis</strong> – The horizontal (x) and vertical (y) reference lines on a graph.</p>  
<p><strong>Curve</strong> – A continuous curved line on a graph, not straight.</p>  
<p><strong>Coordinate</strong> – A pair of numbers (x, y) identifying a point on a graph.</p>  
<p><strong>Plot</strong> – To mark points on a graph according to coordinates.</p>  
<p><strong>Domain</strong> – The set of all possible x-values on a graph.</p>  
<p><strong>Range</strong> – The set of all possible y-values on a graph.</p>
