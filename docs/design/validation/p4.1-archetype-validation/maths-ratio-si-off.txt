9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Ratio</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that ratios compare values and help us share or scale quantities properly.</p>

<h3><strong>Method → reason:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Step 1: Write the ratio clearly using colons or fractions.</li>
<li>Step 2: Simplify the ratio if possible by dividing both parts by their greatest common factor.</li>
<li>Step 3: Identify the total number of parts if the ratio refers to a whole.</li>
<li>Step 4: Use multiplication or division to find missing amounts when the total or one part is known.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>This means you can share quantities given in ratio form or scale recipes, mixtures, and models accurately.</li>
<li>Simplifying ratios makes numbers easier to work with and means answers are in the form examiners expect.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always show the full process: first give the simplified ratio, then your working to find actual amounts, and finally write the answer in the correct format.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up addition and ratio parts when solving problems.</p>  
<p>Students might try to add the ratio numbers directly to the total instead of dividing the total into parts.</p>  
<p>👉 The better way is to divide the total by the sum of ratio parts, then multiply by each part separately.</p>  
<p>This avoids incorrect sharing and earns full marks.</p>

Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Solving a ratio problem with a total amount

Step 1:  
Add all parts of the ratio to find the total parts.

↓

Step 2:  
Divide the known total by the total parts to find one part value.

↓

Step 3:  
Multiply the part value by each ratio number to find the actual quantities.

↓

Step 4:  
Write the final amounts clearly, matching the question units.

This method is standard on many Edexcel GCSE ratio questions involving sharing amounts.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Ratio questions are frequent in GCSE maths exams and assess your ability to break down problems and reason about proportional relationships.</p>  
<p>Understanding ratio fully allows you to tackle real-life problems like recipes, maps, and financial sharing.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always simplify ratios before working with them unless instructed otherwise.</p>  
<p>👉 Show your steps clearly — write the sum of parts, divide, then multiply.</p>  
<p>👉 Write answers in the simplest whole-number form or as required by the question.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio is 3:6 and I divided 18 by 3.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio simplifies to 1:2, so I divided the total 18 by 3 parts to get 6 for one part.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio 3:6 simplifies to 1:2. The total parts are 1 + 2 = 3. Dividing 18 by 3 gives 6 per part. Multiplying gives 6 and 12 for each group.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains simplification clearly.</li>  
<li>Shows total parts and division to find one part.</li>  
<li>Links multiplication with final amounts clearly.</li>  
<li>Uses precise ratio terminology to earn all marks.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
The ratio of cats to dogs in a shelter is 4:7. There are 44 animals in total. How many cats and dogs are there?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Total parts = 4 + 7 = 11  
Number in one part = 44 ÷ 11 = 4  
Number of cats = 4 parts × 4 = 16  
Number of dogs = 7 parts × 4 = 28  
Final answer: 16 cats, 28 dogs  

</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
A lemonade recipe uses lemon juice and water in the ratio 3:4. If you have used 12 ml of lemon juice, how much water is needed?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Ratio 3:4 means 3 parts lemon juice to 4 parts water.  
12 ml lemon juice ÷ 3 = 4 ml per part.  
Water needed = 4 parts × 4 ml = 16 ml.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write the ratio 18:24 in its simplest form.</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> A cake recipe uses eggs and sugar in the ratio 2:5. If the recipe uses 10 eggs, how much sugar is needed?</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 5:6. If there are 55 boys, how many students are there in total? Show your working.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A juice mixture is made with orange and apple juice in the ratio 7:3. If the total mixture is 200 ml, calculate the amount of orange and apple juice used. Include all steps and explanations.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers for Q3 and Q4</summary>  

Q3 full-mark answer:  
Total parts = 5 + 6 = 11  
Value of one part = 55 ÷ 5 = 11  
Total students = 11 parts × 11 = 121 students  

Q4 full-mark answer:  
Total parts = 7 + 3 = 10  
Value of one part = 200 ÷ 10 = 20 ml  
Orange juice = 7 × 20 = 140 ml  
Apple juice = 3 × 20 = 60 ml  
Final amounts: 140 ml orange, 60 ml apple juice  

</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Ratio compares quantities by showing relative sizes using parts.</li>  
<li>Simplify ratios to smallest whole numbers before calculating.</li>  
<li>The total parts equal the sum of the ratio’s numbers.</li>  
<li>Divide the total quantity by total parts to find one part.</li>  
<li>Multiply one part by each ratio number to find each quantity.</li>  
<li>Use clear, step-by-step working in exams for full marks.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Ratio</strong> – A way to compare amounts by showing how many times one quantity relates to another.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To reduce a ratio to its smallest whole numbers with the same proportion.  
<strong>Parts</strong> – The individual sections a whole is divided into in a ratio.  
<strong>Proportion</strong> – The relationship between parts, often expressed as ratios or fractions.  
<strong>Equivalent Ratios</strong> – Different ratios expressing the same relationship.  
<strong>Direct Proportion</strong> – A relationship where two quantities increase or decrease at the same rate.  
<strong>Scale Factor</strong> – A multiplier used to enlarge or reduce quantities in proportion.  
<strong>Unit Ratio</strong> – A ratio where one part is set to a single unit (usually 1).  
<strong>Missing Value</strong> – A quantity in a ratio problem that needs to be calculated.  
<strong>Shared</strong> – How quantities are divided according to a ratio.
