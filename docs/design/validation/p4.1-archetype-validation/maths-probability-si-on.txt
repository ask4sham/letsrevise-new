3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Probability is a number between 0 and 1 that measures how likely an event is to happen.</strong></p>  
<p>It can also be written as a fraction, decimal, or percentage.</p>

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>We use probability to predict likely outcomes, which helps in decisions from daily life to exams and science.</strong></p>  
<p>Because events can be uncertain, understanding probability helps us judge risks and chances clearly.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>The core probability model involves these steps:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>Identify the <strong>sample space</strong> — all possible outcomes.</li>  
<li>Define the <strong>event</strong> — what you want to happen.</li>  
<li>Count the <strong>favourable outcomes</strong> for the event.</li>  
<li>Calculate probability using the formula:</li>  
</ol>  
<p><strong>Probability of event = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes</strong></p>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>Flipping a coin: P(Heads) = 1/2 because there are 2 outcomes, only 1 is heads.</li>  
<li>Rolling a die: P(rolling a 4) = 1/6 because one face shows 4 out of 6.</li>  
<li>Picking a red ball from a bag with 3 red and 7 blue balls: P(red) = 3/10.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Probability</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 The key idea here is that probability values range from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>We list all <strong>possible outcomes</strong> in a sample space.</li>  
<li>We identify how many outcomes match the event we want (favourable outcomes).</li>  
<li>The probability is the ratio of favourable outcomes to possible outcomes.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Sample space → defines total outcomes → sets denominator in fraction.</li>  
<li>Event → defines what we want → counts numerator.</li>  
<li>Divide numerator by denominator → calculate probability → predict chance.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always write your probability as a fraction between 0 and 1, simplify it, and if asked, convert to a decimal or percentage.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is thinking the probability can be greater than 1 or less than 0.</p>  
<p>Students often confuse the probability scale, but probability must always be between 0 and 1, inclusive.</p>  
<p>👉 Another typical error is adding probabilities of events incorrectly when they are not mutually exclusive.</p>  
<p>For example, P(A or B) ≠ P(A) + P(B) if events can both happen at once. You must subtract the overlap.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Calculating Probability for Combined Events (Or)

Step 1:  
Identify all possible outcomes (sample space).

↓

Step 2:  
Identify all outcomes favourable to event A.

↓

Step 3:  
Identify all outcomes favourable to event B.

↓

Step 4:  
Use formula P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B).

Exam link:  
This method is often needed for "or" probability questions in exams to avoid double counting.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding how to calculate and express probability accurately is a common GCSE requirement, as it helps you interpret data and assess risk properly.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always state your answer as a fraction between 0 and 1, and simplify it. If a decimal or percentage is requested, convert correctly.</p>  
<p>👉 For combined events “or,” remember to subtract the overlap if the events are not mutually exclusive.</p>  
<p>👉 Show full working like <em>“Probability = favourable ÷ total outcomes = …”</em> to gain method marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The probability of rolling a 4 is 1/6.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The probability of rolling a 4 is 1 out of 6 because there is one face with 4 and six faces total.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The probability of rolling a 4 is 1/6 because the sample space has 6 equally likely outcomes, and exactly one outcome shows a 4; thus, P(4) = 1 ÷ 6.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses correct terminology like sample space and outcomes.</li>  
<li>It shows the reasoning clearly with cause and effect.</li>  
<li>It explicitly applies the probability formula with working.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Calculate the probability of drawing a king or a queen from a standard deck of 52 cards.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Identify total outcomes (sample space): 52.  
Step 2: Number of kings = 4.  
Step 3: Number of queens = 4.  
Step 4: No overlap since kings and queens are different cards.  
Step 5: Probability = (4 + 4) ÷ 52 = 8 ÷ 52.  
Step 6: Simplify: 8 ÷ 52 = 2 ÷ 13.  

Final answer: P(king or queen) = 2/13.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>Here are some practice questions following GCSE style.</p>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> A spinner is divided into 4 equal sections numbered 1–4. What is the probability of landing on 3?</p>

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> A bag has 5 green and 7 red marbles. What is the probability of choosing a red marble? Give your answer as a fraction.</p>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A die is rolled twice. What is the probability of getting a 2 on the first roll and a 5 on the second roll? Show your working.</p>

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A card is picked from a standard deck. What is the probability that it is a heart or a queen? Explain your answer fully.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

<strong>Q1:</strong> P(3) = 1/4.

<strong>Q2:</strong> Total marbles = 5 + 7 = 12; P(red) = 7/12.

<strong>Q3:</strong> P(2 then 5) = P(2) × P(5) = (1/6) × (1/6) = 1/36.

<strong>Q4:</strong>  
Total hearts = 13, queens = 4, queen of hearts counted twice if added.  
P(heart or queen) = P(heart) + P(queen) − P(queen of hearts) = 13/52 + 4/52 − 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13.  

</details>

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Probability measures likelihood between 0 and 1, or 0% and 100%.</li>  
<li>Calculate probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.</li>  
<li>Sample space is the set of all possible outcomes.</li>  
<li>“Or” calculations add probabilities but subtract overlap if events aren’t exclusive.</li>  
<li>“And” calculations multiply probabilities for independent events.</li>  
<li>Show all steps and include units if needed in exam answers.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: Write probability answers clearly as fractions or decimals unless told otherwise.</p>  
<p>👉 In exams, always explain reasoning with “because” or “so” to get full marks.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Probability</strong> – A number between 0 and 1 measuring likelihood of an event.</p>  
<p><strong>Sample space</strong> – The set of all possible outcomes.</p>  
<p><strong>Event</strong> – The result or outcome we are interested in.</p>  
<p><strong>Favourable outcome</strong> – An outcome that makes the event happen.</p>  
<p><strong>Mutually exclusive</strong> – Events that cannot happen at the same time.</p>  
<p><strong>Independent events</strong> – Events where one does not affect the other.</p>  
<p><strong>Combined events</strong> – Events involving “and” or “or” calculations.</p>  
<p><strong>Complement</strong> – The event that an event does not happen; P(not A) = 1 − P(A).</p>  
<p><strong>Calculate</strong> – Work out a number from given information.</p>  
<p><strong>Solve</strong> – Find an answer, often by working through steps methodically.</p>
