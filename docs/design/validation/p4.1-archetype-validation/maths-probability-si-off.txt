<p>👉 <strong>Key probability rules: probability of all possible outcomes adds up to 1; for two independent events A and B, the probability of both happening is P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B); for mutually exclusive events, the probability of either happening is P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).</strong></p>

9 — CORE TEACHING

<h2><strong>Teaching: Probability</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When we talk about probability, the key thing is that numbers always lie between 0 and 1. 0 means impossible, 1 means certain.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Probability quantifies uncertainty about events.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>You list all possible outcomes.</li>  
<li>Count how many ways the event can happen.</li>  
<li>Calculate probability by dividing the favorable outcomes by total outcomes.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Events have probabilities tied to their likelihoods.</li>  
<li>Two events can be combined using multiplication (independent) or addition (mutually exclusive).</li>  
<li>Using diagrams like sample spaces or tree diagrams helps visualise combinations.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always define your events clearly, show step-by-step reasoning, and use correct probability terms to get full credit.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to add probabilities for independent events instead of multiplying them.</p>  
<p>For example, if you want the probability of two independent events both happening, do NOT say P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) — that only works if events are mutually exclusive.</p>  
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> For independent events happening together, use P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).</p>  
<p>This earns marks because it shows full understanding of event types and rules.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding probability rules and vocabulary lets you explain your answers clearly, helping examiners award full marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exam questions about combined events, say exactly whether events are independent or mutually exclusive.</p>  
<p>👉 Use phrases like <em>“since the events are independent, multiply the probabilities”</em> or <em>“since the events cannot happen at the same time, add the probabilities”</em>.</p>  
<p>👉 This shows full understanding and maximises marks.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Probability of both is found by adding their chances.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Since the events are independent, we multiply their probabilities to find the chance of both happening.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Because the two events are independent and can occur together, the probability of both events occurring is the product of their probabilities, calculated as P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly identifies the event relationship.</li>  
<li>Uses correct terminology “independent” and “product of their probabilities”.</li>  
<li>Shows understanding of the probability rule involved.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>Q1 (1 mark): What is the probability of flipping a coin and getting heads?</p>

<p>Q2 (2 marks): A spinner is divided into 4 equal parts numbered 1, 2, 3, and 4. What is the probability of landing on an even number?</p>

<p>Q3 (3 marks): Two dice are rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 3 on the first die and an even number on the second die?</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer Q3</summary>  
<p>First die: P(3) = 1/6</p>  
<p>Second die even numbers (2,4,6): P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2</p>  
<p>Events are independent → multiply: (1/6) × (1/2) = 1/12</p>  
</details>

<p>Q4 (4 marks): A box has 6 red, 4 blue, and 5 green balls. One ball is taken at random. If the ball is red or green, what is the probability? Explain your reasoning.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer Q4</summary>  
<p>Total balls = 6 + 4 + 5 = 15.</p>  
<p>Since red and green are mutually exclusive events, add their probabilities:</p>  
<p>P(red or green) = P(red) + P(green) = 6/15 + 5/15 = 11/15.</p>  
<p>This is valid because the ball cannot be both red and green at the same time.</p>  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Probability</strong> – A number from 0 to 1 showing how likely an event is to happen.  
<strong>Event</strong> – A specific outcome or set of outcomes from a probability experiment.  
<strong>Outcome</strong> – A possible result of an experiment.  
<strong>Sample space</strong> – The set of all possible outcomes.  
<strong>Independent events</strong> – Events where the outcome of one does not affect the probability of the other.  
<strong>Mutually exclusive</strong> – Events that cannot happen at the same time.  
<strong>Complement</strong> – The event of an outcome not happening.  
<strong>Tree diagram</strong> – A graphical method to show all possible outcomes and their probabilities.  
<strong>Combined events</strong> – Events involving two or more steps or parts happening together.  
<strong>Relative frequency</strong> – An experimental estimate of probability based on observed outcomes.
