3 — DEFINITION  
<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A quadratic expression is an algebraic expression where the highest power of the variable is 2, usually written as ax² + bx + c (with a ≠ 0).</strong></p>  
<p>👉 A quadratic equation is an equation that sets a quadratic expression equal to a value, typically 0, like ax² + bx + c = 0.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Quadratics model many real-world problems involving area, projectile motion, and rates of change, and also form a major part of GCSE exams.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Quadratic expressions and equations work through these key links:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li>Expression → Factorising / Completing the square → Roots/solutions of equation</li>  
<li>Graphing → Shape (parabola) → Features (vertex, axis of symmetry, roots)</li>  
<li>Discriminant → Nature of roots (real or complex; equal or distinct)</li>  
</ul>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Factorising: x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)</li>  
<li>Quadratic formula: Solving 2x² - 4x - 6 = 0 using x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a</li>  
<li>Completing the square: Rewriting x² + 6x + 5 as (x + 3)² - 4</li>  
<li>Graphing y = x² - 4x + 3 to identify vertex and roots.</li>  
<li>Using the discriminant (b² – 4ac) to find number of solutions.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
<h2><strong>Teaching: Quadratics</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that quadratic expressions have a specific shape in graphs and set methods to find solutions.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0, and solutions are the values of x that make this true.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The graph of y = ax² + bx + c is a parabola, which either opens upwards (a > 0) or downwards (a < 0).</li>  
<li>Solutions correspond to x-intercepts (where y = 0) of the graph, also called roots.</li>  
<li>Completing the square rewrites quadratics to reveal the vertex (max/min point) directly.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Quadratic expression → shape of graph (parabola) → find roots and vertex → solve equations and model problems.</li>  
<li>Discriminant calculation (b² – 4ac) → tells the number and type of roots → helps predict graph behaviour without drawing.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining solutions, state the method clearly, name the formula you use, and give exact or simplified answers.</p>  
<p>👉 Always link roots from both algebraic and graph interpretations, showing understanding of both.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to forget the 'a' in the quadratic formula, especially in the denominator 2a, or to mix up the signs in ±.</p>  
<p>👉 The correct quadratic formula is x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a, with careful attention to order and grouping.</p>  
<p>👉 Using the wrong denominator or wrong signs loses method marks and can greatly affect the answer.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
<h2><strong>Step-by-step diagram (process)</strong></h2>  
Process: Solve quadratic by completing the square  

Step 1: Start with ax² + bx + c = 0 (ensure a = 1 by dividing if needed)  
↓  
Step 2: Move the constant term c to the other side: x² + bx = -c  
↓  
Step 3: Add (b/2)² to both sides to complete the square  
↓  
Step 4: Write the left side as a perfect square and solve for x by taking square roots  
Exam link: This method reveals the vertex and is essential for problems needing the minimum or maximum value of the quadratic.

16 — EXAM TIP  
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratic equations are fundamental in GCSE maths exams and appear in different formats — from algebraic solutions to graph interpretation and real-life problem contexts.</p>  
<p>👉 Mastering quadratics improves problem-solving skills and opens doors to higher maths concepts.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always check the form of your quadratic equation before choosing a solution method. Factorising is quick for simple expressions, but more complex quadratics demand completing the square or the quadratic formula.</p>  
<p>👉 Remember to interpret the discriminant first; it saves time by telling you if to expect two roots, one root, or none.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The quadratic formula solves quadratic equations.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The quadratic formula solves equations of the form ax² + bx + c = 0 by calculating the values of x using a formula involving a, b, and c.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The quadratic formula x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a accurately finds the roots of any quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0 by substituting coefficients a, b, and c. The discriminant (b² - 4ac) inside the square root determines if the roots are two distinct real numbers, one repeated root, or complex, which helps in understanding the graph's intersections with the x-axis.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly states the formula and its application.</li>  
<li>It includes the role of the discriminant, linking algebra and graph interpretations.</li>  
<li>It uses precise maths vocabulary and shows reasoning expected by examiners.</li>  
</ul>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Question:  
Solve the quadratic equation x² – 4x – 5 = 0 by factorising.  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Factorise: x² – 4x – 5 = (x – 5)(x + 1) = 0  
Set each factor to zero: x – 5 = 0 or x + 1 = 0  
Solutions: x = 5 or x = -1  
</details>

<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
x² + 8x + 5  
= (x + 4)² – 16 + 5  
= (x + 4)² – 11  
</details>  

Q4 (4 marks)  
Use the quadratic formula to solve 2x² – 4x – 6 = 0. Give your answers exact values.  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Coefficients: a = 2, b = –4, c = –6  
Discriminant: b² – 4ac = (–4)² – 4×2×(–6) = 16 + 48 = 64  
x = [-b ± √(discriminant)] / 2a  
= [4 ± 8] / 4  
Two solutions:  
x = (4 + 8)/4 = 12/4 = 3  
x = (4 – 8)/4 = –4/4 = –1  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<p><strong>Quadratic expression</strong> – An algebraic expression where the highest power of x is 2 (e.g., ax² + bx + c).</p>  
<p><strong>Quadratic equation</strong> – An equation that sets a quadratic expression equal to a value (usually 0).</p>  
<p><strong>Factorising</strong> – Expressing a quadratic as a product of two binomials.</p>  
<p><strong>Completing the square</strong> – Rewriting a quadratic to reveal its vertex form.</p>  
<p><strong>Quadratic formula</strong> – A formula x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a to find roots of any quadratic.</p>  
<p><strong>Roots</strong> – The solutions/x-values that make the quadratic equation true (where graph meets x-axis).</p>  
<p><strong>Vertex</strong> – The highest or lowest point on the quadratic graph (turning point).</p>  
<p><strong>Axis of symmetry</strong> – Vertical line through the vertex that divides the parabola into mirror halves.</p>  
<p><strong>Discriminant</strong> – The part b² – 4ac inside the quadratic formula that indicates the type/nature of roots.</p>  
<p><strong>Parabola</strong> – The U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.</p>
