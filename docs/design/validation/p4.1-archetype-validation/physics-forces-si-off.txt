9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Forces</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that forces can act alone or together, and their combination decides how an object moves or changes.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Each force has a size (magnitude) and a direction.</li>  
<li>When multiple forces act, you combine them as a resultant force - one single force with the same effect.</li>  
<li>If forces balance (resultant force = 0), the object’s motion doesn’t change.</li>  
<li>If forces are unbalanced (resultant force ≠ 0), the object speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Force vectors point in directions → vector sum gives resultant force → resultant force changes motion or shape.</li>  
<li>For example, gravity pulls down → tension in a rope pulls up → if equal, object stays still.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always explain forces in terms of size and direction, state if they balance or not, and link to changes in motion using “resultant force”.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say “forces cancel out and no forces act” when balanced forces are present.</p>  
<p>The correction: <strong>Balanced forces mean forces are equal and opposite, so their resultant force is zero, but forces are still acting on the object.</strong></p>  
<p>This correction earns marks because it shows understanding of forces as vector quantities, not just “turning off” forces.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Forces control how objects move in the real world—from cars stopping to rockets launching. In exams, understanding resultant forces is essential to explain changes in motion properly.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Don’t just say “forces make it move.” Be precise: say forces <em>combine as a resultant force</em>, and if this force isn’t zero, the object accelerates or changes motion. Always include direction and size where possible.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Forces make the object move faster.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The forces act in opposite directions, so the bigger force makes the object speed up.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The forces combine to give a resultant force in the direction of the larger force, causing the object to accelerate that way because unbalanced forces change motion.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly states cause (unbalanced resultant force), process (forces combine), and effect (acceleration in correct direction).</li>  
<li>Uses correct terminology like “resultant force” and “accelerate.”</li>  
<li>Avoids vague phrases like just “make move.”</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What unit is used to measure force?</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> State two effects a force can have on an object.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A car is moving at constant speed. Explain what this tells you about the forces acting on it.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Two people pull on a rope in opposite directions. Person A pulls with 150 N, Person B pulls with 100 N. Explain the resultant force on the rope and what will happen.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: The unit is newton (N).  

Q2: Forces can change an object’s speed or direction, and can also change its shape.  

Q3: The forces are balanced, so the resultant force is zero, meaning the car’s velocity doesn’t change and it moves at constant speed.  

Q4: The resultant force is 150 N − 100 N = 50 N in the direction Person A is pulling. This unbalanced force will cause the rope (and the people holding it) to accelerate towards Person A’s side.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Force</strong> – A push or pull on an object.  
<strong>Resultant force</strong> – The single force which has the same effect as all forces combined.  
<strong>Balanced forces</strong> – Forces that cancel each other, resultant force is zero.  
<strong>Unbalanced forces</strong> – Forces that don’t cancel; cause acceleration.  
<strong>Friction</strong> – A force resisting motion between surfaces in contact.  
<strong>Weight</strong> – The force of gravity acting on a mass.  
<strong>Tension</strong> – A pulling force in a stretched object like a rope.  
<strong>Contact force</strong> – A force that acts through physical contact.  
<strong>Non-contact force</strong> – A force that acts at a distance, like gravity.  
<strong>Newton (N)</strong> – The unit of force.
