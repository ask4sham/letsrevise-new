<p>👉 <strong>To solve right-angled triangles using trigonometry:</strong></p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Trigonometry</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is understanding how the sides of a right-angled triangle relate to its angles through ratios.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>If you know an angle and one side, you can find the other sides.</li>  
<li>By choosing the appropriate ratio (sine, cosine, or tangent), you connect sides and angles precisely.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Sine</strong> relates the <em>opposite</em> side to the <em>hypotenuse</em>.</li>  
<li><strong>Cosine</strong> relates the <em>adjacent</em> side to the <em>hypotenuse</em>.</li>  
<li><strong>Tangent</strong> relates the <em>opposite</em> side to the <em>adjacent</em> side.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Identify sides → select the correct trig function → substitute known values → calculate using a calculator.</li>  
<li>For angles, use the inverse function on your calculator to get the angle from the ratio.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always label the sides relative to the angle you're working from, show the formula used, substitute values clearly, and include units in your final answer.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing the "opposite" and "adjacent" sides relative to the given angle. This causes the wrong ratio to be used and leads to incorrect answers.</p>  
<p>A better way to say it is: always identify the angle you're working from, then spot the side directly opposite it and the side next to it that isn't the hypotenuse.</p>  
<p>This correction earns marks by showing clear reasoning and correct application of trig ratios.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Trigonometry questions are common in GCSE Maths because they test your understanding of angle-side relationships, problem-solving and algebra rearrangement all in one.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down the formula used and label the triangle sides clearly before starting calculations. This shows examiners your method and can earn method marks even if the final answer slips.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Use sine to find the side.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Use sine because it relates the opposite side to the hypotenuse.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Use the sine formula sin(θ) = opposite/hypotenuse because you know the angle θ and one side; rearrange to find the missing side by multiplying the hypotenuse by sin(θ).</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It shows understanding of the formula choice based on the triangle's sides.</li>  
<li>Clearly explains the rearrangement and calculation step.</li>  
<li>Uses precise terminology, demonstrating clear logic.</li>  
</ul>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
In a right-angled triangle, the angle is 30° and the hypotenuse is 12 cm. Calculate the length of the side opposite the 30° angle.

[Provide full solution reasoning.]

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Use sine since opposite/hypotenuse.  
Step 2: sin(30°) = opposite / 12  
Step 3: opposite = 12 × sin(30°) = 12 × 0.5 = 6 cm  
Answer: The opposite side is 6 cm.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Question 1 (1 mark):</strong> Calculate sin 45° to 3 decimal places.</p>

<p><strong>Question 2 (2 marks):</strong> In a right-angled triangle, the opposite side is 7 cm and the hypotenuse is 25 cm. Calculate the size of the angle opposite the 7 cm side.</p>

<p><strong>Question 3 (3 marks):</strong> A ladder leans against a wall making an angle of 60° with the ground. If the foot of the ladder is 4 m from the wall, calculate the length of the ladder.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Q1: sin 45° ≈ 0.707 (3 sf)  
Q2: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse = 7/25 = 0.28; θ = sin⁻¹(0.28) ≈ 16.26°, so angle ≈ 16.3° (2 sf)  
Q3: cos 60° = adjacent/hypotenuse → cos 60° = 4 / length → length = 4 / cos 60° = 4 / 0.5 = 8 m  
</details>  

<p><strong>Question 4 (4 marks):</strong> A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 15 cm and an angle of 40°. Calculate the lengths of the other two sides.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>  
Step 1: Label sides relative to 40°.  
Step 2: Opposite side = hyp × sin 40° = 15 × 0.6428 = 9.64 cm  
Step 3: Adjacent side = hyp × cos 40° = 15 × 0.7660 = 11.49 cm  
Answer: Opposite ≈ 9.6 cm, Adjacent ≈ 11.5 cm (both 3 sf)  
</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Trigonometry studies ratios relating sides and angles in right-angled triangles.</li>  
<li>The three main ratios: sine, cosine, and tangent link opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides.</li>  
<li>Use the ratio matched to the sides you know and the side or angle you want to find.</li>  
<li>For missing angles, use inverse trig functions on a calculator (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹).</li>  
<li>Label your triangle carefully to avoid confusion between opposite and adjacent sides.</li>  
<li>Always show clear formula substitution and final answers with correct units or angle measures.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Remember: <em>Identify → Select formula → Substitute → Calculate → Check.</em> This straightforward pathway leads to success in exams.</p>  
<p>👉 In the exam, do not just write the answer; show the reasoned steps with labels and formula usage to maximise marks.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Sine</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to hypotenuse in a right-angled triangle.  
<strong>Cosine</strong> – Ratio of adjacent side to hypotenuse in a right-angled triangle.  
<strong>Tangent</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to adjacent side in a right-angled triangle.  
<strong>Opposite</strong> – The side across from the angle being considered.  
<strong>Adjacent</strong> – The side next to the angle being considered (not the hypotenuse).  
<strong>Hypotenuse</strong> – The longest side opposite the right angle in a right-angled triangle.  
<strong>Inverse function</strong> – Function (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) used to find angles from ratios.  
<strong>Angle of elevation</strong> – Angle between horizontal and line of sight upwards.  
<strong>Calculate</strong> – To work out a numerical answer using mathematics.  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find the value of a missing side or angle using given information.
