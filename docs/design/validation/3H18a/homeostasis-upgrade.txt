<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The basic homeostasis pathway:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect a stimulus (change in internal/external environment).</li>
<li>Information goes to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> (brain/spinal cord/pancreas).</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> (muscles/glands) act to restore conditions to the <strong>optimum</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<ol>
  <li>Change happens (e.g. temperature rises)</li>
  <li>Receptors sense the change</li>
  <li>Coordination centre processes the signal</li>
  <li>Effector responds (e.g. sweats or releases hormone)</li>
  <li>Internal condition returns to optimum</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Homeostasis is about keeping your body’s internal environment stable — not fixed, but in its “sweet spot”.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The body must keep things like temperature, water, and blood sugar within narrow limits for cells and enzymes to work.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A change (stimulus) threatens the optimum condition (e.g. temp rises, blood glucose drops).</li>
<li>Special cells (receptors) pick up this change.</li>
<li>The brain, spinal cord, or pancreas (coordination centres) decide what to do.</li>
<li>Effectors (like sweat glands or the pancreas) make a change to restore the balance.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Receptors</strong> (e.g. skin temperature sensors) are adapted to detect small changes quickly.</li>
  <li><strong>Coordination centres</strong> (like the brain) process information from many sources.</li>
  <li><strong>Effectors</strong> (e.g. muscles/sweat glands) directly change body conditions.</li>
  <li>Working together, this keeps enzyme conditions close to optimum → enzymes stay active → you stay healthy.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Explain how…</strong></h3>
<p>Core temperature rises → Thermoreceptors detect this → Hypothalamus (brain) receives info → Sweat glands activated → Sweat evaporates → Body cools down → Back to optimum.</p>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, say which “part” is doing the job (receptor, coordination centre, effector), describe the process, and finish with the <em>effect</em> ("returns to optimum").</p>
<p>👉 Examiners expect a chain — not just “The body sweats.”</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is just naming a body part or response without linking the structure and function.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wrong:</strong> “The brain makes you sweat if you get hot.”</li>
<li><strong>Correct:</strong> “Thermoreceptors detect a rise in temperature. The hypothalamus (coordination centre) processes this, and sweat glands (effectors) produce sweat to lower body temperature.”</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Marks are lost if you skip steps — examiners want to see a clear sequence: receptor → coordination centre → effector → return to optimum.</p>
<p>👉 Students often confuse the <strong>stimulus</strong> (the change) and the <strong>response</strong> (the body’s action). Get the order right for top marks!</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 If your body didn’t use homeostasis, even small changes in temperature or blood sugar could quickly make you sick. Homeostasis lets animals, including humans, survive in lots of different conditions. For the exam, this topic links to every major system the body uses to keep you alive.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Always use the full chain: <strong>“Receptor detects… Coordination centre processes… Effector returns to optimum.”</strong> Examiners will not award full marks for partial descriptions, e.g. “brain tells sweat glands.” Name all three steps!</p>
<p>👉 Common mark-losing phrases: “the body cools itself” or “the enzymes work less” (too vague). Instead, use: “homeostasis restores optimum conditions so enzymes can work efficiently.”</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Homeostasis controls your body’s conditions so you don’t get sick.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions, using receptors, coordination centres, and effectors to keep factors like temperature and blood glucose at optimum levels so cells and enzymes work efficiently.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds if blood glucose (sugar) becomes higher than the optimum level after eating a meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Eating increases blood glucose (stimulus).
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this rise.
3. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases insulin (hormone).
4. Insulin triggers the liver and muscle cells (effectors) to remove glucose from the blood and store it as glycogen.
5. Blood glucose falls back to the optimum.
</details>

19— SYNTHESIS

<h2><strong>Synthesis: Homeostasis links</strong></h2>
<p>👉 Homeostasis is not one separate process – it involves every system in the body working together:</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>nervous system</strong> coordinates fast responses for things like temperature and water levels.</li>
<li>The <strong>endocrine system</strong> (hormones) manages slower, longer-lasting changes like blood sugar.</li>
<li>Cells, organs, and systems all depend on the right internal environment to stay alive and work.</li>
</ul>
<p>👉 In the exam, show that homeostasis means <strong>responding to change</strong>, not just “staying the same.”</p>

<strong>Osmoregulation</strong> – Control of water balance in the body</li>
