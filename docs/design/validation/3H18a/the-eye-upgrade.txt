<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Cornea → Lens → Retina</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea.</li>
<li>Cornea bends (refracts) most of the incoming light rays.</li>

<li>Special cells in the retina detect light and send electrical impulses to the brain for interpretation.</li>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is structure leads to function: each part of the eye is adapted to a specific role in vision.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The cornea, iris, lens, and retina work together to focus light and create clear images.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Cornea and lens refract (bend) light so it focuses on the retina.</li>
<li>Iris and pupil control the amount of light entering.</li>
<li>Lens can change shape (accommodation) to allow focusing on near or distant objects.</li>
<li>Retina’s photoreceptor cells detect light and convert it to electrical impulses.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cornea:</strong> curved surface → bends most entering light → starts focusing.</li>
<li><strong>Lens:</strong> elastic, changes shape → fine-focuses light → image sharp on retina.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles & suspensory ligaments:</strong> contract/relax → change lens shape → focus on near/far.</li>
<li><strong>Retina:</strong> layer of photoreceptors → detects focused light → sends signal to brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Examiners expect you to link structure and function: use phrases like “The lens changes shape to focus light (accommodation)” and “The retina contains photoreceptors that detect light and send impulses.</p>
<p>” Don’t just list parts—explain the effect of each part clearly.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying, “The lens does all the focusing” without mentioning the cornea, or saying "the retina sees the image".</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wrong:</strong> “The lens focuses the image so we can see.”</li>
<li><strong>Correct:</strong> “The cornea bends most light entering the eye, and the lens fine-focuses it onto the retina, which detects the light.”</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Examiners deduct marks for missing steps. You must link cornea → lens → retina, and state the retina detects, not “sees”, the image.</p>
<p>👉 Students often forget to explain <strong>how</strong> the lens focuses (mentioning ciliary muscles and ligaments for top marks on accommodation).</p>

16— EXAM TIP
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)
<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The eye lets us respond to changing environments – finding food, avoiding danger, and carrying out daily life. Vision is a fast feedback system for survival. In exams, structure and process links are often tested in 4–6 mark questions, so nailing these connections is key to top grades.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When explaining focusing (accommodation), <strong>always mention ciliary muscles AND suspensory ligaments</strong>. For highest marks, describe their action AND the lens change AND the effect on light rays. Use correct AQA terms: “accommodation”, “photoreceptors”, “refracts light”, “focused on the retina”. Avoid casual language like “the eye zooms in”.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>
<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The lens changes shape to focus on objects.</p>
<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The lens changes shape because of ciliary muscles so that it focuses light onto the retina.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>Ciliary muscles contract (for near objects), suspensory ligaments loosen, so the lens becomes thicker and more curved. This increases refraction so light rays from close-up objects are focused sharply on the retina, allowing us to see clearly.</p>
<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>It gives a clear cause → effect chain.</li>
<li>It links muscle action, ligament effect, lens shape, and light focusing – all mark scheme targets.</li>
<li>It avoids vague or casual phrasing and uses precise scientific terms.</li>
</ul>

Question:
Explain how the eye focuses light from a near object onto the retina. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. When focusing on a near object, the ciliary muscles contract.<br />
2. This causes the suspensory ligaments to slacken (loosen).<br />
3. The lens becomes thicker and more curved.<br />
4. The thicker lens refracts the light rays more, so they are focused exactly onto the retina for a sharp image.
</details>

<h2><strong>Synthesis: Linking Structure, Process, and Outcome</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The eye’s cornea and lens structure work together to focus light, the iris and pupil adjust light entry, and the retina’s photoreceptors detect the image. The process is a seamless chain, turning light energy into nerve signals so the brain can produce vision.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Structure → function:</strong> Each part’s shape and position enables a precise function in vision.</li>
<li><strong>Adaptation → advantage:</strong> Accommodation allows humans to see objects at different distances sharply, a crucial evolutionary benefit.</li>
<li><strong>Exam focus:</strong> Always link muscle/ligament actions to changes in lens shape and final focus on the retina.</li>
</ul>

<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>

</details>
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Explain how the cornea and lens work together to produce a clear image on the retina.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
The cornea first bends (refracts) most of the incoming light rays. The lens then changes shape for fine focusing (accommodation), ensuring that the light converges exactly on the retina. Both structures work together, so focused light stimulates photoreceptors, producing a clear image for the brain to interpret.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent curved front part of the eye, refracts most light entering.</li>
<strong>Lens</strong> – Elastic structure that changes shape to focus light onto the retina.</li>
<strong>Retina</strong> – Light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye; contains photoreceptors.</li>
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured ring of muscle controlling pupil size and light entry.</li>
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Opening in the centre of the iris that lets light into the eye.</li>
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that change lens shape during accommodation.</li>
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres connecting ciliary muscles to the lens, helping change its shape.</li>
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on objects at different distances.</li>
<strong>Photoreceptors</strong> – Cells in the retina that detect light and start nerve impulses.</li>
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes into the eye through structures like the cornea and lens.</li>
