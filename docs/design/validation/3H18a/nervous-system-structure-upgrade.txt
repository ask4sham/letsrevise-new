5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS (brain or spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A change (stimulus) is detected by a receptor (e.g. in the skin or eye).</li>
<li>The receptor sends an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone to the central nervous system (CNS).</li>
<li>The impulse is processed in the CNS (sometimes via a relay neurone).</li>
<li>An impulse travels along a motor neurone to an effector (muscle or gland).</li>
<li>The effector creates a response (muscle contracts or gland secretes).</li>
<li>This process is extremely rapid—sometimes forming a reflex to protect the body.</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The core idea is that the nervous system is structured to be fast and precise—linking receptors, neurones, the CNS, and effectors in a chain.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>Each part of the pathway is specialised (adapted) for its role.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors (like nerve endings in skin or cells in the eyes) are highly sensitive to specific types of stimulus—like touch, light, or temperature.</li>
<li>When a receptor is stimulated, it produces an electrical impulse, which is carried along a sensory neurone.</li>
<li>Sensory neurones bring information into the CNS (brain or spinal cord).</li>
<li>The impulse may pass to a relay neurone in the CNS, especially during reflexes.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses out from the CNS to effectors (muscles or glands).</li>
<li>Effectors then create the response—muscles contract (movement) or glands secrete chemicals.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Neurones are long, thin, and insulated (myelin sheath) for fast impulse transmission.</li>
<li>Synapses connect neurones, allowing signals to transfer but slow down just slightly so the system is controlled.</li>
<li>Adaptations like myelin mean signals quickly reach effectors—essential for survival!</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Examiners expect you to describe how the structure of each part (receptor, neurone, CNS, effector) lets it do its job, and use "carries impulses" instead of just "sends messages".</p>
<p>Don’t forget to name both the neurones and the direction of signal flow for full marks.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is writing “nerves send messages to the brain” without specifying <em>how</em> or in which direction.</p>
<ul>
<li>Wrong: “The nerve sends messages to the brain.”</li>
<li>Correct: “The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses from the receptor to the CNS.”</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> In the exam, just saying “messages” doesn’t score; you must mention electrical impulses, name the neurone, and say which way the impulse travels!</p>
<p>👉 Students often confuse muscles (which contract) and glands (which secrete) as effectors. Always specify the correct response for the type of effector in your answer.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Describe the pathway by which your body withdraws your hand from a sharp object, and explain why this response is so rapid. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The sharp object (stimulus) is detected by pain receptors in the skin.
2. The receptors send an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord (CNS).
3. In the spinal cord, the impulse passes across a relay neurone and then to a motor neurone.
4. The motor neurone carries the impulse to an effector (muscle), causing it to contract, which withdraws the hand quickly—this is a rapid reflex, meaning the brain is not directly involved so the response is almost instant.
</details>

19— SYNTHESIS
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)
<h2><strong>Synthesis: Connecting nervous system structure and function</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The nervous system is highly organised—every structure (from receptor to effector) is adapted to its role, creating a communication network that keeps you alive, moving, and responsive.</p>
<ul>
<li>Nerves are bundles of neurones that send fast electrical impulses—myelinated for speed.</li>
<li>Reflex arcs enable instant reactions without waiting for full brain processing.</li>
<li>Brain regions (cortex, cerebellum, medulla) have specialised roles—thinking, balance, vital functions.</li>
<li>Complex sense organs like the eye use precise structure (cornea, lens, retina) to create specialised responses to light.</li>
<li>Thermoregulatory centre in the brain and skin receptors work together to keep your core temperature safe.</li>
</ul>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by the body.<br />
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialised cell or group of cells that detects a specific stimulus.<br />
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.<br />
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Found in the CNS, connects sensory and motor neurones for fast responses.<br />
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Carries impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles or glands).<br />
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that brings about a response.<br />
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central Nervous System; includes the brain and spinal cord.<br />
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The tiny gap where impulses are transmitted chemically between neurones.<br />
<strong>Cerebral cortex</strong> – The outer layer of the brain; controls thought, memory, intelligence, language.<br />
<strong>Thermoregulatory centre</strong> – Area of the brain (hypothalamus) that monitors and controls core body temperature.
