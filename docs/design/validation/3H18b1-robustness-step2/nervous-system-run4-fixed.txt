9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is the nervous system allows fast communication to protect and control the body.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors detect a specific stimulus—like light, heat, or pressure—and convert it into an electrical impulse.</li>
<li>This impulse travels along sensory neurones to the CNS (brain and spinal cord), where the information is processed.</li>
<li>The CNS sends an impulse via motor neurones to effectors which produce a response by contracting muscles or secreting chemicals from glands.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are specialised cells sensitive to a certain type of stimulus, quickly converting it into electrical impulses.</li>
<li>Sensory neurones have dendrites to receive impulses and long axons to transmit impulses fast to the CNS.</li>
<li>Relay neurones connect sensory and motor neurones inside the CNS.</li>
<li>Motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS to the effectors.</li>
<li>Myelin sheath on neurones insulates axons and speeds up impulse transmission by saltatory conduction.</li>
<li>Effectors respond by contracting muscles or secreting hormones to create a fast, coordinated response.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Brain organisation:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cerebral cortex:</strong> responsible for conscious thought, memory, language, and intelligence.</li>
<li><strong>Cerebellum:</strong> controls balance, coordination, and fine movement.</li>
<li><strong>Medulla:</strong> controls unconscious activities like breathing and heartbeat.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Eye structure:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The eye contains receptors (photoreceptors) in the retina that detect light intensity and colour.</li>
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating light entry.</li>
<li>The lens focuses light onto the retina for clear vision.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Thermoregulation:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Temperature receptors in skin detect external temperature changes.</li>
<li>These impulses reach the thermoregulatory centre in the hypothalamus (part of the brain).</li>
<li>The body then responds by activating effectors like muscles to shiver or glands to sweat, maintaining body temperature.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always explain the full pathway from stimulus to response. Don’t just name parts; say how structure helps function and how one step leads to the next.</p>
<p>👉 Use the terms receptor, sensory neurone (to CNS), relay neurone (in CNS), motor neurone (to effector), effector, and response. Be precise about impulse direction and action.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistakes</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying “Nerves send messages” without explaining that these are electrical impulses carried by neurones and specifying the direction (sensory to CNS, then motor to effectors).</p>
<p>👉 Students often confuse motor and sensory neurones, reversing their functions.</p>
<p><strong>Correct this by:</strong> Explaining sensory neurones carry impulses <em>to</em> the CNS, while motor neurones carry impulses <em>away from</em> the CNS to muscles or glands.</p>
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> This precision is needed to score marks for describing the nervous system’s function properly.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When you see command words like “describe” or “explain,” always write answer chains that link: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response. Use correct terms and directional phrases like “carries impulses to” or “carries impulses away from.”</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing loss of electrical impulses.
2. Impulses jump between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).
3. This jumping speeds up transmission compared to continuous conduction in unmyelinated neurones.
4. Faster impulse transmission allows quicker responses to stimuli, improving survival chances.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialised cell that detects a stimulus.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by a receptor.
<strong>Response</strong> – The reaction to a stimulus by an effector.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone in the CNS that connects sensory to motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where impulses pass chemically.
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system; brain and spinal cord.
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system; nerves outside CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that brings about a response.
