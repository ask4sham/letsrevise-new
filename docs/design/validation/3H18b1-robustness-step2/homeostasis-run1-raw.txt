3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the process of keeping the internal conditions of an organism stable and within a narrow, optimum range, despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>This matters because enzymes and cells only work efficiently within certain conditions; if these conditions change too much, reactions slow or stop and the organism may become unhealthy or die.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>The key homeostasis model is: Receptors detect changes (stimuli) → Coordination centres process this information → Effectors produce a response to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>  
<p>Here is the detailed cause → effect reasoning chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus changes an internal condition away from its set optimum.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this change and send information to the coordination centre.</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes the information and decides the correct response.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) carry out the response to counteract the change.</li>  
<li>The internal condition moves back towards the optimum.</li>  
<li>The receptor detects the restored condition and stops triggering the response.</li>  
</ol>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Body temperature regulation:</strong> Thermoreceptors detect temperature changes; the hypothalamus coordinates; sweat glands and muscles help cool or warm the body.</li>  
<li><strong>Blood glucose level control:</strong> Pancreas acts as coordination centre; insulin and glucagon hormones are effectors to lower or raise glucose levels.</li>  
<li><strong>Water balance:</strong> Receptors detect water levels; brain and kidneys coordinate and respond by adjusting urine concentration or thirst.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions stable for optimum cell and enzyme function, no matter what happens outside.</p>  
  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>If an internal condition like temperature or blood glucose changes, that is called a <strong>stimulus</strong>.</li>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> detect this stimulus and send signals to the <strong>coordination centre</strong>, such as the brain or pancreas.</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes the information and sends commands to <strong>effectors</strong>, which include muscles or glands.</li>  
<li>The effectors respond by producing changes to restore the internal environment to the <strong>optimum</strong> level.</li>  
</ul>  
  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin have nerve endings sensitive to temperature changes → this allows them to detect small rises or falls quickly.</li>  
<li>The hypothalamus acts as a coordination centre → it processes receptor signals and triggers heat loss or gain mechanisms.</li>  
<li>Sweat glands produce sweat, which evaporates and removes heat → this cools the body down.</li>  
<li>Muscles causing shivering generate heat when cold → this warms the body up.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In your exam answers, don’t just say “the body cools down.” Instead say, “Thermoreceptors detect a rise in temperature and send signals to the hypothalamus (coordination centre). Sweat glands (effectors) produce sweat, which evaporates to remove heat, returning temperature to the optimum.”</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how negative feedback helps control blood glucose levels after a meal. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. Blood glucose rises after a meal (stimulus).  
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this rise.  
3. The pancreas acts as a coordination centre and releases insulin (effector hormone).  
4. Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose and the liver to store glucose as glycogen, lowering blood glucose back to the optimum.  
</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Homeostasis keeps internal conditions stable to ensure optimum function.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect changes (stimuli) in the body’s internal environment.</li>  
<li>Coordination centres like the brain or pancreas process this information.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles, glands) produce responses to restore conditions.</li>  
<li>Common examples include temperature, blood glucose, and water balance.</li>  
<li>Negative feedback ensures responses stop once optimum conditions are reached.</li>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions despite external changes.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment that is detected.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells that detect changes (stimuli) in the environment.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information from receptors and directs a response.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response to restore optimum conditions.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by effectors to counteract a change.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or most favourable conditions for enzyme and cell function.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control system that reverses a change to return conditions to optimum.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger released by glands to regulate body functions.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that speeds up biochemical reactions and works best under certain conditions.
