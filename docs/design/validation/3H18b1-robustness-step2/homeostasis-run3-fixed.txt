5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>The core process of homeostasis is: Receptors detect a change (stimulus) → Coordination centres process this information → Effectors produce a response to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>
<p>Let's break down a common pathway showing negative feedback: </p>
<ol>
<li>Stimulus causes a condition to move away from its ideal level.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this change and send signals to the coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes this information and decides the response.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) act to reverse the change.</li>
<li>The condition moves back towards its optimum level.</li>
<li>The response is switched off when the optimum is restored, preventing over-correction.</li>
</ol>
<p>👉 This cycle is called negative feedback and keeps conditions stable.</p>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions stable, even when external conditions change.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors detect stimuli – changes in the environment or inside the body.</li>
<li>These receptors send signals to coordination centres like the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas.</li>
<li>Coordination centres process this info and send commands to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) act to reverse the change and restore conditions to optimum.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors are cells sensitive to specific stimuli, such as thermoreceptors detecting temperature changes.</li>
<li>The brain acts as a coordination centre to process input and send out instructions.</li>
<li>Sweat glands as effectors secrete sweat that evaporates to cool the skin.</li>
<li>This cooling reduces core temperature back towards the ideal range, allowing enzymes to work properly.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the body gets hot so you sweat.</p>
<p>” Instead, explain: “Thermoreceptors detect an increase in temperature → information is processed by the hypothalamus (coordination centre) → sweat glands (effectors) produce sweat → evaporation removes heat → body temperature returns towards the optimum.</p>
<p>”</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body uses negative feedback to control blood glucose concentration after eating a meal. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Blood glucose levels rise after eating (stimulus).
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this rise.
3. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases insulin (effector).
4. Insulin causes cells to absorb glucose, lowering blood glucose back to optimum.
5. Once normal levels are restored, insulin secretion stops, preventing blood glucose from dropping too low.
</details>

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>

<p>👉 This cycle, known as negative feedback, prevents extremes and maintains stable internal conditions essential for life.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions despite external or internal changes
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects stimuli like temperature or chemical changes
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Part of the body (e.g., brain or pancreas) that processes information and directs responses
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that produces a response to restore balance
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to correct internal conditions
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The ideal condition for efficient enzyme and cell function
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A process that reverses a change to return the system to optimum
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger released into the blood to regulate body functions
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that speeds up biochemical reactions in the body, sensitive to conditions
