5— CORE MODEL

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p><strong>👉 Light passes through the cornea → then through the lens → and is focused onto the retina.</strong></p>
<p>Let’s break it down into cause and effect steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea</li>
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays</li>
<li>The iris adjusts the pupil size to control light amount</li>
<li>The lens changes shape to fine-focus the light (accommodation)</li>
<li>The focused light rays converge on the retina</li>
<li>Photoreceptor cells in the retina detect the light and convert it into electrical signals</li>
<li>Signals are sent to the brain for interpretation</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p><strong>👉 The key idea is:</strong> The eye works by bending and focusing light onto the retina to form clear images.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light enters first through the cornea, which is a curved, transparent layer.</li>
<li>Because the cornea is curved and denser than air, it refracts the light rays, bending them towards the inside of the eye.</li>
<li>The iris controls the pupil size to regulate how much light enters—bright lights cause the pupil to shrink, less light makes it open wider.</li>
<li>The lens then fine-tunes this focus. It changes shape with the help of ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</li>
<li>The retina, which lines the back of the eye, contains photoreceptor cells that detect focused light and send signals via the optic nerve to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The cornea’s curved shape refracts light → this initial bending directs light into the eye.</li>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker → light bends more strongly → image is focused on the retina for near objects.</li>
<li>Ciliary muscles relax → suspensory ligaments tighten → lens becomes thinner → light bends less → image is focused on the retina for distant objects.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say the lens 'focuses' light. Explain <em>how</em> the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments change the lens shape to adjust light bending, producing a focused image on the retina.</p>
<p>👉 Always use correct terms like <strong>accommodation</strong> and state that refraction happens mainly at the cornea and lens.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the eye focuses light from a nearby object onto the retina. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The ciliary muscles contract to reduce tension on suspensory ligaments.
2. This causes the suspensory ligaments to slacken.
3. As a result, the lens becomes thicker and more curved.
4. The thicker lens bends (refracts) light more strongly so the image of the nearby object is focused sharply on the retina.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – the transparent outer layer that refracts light into the eye.
<strong>Iris</strong> – colored part controlling pupil size to regulate light entry.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – the opening that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – a flexible structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – the layer at the back of the eye with photoreceptor cells detecting light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – connect ciliary muscles to the lens and alter its tension.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – process of changing lens shape to focus on objects at different distances.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – bending of light when it passes between different substances.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – cells in the retina sensitive to light, converting it to electrical signals.
