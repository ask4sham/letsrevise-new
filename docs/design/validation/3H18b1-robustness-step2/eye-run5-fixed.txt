9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 The eye focuses light onto the retina so images can be seen clearly.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The cornea provides most of the refraction — it bends light as it enters the eye.</li>
<li>The iris adjusts pupil size to control the amount of light entering the eye.</li>
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focus by changing shape depending on the distance of the object (accommodation).</li>
<li>Light is focused onto the retina, which contains photoreceptor cells that detect it.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent, so it refracts light efficiently to start focusing images.</li>
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> controls the pupil size, regulating light intensity to protect the retina and improve vision.</li>
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> contract or relax to change tension in the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, altering lens shape.</li>
<li>A thicker, more curved lens refracts light rays more strongly for near objects; a thinner lens does so less for distant objects.</li>
<li>The <strong>retina</strong> has photoreceptors (rods and cones) that detect light intensity and colour, converting this to electrical signals sent to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always explain how each structure causes the next step, like “The ciliary muscles contract causing the suspensory ligaments to slacken, so the lens becomes thicker and refracts light more strongly.”</p>
<p>👉 The phrase “light is refracted” is key to get marks. Avoid vague wording like “lens focuses” without detailing process.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – The curved, transparent front part of the eye that refracts light.
<strong>Iris</strong> – The coloured part of the eye that controls pupil size to regulate light.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – The hole in the iris that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – A flexible structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – The inner layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors that detect light.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape for accommodation.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Ligaments that attach to the lens and change its tension.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus on near or far objects.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light as it passes through different materials.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Special cells in the retina that detect light and send signals to the brain.
