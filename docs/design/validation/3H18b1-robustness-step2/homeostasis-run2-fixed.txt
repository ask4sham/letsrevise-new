9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps your internal environment stable so that your cells and enzymes perform best.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>If a condition like temperature or blood sugar moves away from the optimum, sensors called <strong>receptors</strong> detect this stimulus.</li>
<li>These receptors send information to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> which processes the data and decides what to do.</li>
<li>The coordination centre sends signals to <strong>effectors</strong> that respond to reverse the change and bring the condition back to optimum.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialised cells sensitive to specific stimuli (like temperature or glucose levels), allowing detection of even small changes.</li>
<li>The <strong>brain</strong> as a coordination centre integrates information quickly, coordinating complex responses.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> such as muscles can contract to produce heat, or glands can secrete hormones to adjust blood sugar.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the body reacts.” Use precise words: name the receptor, name the coordination centre, describe the effector’s action, and explain how that restores the optimum condition.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds when core temperature rises during exercise. (5 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Core body temperature rises above the optimum of around 37°C when exercising.
2. Thermoreceptors in the skin and brain detect this increase in temperature (receptors).
3. The hypothalamus acts as the coordination centre and receives this information.
4. The hypothalamus signals sweat glands to produce sweat and blood vessels near the skin to dilate (effectors).
5. Sweat evaporates from the skin surface, removing heat energy, and dilation of blood vessels increases heat loss, so temperature returns towards the optimum.
This is an example of negative feedback that restores balance.
</details>

19— SYNTHESIS

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The main idea in homeostasis is that the body uses receptors to detect changes (stimuli), coordination centres to process the information, and effectors to respond and restore conditions to their optimum.</p>
<p>👉 This process is controlled by negative feedback, which switches off the response once the condition returns to the normal range.</p>
<p>👉 Understanding this mechanism explains how temperature, blood glucose, and water levels stay stable despite external changes.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The process of maintaining a constant internal environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the internal or external environment.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells that detect stimuli or changes in conditions.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Structures like the brain or pancreas that process information.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that produce a response to restore conditions.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors to correct a change.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best or ideal conditions for enzymes and cells to work.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control system that reverses changes to maintain balance.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemicals released by glands that regulate body functions, such as insulin.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological molecules that speed up reactions and work best at stable conditions.
