<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea, which refracts (bends) it → then passes through the lens, which fine-tunes the focus by changing shape (accommodation) → finally, light rays are focused onto the retina where photoreceptors detect it and send signals to the brain.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Detailed cause → effect chain:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Light enters through the cornea</li>
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays</li>
<li>The iris adjusts the pupil size based on light intensity (= controls amount of light)</li>
<li>The lens changes shape via accommodation (ciliary muscles & suspensory ligaments)</li>
<li>Light rays converge onto the retina</li>
<li>Photoreceptors in the retina detect light and convert it into electrical impulses</li>
<li>Signals travel to the brain to be interpreted as images</li>
</ol>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong></p>
<p>The eye focuses light using multiple structures working together, enabling us to see clearly at different distances.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Light first passes through the cornea, a clear curved surface, which refracts light to start focusing it toward the retina.</li>
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil, adjusting the amount of light entering the eye based on brightness.</li>
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focus by changing shape via the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</li>
<li>This process is called accommodation and adjusts for near or distant vision.</li>
<li>Finally, the retina, which contains photoreceptor cells, detects the focused light and sends electrical impulses to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>The cornea is curved and transparent → it refracts light rays so they begin to focus toward the back of the eye.</li>
<li>The lens is flexible and changes shape → accommodation adjusts the focal length, allowing clear images at different distances.</li>
<li>Ciliary muscles contract or relax → suspensory ligaments tighten or slacken → lens shape changes.</li>
<li>The retina contains rods and cones → detects light intensity and colour → sends signals to the brain.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say "the eye focuses light" — say exactly how the cornea and lens refract light, and how accommodation involves ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments changing lens shape to focus images on the retina.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to write <em>"The lens focuses light"</em> without explaining how it happens.</p>
<p>This loses marks because <strong>examiners expect a mechanism</strong>: the lens changes shape via the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments to adjust light focus (accommodation).</p>
<p>👉 Another mistake is saying <em>"The retina sees the image."</em> Retinas <strong>do not see</strong> but <strong>detect light</strong> with photoreceptors, sending electrical signals to the brain where the image is formed.</p>
<p>Explaining these processes clearly and using correct terms can gain better marks.</p>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent front layer that refracts incoming light to start focusing it.
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured part that controls pupil size to regulate light entry.
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Opening in the iris that lets light into the eye.
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure that changes shape to fine-focus light on the retina.
<strong>Retina</strong> – The light-sensitive layer at the back containing photoreceptors.
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that contract/relax to change lens shape by affecting suspensory ligaments.
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that pull on or slacken the lens, controlling its shape.
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of adjusting lens shape to focus on objects at different distances.
<strong>Refraction</strong> – The bending of light when it passes between materials of different densities.
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Cells in the retina that detect light and convert it into nerve signals sent to the brain.
