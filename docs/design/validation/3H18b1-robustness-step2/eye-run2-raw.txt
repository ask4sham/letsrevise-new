5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → the lens fine-tunes the focus → the retina detects the focused image.</strong></p>  
<p>To fully explain, here is a detailed cause-effect chain:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the transparent cornea.</li>  
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays towards the centre of the eye.</li>  
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating light intensity entering the eye.</li>  
<li>The lens adjusts its shape via accommodation to fine-focus light rays.</li>  
<li>Light converges onto the retina, forming a sharp image.</li>  
<li>Light-sensitive photoreceptor cells in the retina detect the light and send signals to the brain.</li>  
</ol>

<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Cornea refraction:</strong> The cornea bends light rays sharply at entry because it is curved and transparent.</li>  
<li><strong>Lens accommodation:</strong> Ciliary muscles contract or relax changing the lens shape to focus near or far objects.</li>  
<li><strong>Retina image formation:</strong> The retina contains photoreceptors that detect light intensity and colour to form a visual image.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong> The eye is built to focus light perfectly onto the retina where images are detected and passed to the brain.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light passes through the cornea, which refracts it because of its curved transparent surface.</li>  
<li>The iris adjusts the pupil size to control how much light enters — smaller in bright light, larger in dim light.</li>  
<li>The lens fine-tunes focus through accommodation by changing shape, controlled by ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments.</li>  
<li>Finally, the focused light hits the retina where photoreceptors convert it into electrical signals.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The <strong>cornea</strong> is curved and transparent, designed to refract light towards the back of the eye, starting the focusing process.</li>  
<li>The <strong>iris</strong> is muscular to change the size of the <strong>pupil</strong>, controlling light amount, protecting the retina and optimizing vision.</li>  
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> contract to slacken the <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong>, allowing the <strong>lens</strong> to become thicker for near focusing; when they relax, the ligaments tighten, thinning the lens for distant focusing.</li>  
<li>The <strong>retina</strong> contains photoreceptors that respond to light intensity and colour, converting light into nerve impulses sent to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Explain how the eye focuses light on the retina:</strong></h3>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters through the cornea → it is refracted forward towards the lens.</li>  
<li>Iris adjusts pupil size to control light intensity entering.</li>  
<li>The lens changes shape via ciliary muscles; thickening for nearer objects focuses rays accurately.</li>  
<li>Light rays converge precisely on the retina → forming a sharp image.</li>  
<li>Photoreceptors detect this focused image and send impulses to the brain.</li>  
</ol>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In exam answers, always explain the process as a chain: say what happens first, what causes the next step, and what effect it has on vision.</p>  
<p>👉 Do NOT just name parts; explain how the cornea and lens refract light, how accommodation adjusts lens shape, and how this focuses images on the retina.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake: confusions about how the eye focuses light</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is saying <strong>"The lens focuses light"</strong> with no explanation of how it changes shape.</p>  
<p><strong>Why this is wrong:</strong> Examiners expect you to mention <strong>ciliary muscles</strong> and <strong>suspensory ligaments</strong> that change lens shape during accommodation.</p>  
<p><strong>Correct explanation:</strong> The ciliary muscles contract → suspensory ligaments slacken → lens becomes thicker → light bends more for near objects, ensuring clear focus.</p>  
<p><strong>Exam link:</strong> Missing this sequence loses marks because it doesn’t explain the cause-effect process.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE

Question:  
Explain how the eye focuses light to form a clear image on the retina. (6 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
1. Light enters through the cornea, which refracts (bends) the light rays forward.  
2. The iris adjusts the pupil to control the light amount.  
3. The lens changes shape by accommodation: ciliary muscles contract or relax.  
4. When focusing on a near object, ciliary muscles contract and suspensory ligaments slacken.  
5. This makes the lens thicker and more curved, increasing refraction.  
6. Light rays converge perfectly on the retina, where photoreceptors detect the image and send signals to the brain.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – Transparent curved front of eye that refracts light.<br />  
<strong>Iris</strong> – Coloured muscular part controlling pupil size.<br />  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – Opening controlling light entrance.<br />  
<strong>Lens</strong> – Flexible structure that changes shape for focusing.<br />  
<strong>Retina</strong> – Layer containing photoreceptors detecting light.<br />  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – Muscles that control lens shape.<br />  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – Fibres that connect ciliary muscles to lens.<br />  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – The process of changing lens shape to focus.<br />  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – Bending of light as it passes between materials.<br />  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – Light-sensitive cell in the retina that detects light intensity and colour.<br />
