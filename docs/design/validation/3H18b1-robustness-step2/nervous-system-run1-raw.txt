5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → central nervous system (CNS) → motor neurone → effector → response</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>The receptor sends an electrical impulse along a sensory neurone to the CNS.</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the information and sends an impulse via a motor neurone.</li>  
<li>The motor neurone signals an effector (muscle or gland) to produce a response.</li>  
<li>This pathway enables quick and coordinated reactions to changes.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system detects stimuli using specialised receptors and quickly sends electrical impulses to bring about coordinated responses.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors detect changes (stimuli) in the environment—like light, heat, or pressure.</li>  
<li>These receptors convert the stimulus into an electrical impulse.</li>  
<li>The impulse travels along sensory neurones to the CNS, which includes the brain and spinal cord.</li>  
<li>The CNS processes the information and sends impulses via motor neurones to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce responses, such as contracting muscles or secreting hormones.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors in eyes).</li>  
<li><strong>Sensory neurones</strong> have long dendrites and axons that carry impulses quickly to the CNS.</li>  
<li><strong>Relay neurones</strong> in the CNS link sensory and motor neurones for coordination.</li>  
<li><strong>Motor neurones</strong> have long axons to send impulses to effectors effectively.</li>  
<li><strong>Myelin sheath</strong> insulates neurones, making impulse transmission faster.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “messages go through nerves.” Instead, say “electrical impulses travel along sensory neurones to the CNS, then motor neurones carry impulses to effectors which respond.” Use correct terms like receptor, sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, and effector for full marks.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon, preventing loss of electrical signal.  
2. Impulses jump across gaps called nodes of Ranvier in a process called saltatory conduction.  
3. This reduces the time taken for impulses to travel along the neurone.  
4. As a result, effectors respond faster to stimuli, helping the organism react quickly to danger or changes.  
</details>

Question:  
Describe the role of motor neurones in the nervous system and explain how their structure helps this function.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  
Motor neurones carry electrical impulses away from the CNS to effectors (muscles or glands). Their long axon allows fast transmission over distances, and the myelin sheath insulates the axon to increase impulse speed, enabling rapid responses.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialised cell that detects a stimulus.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by effectors after receiving a signal.  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone within the CNS that connects sensory and motor neurones.  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between two neurones where chemical transmission occurs.  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system; the brain and spinal cord, processing information.  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system; all neurones outside the CNS.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that responds to a nerve impulse.
