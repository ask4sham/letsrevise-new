<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis is the process of regulating internal conditions to keep them at optimum levels for cells and enzymes, despite changes inside or outside the body.</strong></p>

4 — WHY IT MATTERS

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis relies on a pathway: Receptors detect changes → Coordination centres process information → Effectors respond to restore optimum conditions.</strong></p>  
<p><strong>Example of cause → effect chain:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>A change occurs that moves conditions away from the optimum.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus (a change in condition).</li>  
<li>Coordination centre (like the brain) receives and processes the information.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) bring about a response.</li>  
<li>The response restores the condition to the optimum level.</li>  
<li>Once restored, the receptors stop sending signals (negative feedback).</li>  
</ol>

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Key examples</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Body temperature regulation:</strong> Thermoreceptors detect temperature changes, the hypothalamus coordinates, and sweat glands or muscles adjust heat loss or generation.</li>  
<li><strong>Blood glucose control:</strong> Receptors in the pancreas detect blood glucose levels, the pancreas coordinates, and effectors like the liver or pancreas release hormones like insulin and glucagon.</li>  
<li><strong>Water balance:</strong> Receptors sense blood water levels, the brain coordinates, and kidneys act as effectors by changing urine concentration.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that homeostasis keeps internal conditions steady so cells work efficiently.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus causes a change in an internal condition, such as temperature or glucose level.</li>  
<li>Receptors detect this stimulus and send electrical or chemical signals to coordination centres (brain, spinal cord, or pancreas).</li>  
<li>The coordination centre processes the information and decides on a response.</li>  
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) act to reverse the change and restore optimum conditions.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors like thermoreceptors are sensitive to specific stimuli (temperature).</li>  
<li>The brain acts as a coordination centre, integrating sensory input.</li>  
<li>Sweat glands as effectors produce sweat, which evaporates to cool the body, lowering temperature to optimum.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In your answer, always explain the full chain: the stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → return to optimum.</p>  
<p>👉 Use specific terms like thermoreceptors, hypothalamus, sweat glands, and evaporation to gain full marks.</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like “the body sweats to cool down” without mentioning how evaporation removes heat.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose levels rise after eating a meal. (4 marks)  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. The rise in blood glucose is the stimulus.  
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect this increase.  
3. The pancreas acts as the coordination centre and releases insulin into the blood.  
4. Insulin causes effectors like the liver and muscle cells to absorb glucose and convert it to glycogen.  
5. Blood glucose levels fall back to the optimum.  

</details>

19 — SYNTHESIS  
Paste into: Synthesis (key idea)

<h2><strong>Synthesis</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Homeostasis depends on a precise communication system: receptors sense change, coordination centres decide on correction, and effectors act to fix the problem.</p>  
<p>👉 This process works through negative feedback, switching effectors on and off to keep conditions stable.</p>  
<p>👉 Without homeostasis, enzymes would denature and cells would malfunction, leading to illness or death.</p>  

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Why do enzymes need the body to maintain optimum conditions through homeostasis?  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Enzymes work only within narrow optimum conditions of temperature and pH. If conditions change, enzymes become less effective or denature, disrupting biochemical reactions essential for life. Homeostasis keeps internal conditions stable so enzymes function properly.  

</details>  

21 — FINAL MEMORY RULE  
Paste into: Final memory rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>💡 Key Insight</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis uses negative feedback: a change away from optimum is detected by receptors → information sent to coordination centres → effectors produce a response → conditions return to optimum → the response stops.</strong></p>  
<p>👉 This cycle continuously protects cells and enzymes so your body functions reliably.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – Maintaining stable internal conditions for best cell function.  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that affects conditions.  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Detects changes in internal or external environment.  
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Processes information and signals effectors.  
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscle or gland that responds to restore optimum conditions.  
<strong>Response</strong> – Action taken to reverse change and restore balance.  
<strong>Optimum</strong> – Best conditions for enzymes and cells to work.  
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – Mechanism that stops responses once conditions return to optimum.  
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messenger transported in blood affecting effectors.  
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Protein that speeds up reactions but needs stable conditions to work well.
