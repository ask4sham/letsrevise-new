5— CORE MODEL
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Homeostasis works through a simple pathway:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>A <strong>receptor</strong> detects a change (stimulus) in internal conditions.</li>
<li>The information is sent to a <strong>coordination centre</strong> (such as the brain, spinal cord, or pancreas) which processes it.</li>
<li>The coordination centre activates an <strong>effector</strong> (a muscle or gland) that produces a <strong>response</strong> to counteract the change.</li>
<li>The response restores internal conditions back to their <strong>optimum</strong> level.</li>
<li>When optimum is reached again, the response stops, maintaining balance.</li>
</ol>
<p>Example chain: Stimulus (temperature rises) → receptor detects change → brain coordinates → sweat glands activated → body cools down → temperature returns to optimum.</p>

9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Homeostasis</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is homeostasis maintains steady internal conditions despite changes outside or inside the body.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A stimulus causes internal conditions to move away from the optimum.</li>
<li>Receptors detect this change and send signals to a coordination centre.</li>
<li>The coordination centre processes this information and sends instructions to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors respond to reverse the change, restoring optimum conditions.</li>
<li>Once balance is restored, the response stops, preventing overcorrection.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Receptors</strong> are specialized cells sensitive to specific stimuli, allowing detection of changes quickly.</li>
<li>The <strong>coordination centre</strong> (like the hypothalamus for temperature) integrates information to decide the right response.</li>
<li><strong>Effectors</strong> such as muscles or glands act to restore balance (muscles shiver to generate heat, glands sweat to cool).</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Explain how this works in temperature regulation:</strong></h3>
<ol>
<li>Core body temperature rises above 37°C (optimum).</li>
<li>Thermoreceptors in the skin and brain detect the rise (stimulus).</li>
<li>Hypothalamus (coordination centre) receives this information.</li>
<li>It activates effectors: sweat glands release sweat; blood vessels near skin dilate.</li>
<li>Sweat evaporates, removing heat energy; dilation increases heat loss.</li>
<li>Core temperature decreases back toward optimum, switching off the response.</li>
</ol>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Don’t just say “the body sweats,” say “thermoreceptors detect a rise in temperature, the hypothalamus activates sweat glands, sweat evaporation removes heat, returning temperature to optimum.</p>
<p>” This clear chain earns full marks.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain how the body responds when blood glucose concentration is too high.
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. Eating food causes blood glucose concentration to rise (stimulus).
2. Receptors in the pancreas detect the high glucose level.
3. The pancreas acts as a coordination centre and releases insulin (effector signal).
4. Insulin causes liver and muscle cells to absorb glucose and convert it to glycogen for storage (response).
5. Blood glucose concentration decreases back to the optimum level.
6. When optimum is reached, insulin release stops, maintaining balance.
</details>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What term describes the process of keeping internal conditions stable?</p>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain how homeostasis regulates body temperature when it is too low.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
Thermoreceptors detect a drop in body temperature and send information to the hypothalamus. The hypothalamus activates muscles to shiver, producing heat through muscle contractions and reduces blood flow to the skin to conserve heat, returning temperature towards the optimum.
</details>
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Describe how the pancreas helps to regulate blood glucose concentration after eating a large meal.</p>
<details>
<summary>Reveal Model Answer</summary>
After a large meal, blood glucose concentration rises (stimulus). Receptors in the pancreas detect this change. The pancreas (coordination centre) releases insulin, which causes muscle and liver cells (effectors) to take up glucose and convert it to glycogen. This lowers blood glucose back to the optimum level, and insulin secretion stops once balance is restored.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Homeostasis</strong> – The process of keeping internal body conditions stable.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment detected by receptors.
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells that detect stimuli and send information.
<strong>Coordination centre</strong> – Part of the body, like the brain, that processes information.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that produce a response to restore balance.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken to reverse a change in conditions.
<strong>Optimum</strong> – The best level or condition for enzymes and cells to function.
<strong>Negative feedback</strong> – A control system that reverses changes to maintain stability.
<strong>Hormone</strong> – Chemical messengers involved in homeostasis, such as insulin.
<strong>Enzyme</strong> – Biological catalysts affected by changes in conditions like temperature and pH.
