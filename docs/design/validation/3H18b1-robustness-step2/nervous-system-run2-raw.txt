5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → CNS (brain and spinal cord) → Motor neurone → Effector → Response</strong></p>  
<p>👉 This sequence explains how the body detects a change and brings about a reaction.</p>  
<ol>  
<li>A stimulus is detected by receptors.</li>  
<li>Receptors send electrical impulses through sensory neurones.</li>  
<li>The impulses reach the CNS, where they are processed.</li>  
<li>The CNS sends impulses via motor neurones to effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors respond by contracting muscles or secreting from glands.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system lets the body detect and react to stimuli very rapidly using electrical impulses.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>A stimulus, like heat or pressure, activates special receptor cells in the body.</li>  
<li>These receptors convert the stimulus into an electrical impulse.</li>  
<li>Impulses are carried by sensory neurones to the central nervous system (the brain and spinal cord).</li>  
<li>The CNS processes this information and sends instructions via motor neurones to the effectors.</li>  
<li>Effectors—muscles or glands—then produce the appropriate response, like contracting or secreting.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Receptors are adapted to detect specific stimuli (e.g., light receptors in the eye detect light).</li>  
<li>Sensory neurones have long dendrites to carry impulses quickly from receptors to the CNS.</li>  
<li>Motor neurones have long axons to carry impulses from the CNS to effectors efficiently.</li>  
<li>The myelin sheath on many neurones speeds up impulse transmission by insulating the axon.</li>  
<li>Sarcolemma and contractile fibres in muscles allow rapid contraction as a response effect.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering exam questions, always explain the flow: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector → response. Use terms like “sensory neurone carries impulses to the CNS” and “motor neurone carries impulses away from the CNS to effectors.”</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague descriptions like “messages travel through nerves” without mentioning electrical impulses or the direction of travel.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake: confusing sensory and motor neurones</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say “motor neurones carry impulses to the brain.” This is incorrect.</p>  
<p>👉 Sensory neurones carry impulses <em>to</em> the CNS (brain or spinal cord), while motor neurones carry impulses <em>away</em> from the CNS to effectors.</p>  
<p>👉 <strong>Exam link:</strong> Not specifying the direction of impulse means losing marks because the pathway’s correct flow is essential.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The nervous system’s rapid communication is vital for survival, helping organisms respond to danger and maintain body functions quickly and efficiently.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 For command words like "Explain" or "Describe," always show the cause and effect chain clearly:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>Start with the stimulus and receptor.</li>  
<li>Use precise terms like "sensory neurone," "CNS," "motor neurone," and "effector."</li>  
<li>Explain the direction of impulse travel.</li>  
<li>Describe the effector’s response.</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague answers like "messages travel" without specifying electrical impulses or direction.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone sends messages to the brain, and the muscles respond.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses to the brain, which sends impulses to muscles to contract.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The sensory neurone carries electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS, where impulses are processed. The motor neurone then carries impulses away from the CNS to the muscles, causing them to contract and produce a response.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the direction of impulses clearly.</li>  
<li>It uses correct terminology aligned with the specification.</li>  
<li>It links structure (neurone types) to function (impulse transmission and response).</li>  
</ul>

Answer:

<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

2. The receptors generate an electrical impulse that travels along the sensory neurone.<br />

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A specialized cell that detects stimuli.<br />  
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.<br />  
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by effectors after stimulation.<br />  
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – Neurone that connects sensory and motor neurones in the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – Neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.<br />  
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The small gap where neurones communicate using chemicals.<br />  
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system, consisting of the brain and spinal cord.<br />  
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system, all neurones outside the CNS.<br />  
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response to an impulse.<br />
