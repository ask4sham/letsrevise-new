9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is that the nervous system coordinates rapid responses to environment changes by transmitting electrical impulses.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>A receptor detects a specific stimulus such as light, temperature, or touch.</li>
<li>The receptor converts the stimulus into an electrical impulse transmitted by a sensory neurone.</li>
<li>The impulse travels to the CNS (brain and spinal cord), where it is processed.</li>
<li>The CNS sends an impulse along a motor neurone to an effector (muscle contracts or gland secretes).</li>
<li>This causes an immediate response, such as moving away from harm.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors have cell membranes sensitive to specific stimuli, enabling detection.</li>
<li>Sensory neurones have long dendrites and axons carrying impulses quickly to the CNS.</li>
<li>The CNS acts as a coordination centre, integrating information from many sensory inputs.</li>
<li>Motor neurones have long axons to send impulses rapidly to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles/glands) are adapted to perform quick contractions or secretion.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 When answering, use the structure → function link and describe the pathway step-by-step: receptor detects stimulus → sensory neurone transmits to CNS → motor neurone transmits to effector → response produced.</p>
<p>Use terms like sensory neurone carries impulses “to” the CNS and motor neurone carries impulses “away” from the CNS.</p>
<p>Avoid vague phrases like “messages are sent” without specifying electrical impulses or neurones involved.</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The nervous system controls how organisms quickly react to dangers and changes in their environment, increasing survival chances.</p>
<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>
<p>👉 When the exam question asks to explain or describe the nervous system pathway, always include the direction of impulses and name the neurones.</p>
<p>👉 Use exact terms like “sensory neurone carries impulses to the CNS” and “motor neurone carries impulses away from the CNS.”</p>
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases such as “messages” or “signals” without specifying electrical impulses or neurone types.</p>

<p>The sensory neurone carries impulses to the brain, then motor neurones send impulses to muscles to contract.</p>
<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>
<p>The receptor detects the stimulus and sends an electrical impulse along the sensory neurone to the CNS (brain or spinal cord). The CNS processes the information and sends impulses along the motor neurone to the effector (muscle), which contracts to produce a response.</p>
<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Clearly uses correct terminology: receptor, sensory neurone, CNS, motor neurone, effector.</li>

Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon preventing electrical impulse loss.
2. Impulses jump between gaps called nodes of Ranvier in a process called saltatory conduction.
3. This speeds up the transmission of impulses along the neurone.
4. Faster transmission allows quicker responses to stimuli, increasing survival chances.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – Cells that detect stimuli in the environment.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action taken by an organism after receiving a stimulus.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – A neurone within the CNS that passes impulses between sensory and motor neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between neurones where chemical neurotransmitters cross.
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central Nervous System; brain and spinal cord that coordinate responses.
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral Nervous System; all neurones outside the CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – Muscles or glands that carry out the response.
