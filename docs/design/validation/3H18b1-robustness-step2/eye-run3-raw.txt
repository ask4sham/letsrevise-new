5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<h2><strong>Core model</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Light enters the eye through the cornea → is refracted and focused by the lens → and forms an image on the retina.</strong></p>  
<p>👉 Here is a detailed chain to help explain focusing:</p>  
<ol>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea.</li>  
<li>The cornea refracts (bends) the light rays towards the pupil.</li>  
<li>The iris controls pupil size to regulate light intensity entering.</li>  
<li>The lens further refracts light, fine-tuning focus by changing shape through accommodation.</li>  
<li>The light converges on the retina.</li>  
<li>Photoreceptor cells in the retina detect light and send impulses via the optic nerve to the brain.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: The eye</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong> The eye focuses light so the brain can form a clear image, enabling us to see.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Light enters the eye through the cornea, which is transparent and curved to bend the light rays.</li>  
<li>The iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating how much light enters depending on brightness.</li>  
<li>The lens fine-tunes the focus by changing shape using the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments. This process is called accommodation.</li>  
<li>Light rays focus onto the retina, where photoreceptors detect the image and send signals to the brain.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The <strong>cornea’s curved surface</strong> refracts light sharply → this starts focusing light towards the retina.</li>  
<li><strong>Iris</strong> adjusts pupil size → controls light intensity entering the eye → protects retina from damage and improves vision.</li>  
<li><strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> contract → suspensory ligaments relax → lens becomes thicker and more curved → light refracts more strongly → near object focused on retina.</li>  
<li>Opposite happens for distant objects → ciliary muscles relax → ligaments tighten → lens is thinner → light refracted less.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, don’t just say “the lens focuses light.” Instead, explain how <strong>ciliary muscles contract to change lens shape</strong>, adjusting focus to make the image clear on the retina.</p>

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say <em>"The lens zooms in to focus."</em> This is incorrect in exam answers.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Clear vision relies on the eye focusing light accurately on the retina. If the focus is wrong, images are blurry, leading to poor vision or even blindness.</p>  
<p>👉 Understanding accommodation is essential because it explains how we see clearly at different distances.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering exam questions, always explain mechanism steps — for example, "Ciliary muscles contract causing suspensory ligaments to slacken, making the lens thicker and increasing refraction."</p>  
<p>👉 Avoid vague phrases like "the eye focuses" without explaining how.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light to help us see.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The eye focuses light because the lens changes shape.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Light is refracted by the cornea and then the lens. The ciliary muscles contract to slacken suspensory ligaments, making the lens thicker to refract light more strongly, so the image focuses precisely on the retina.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It describes the role of both cornea and lens in refraction.</li>  
<li>It explains the process of accommodation with specific structures.</li>  
<li>It shows clear cause and effect leading to the final result of focusing light on the retina.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<h2><strong>Key words</strong></h2>  
<strong>Cornea</strong> – transparent, curved front surface that refracts light entering the eye  
<strong>Iris</strong> – coloured part of the eye that controls the size of the pupil  
<strong>Pupil</strong> – opening in the iris that allows light to enter the eye  
<strong>Lens</strong> – flexible structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina  
<strong>Retina</strong> – light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing photoreceptors  
<strong>Ciliary muscles</strong> – muscles that contract or relax to change lens shape during accommodation  
<strong>Suspensory ligaments</strong> – fibres that connect the lens to ciliary muscles and adjust lens tension  
<strong>Accommodation</strong> – the process of changing lens shape to focus light from different distances  
<strong>Refraction</strong> – bending of light as it passes through different materials  
<strong>Photoreceptor</strong> – light-detecting cell in the retina that sends nerve impulses to the brain
