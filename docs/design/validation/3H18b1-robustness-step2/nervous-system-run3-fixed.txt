9— CORE TEACHING
Paste into: Text (concept)
<p>Let's build this step by step.</p>
<h2><strong>Teaching: Structure and function of the nervous system</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The nervous system controls rapid responses by detecting stimuli and coordinating effectors through electrical impulses.</p>
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>
<p>The nervous system structures are specialised and linked to their functions for fast, accurate responses.</p>
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors detect specific stimuli (like light, heat, or pressure) and convert them into electrical impulses.</li>
<li>Sensory neurones carry electrical impulses from receptors to the CNS.</li>
<li>Within the CNS, relay neurones process and coordinate signals.</li>
<li>Motor neurones transmit impulses from the CNS to effectors.</li>
<li>Effectors (muscles or glands) produce a response such as muscle contraction or hormone secretion.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Receptors have membranes sensitive to specific stimuli, enabling detection.</li>
<li>Neurones have long axons insulated by myelin sheath, speeding impulse transmission.</li>
<li>Dendrites receive impulses and transmit them to the cell body.</li>
<li>The CNS integrates information efficiently using relay neurones.</li>
<li>Effectors act precisely to respond, protecting the body or adjusting internal conditions.</li>
</ul>
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Always explain the direction of impulse travel for marks — e. g. , sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS, motor neurones carry impulses away from the CNS to effectors.</p>
<p>Use terms like receptor, neurone, CNS, and effector clearly.</p>

12— COMMON MISTAKE
Paste into: Common mistake
<h2><strong>Common mistake: Confusing sensory and motor neurones roles</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is to say "motor neurones send impulses to the brain" or that "sensory neurones carry impulses away from the CNS."</p>
<p>👉 The correct explanation is sensory neurones carry impulses <strong>to</strong> the CNS, allowing the body to detect information, while motor neurones carry impulses <strong>away</strong> from the CNS to effectors to produce a response.</p>
<p>👉 <strong>Exam link:</strong> Using the correct direction of impulse travel and naming the correct neurone type is essential to gain full marks.</p>

17— WORKED EXAMPLE
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)
Question:
Explain why myelinated neurones transmit electrical impulses faster than unmyelinated neurones. (4 marks)
Answer:
<details>
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>
1. The myelin sheath insulates the axon of the neurone.
2. Electrical impulses jump between gaps in the myelin called nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction).
3. This reduces the loss of electrical signal and speeds up impulse transmission.
4. As a result, impulses reach effectors faster, allowing quicker responses to stimuli.
</details>

25— KEY WORDS
Paste into: Key words
<p><strong>👉 Keywords</strong></p>
<strong>Receptor</strong> – A cell that detects a stimulus and starts an electrical impulse.
<strong>Stimulus</strong> – A change in the environment that is detected by receptors.
<strong>Response</strong> – The action carried out by an effector after receiving an impulse.
<strong>Sensory neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses to the CNS from receptors.
<strong>Relay neurone</strong> – A neurone inside the CNS that passes impulses between other neurones.
<strong>Motor neurone</strong> – A neurone that carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
<strong>Synapse</strong> – The gap between two neurones where chemicals transmit impulses.
<strong>CNS</strong> – Central nervous system; brain and spinal cord.
<strong>PNS</strong> – Peripheral nervous system; all neurones outside the CNS.
<strong>Effector</strong> – A muscle or gland that produces a response when stimulated.
