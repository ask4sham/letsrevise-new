9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Simultaneous Equations</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>📘 The key idea is:</strong></p>  
<p>We have two equations with two unknowns, and solving simultaneously finds values of both variables that work in both equations.</p>  

<p><strong>Method → reason:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Substitution:</strong> Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation, then substitute into the other equation. This reduces two variables to one, making it easier to solve.</li>  
<li><strong>Elimination:</strong> Multiply one or both equations to get the same coefficient for one variable, then add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable and solve for the other.</li>  
</ul>  

<p><strong>Step → result:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li>Get one equation with one variable → solve it → substitute back → find second variable.</li>  
</ul>  

<p><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></p>  
<p>👉 In the exam, show every substitution and rearrangement clearly. Label variables and keep your working tidy. Write your solution as a pair (x = value, y = value) to earn full marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is not multiplying the whole equation evenly during elimination, resulting in incorrect coefficients and no variable eliminated.</p>  
<p>Students sometimes multiply only one term, forgetting to distribute across the entire equation.</p>  
<p>👉 Always check both sides of the equation are multiplied by the same number to keep equations equivalent, so elimination works properly and you don't lose marks in the exam.</p>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Solve the simultaneous equations: 3x + 2y = 12 and x - y = 1. Give your answer as (x, y).

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

From x - y = 1, rearrange: x = y + 1  
Substitute into 3x + 2y = 12: 3(y + 1) + 2y = 12 → 3y + 3 + 2y = 12 → 5y + 3 = 12  
5y = 9 → y = 9/5 = 1.8  
x = 1.8 + 1 = 2.8  
Answer: (2.8, 1.8)  

</details>

21 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the definition of simultaneous equations?</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve the system: x + y = 5 and x - y = 1.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain how substitution works to solve simultaneous equations and solve: 2x + y = 8 and y = x + 1.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 20 and 5x - 4y = 8. Show all steps clearly and state your final solution as a pair.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: Two or more equations with two or more unknowns solved together to find common values.  

Q2:  
Add: (x + y) + (x - y) = 5 + 1 → 2x = 6 → x = 3  
Substitute: 3 + y = 5 → y = 2  
Answer: (3, 2)  

Q3:  
Substitution means rearranging one equation to express a variable, then replacing that variable in the other equation so only one variable remains and can be solved.  
Solve: y = x + 1 → substitute y into 2x + y = 8  
2x + (x + 1) = 8 → 3x + 1 = 8 → 3x = 7 → x = 7/3  
Find y: y = 7/3 + 1 = 10/3  
Answer: (7/3, 10/3)  

Q4:  
Add equations to eliminate y:  
3x + 4y = 20  
5x - 4y = 8  
Adding gives: 8x = 28 → x = 3.5  
Substitute into first: 3(3.5) + 4y = 20 → 10.5 + 4y = 20 → 4y = 9.5 → y = 2.375  
Answer: (3.5, 2.375)  

</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  

<ul>  
<li>Simultaneous equations are sets of two or more equations with shared unknown variables.</li>  
<li>You can solve them by substitution or elimination methods.</li>  
<li>Substitution involves rearranging one equation and replacing variables into the other.</li>  
<li>Elimination aligns coefficients to remove one variable by adding or subtracting equations.</li>  
<li>Clear working and correct labelling of variables improve exam answers and help avoid errors.</li>  
<li>Always check solutions by substituting values back into original equations.</li>  
</ul>

23 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Simultaneous equations</strong> – two or more equations solved together with shared unknowns.</p>  
<p><strong>Substitution</strong> – replacing one variable with an expression from another equation.</p>  
<p><strong>Elimination</strong> – adding or subtracting equations to remove one variable.</p>  
<p><strong>Linear equation</strong> – an equation where variables have power 1 only.</p>  
<p><strong>Unknown variable</strong> – a letter representing a value we need to find.</p>  
<p><strong>Rearrange</strong> – change the equation to make one variable the subject.</p>  
<p><strong>Coefficient</strong> – the number multiplying a variable.</p>  
<p><strong>Solution</strong> – values of variables that satisfy all equations.</p>  
<p><strong>Consistent</strong> – system of equations that has at least one solution.</p>  
<p><strong>Solution pair</strong> – the ordered pair (x, y) that solves the equations.</p>
