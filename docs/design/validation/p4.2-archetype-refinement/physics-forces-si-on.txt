<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A force is a push or a pull that acts on an object and can change its shape, speed, or direction.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Understanding forces explains everyday motion — why cars speed up, why objects fall, and how balanced forces keep things moving steadily.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<p>👉 <strong>Forces acting on an object combine to form a resultant force; if the resultant force is zero, the object stays at steady speed or at rest, but if it’s not zero, the object accelerates according to the equation F = ma (force = mass × acceleration).</strong></p>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<ul>  
<li>A car accelerating: the engine exerts a forward force greater than the resistive forces.</li>  
<li>A book resting on a table: gravity pulls down but the table pushes up with an equal force (balanced forces).</li>  
<li>A parachutist falling: air resistance balances weight eventually, causing steady terminal velocity.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Forces</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let’s break down how forces act.</p>  
<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Forces can change an object’s speed or direction if the forces don’t balance out.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>If forces on an object are balanced (resultant force zero), it keeps moving at the same speed or stays still.</li>  
<li>If forces are unbalanced (a resultant force exists), the object accelerates or decelerates.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Force (N) applied → Unbalanced resultant force → Changes velocity → Means object accelerates</li>  
<li>Formula link: F = m × a (force equals mass times acceleration)</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, say what the forces are, say if they balance or not (resultant force = 0 or not), then explain whether the object speeds up, slows down, or stays steady — always link forces to motion.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say “a moving object must have a force pushing it all the time to keep moving.”</p>  
<p>👉 The correction is that an object moving at steady speed does <em>not</em> need a force to keep moving; balanced forces mean no change in velocity.</p>  
<p>👉 This matters because Newton’s first law says an object continues in its state of rest or uniform motion unless acted on by an unbalanced force.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Forces decide how things move in the real world, from vehicle safety designs to sports performance—knowing how to explain forces and resultant forces is essential for answering GCSE exam questions on motion.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When asked to explain motion in a question, always include if forces are balanced or unbalanced, and then link this clearly to acceleration or steady speed using the phrase “because” to join your reasoning.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The object moves faster.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The object moves faster because there is a force pushing it.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The object accelerates because the resultant force is unbalanced, causing a change in velocity according to F=ma.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly states acceleration, not just “moving faster.”</li>  
<li>It uses the term “resultant force” showing understanding of net forces.</li>  
<li>It links the physics formula F = ma to the explanation.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What unit is force measured in?</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> A 5 kg object experiences a 15 N total force. Calculate its acceleration.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Explain what happens to a car if the engine provides a force greater than resistive forces. Use the ideas of resultant force and acceleration in your answer.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A skydiver falls under gravity and air resistance. Describe and explain how forces change throughout the fall until terminal velocity is reached.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

<p><strong>Q1:</strong> Newtons (N).</p>  

<p><strong>Q2:</strong>  
Use a = F/m = 15 N ÷ 5 kg = 3 m/s².</p>  

<p><strong>Q3:</strong>  
The car accelerates because the engine force is greater than resistive forces, creating an unbalanced resultant force. This force causes the car to speed up according to F = ma.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4:</strong>  
At the start, weight pulls the skydiver down with a greater force than air resistance, causing acceleration. As speed increases, air resistance grows until it balances weight, resulting in a zero resultant force. At this point, the skydiver reaches terminal velocity and falls at steady speed.</p>  
</details>  

23 — SUMMARY  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<p>👉 Forces are pushes or pulls that cause changes in motion or shape.</p>  
<p>👉 When all forces on an object balance, it remains at steady speed or rest.</p>  
<p>👉 An unbalanced resultant force causes acceleration, described by F = ma.</p>  
<p>👉 Free-body diagrams help show forces clearly.</p>  
<p>👉 Understand and explain how forces affect motion to score well in exams.</p>  
<p>👉 Remember to always link forces to their effect on velocity in your answers.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<p><strong>Force</strong> – A push or pull on an object affecting its motion or shape.</p>  
<p><strong>Resultant force</strong> – The overall force after combining all forces on an object.</p>  
<p><strong>Acceleration</strong> – The rate at which an object changes its velocity.</p>  
<p><strong>Mass</strong> – The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms.</p>  
<p><strong>Newton (N)</strong> – The unit of force.</p>  
<p><strong>Balanced forces</strong> – Forces that cancel each other out, producing no change in motion.</p>  
<p><strong>Unbalanced forces</strong> – Forces that cause a change in motion or acceleration.</p>  
<p><strong>F = ma</strong> – Force equals mass times acceleration, the key formula linking force and motion.</p>  
<p><strong>Free-body diagram</strong> – A diagram showing all forces acting on an object using arrows.</p>  
<p><strong>Terminal velocity</strong> – The constant speed reached when air resistance balances weight during a fall.</p>
