3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A <strong>quadratic expression</strong> is a polynomial of degree 2, usually written as <em>ax² + bx + c</em> where a ≠ 0, and a <strong>quadratic equation</strong> sets this expression equal to zero, like <em>ax² + bx + c = 0</em>.</p>

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratic equations model many real-world problems, from projectile motion in physics to profit maximisation in business, and mastering them is key to success in GCSE maths exams.</p>

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 To solve a quadratic equation <em>ax² + bx + c = 0</em>, first identify the coefficients a, b, and c, then choose a method: factorisation if possible, completing the square, or use the quadratic formula <em>x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a</em>. Finally, interpret the roots (solutions) and the graph features like vertex and axis of symmetry.</p>

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>Factorisation: Solve <em>x² - 5x + 6 = 0</em> by finding two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5.</li>  
<li>Completing the square: Rewrite <em>x² + 6x + 5 = 0</em> as a perfect square trinomial and solve.</li>  
<li>Quadratic formula: Use on <em>2x² - 4x - 3 = 0</em> where factorisation is hard.</li>  
</ul>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up the signs for b in the quadratic formula.</p>  
<p>For example, students often forget to change the sign in \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\) and just write \(+b\), which leads to wrong answers.</p>  
<p>👉 Always remember the negative sign before b is part of the formula itself – it’s not "change b’s sign", it’s "minus b". This earns method marks and avoids careless slip mistakes.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Solving a quadratic using the quadratic formula  

Step 1:  
Identify coefficients a, b, and c from the equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\)  

↓  

Step 2:  
Calculate the discriminant \(D = b^2 - 4ac\)  

↓  

Step 3:  
Substitute a, b, and D into the quadratic formula: \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}\)  

↓  

Step 4:  
Calculate the two roots using plus and minus values, then simplify  

Exam link:  
This method works for any quadratic and helps find exact solutions for exam questions.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Knowing how to solve quadratic equations and interpret their graphs is essential for scoring high marks on the Higher tier. Your examiners look for clear methods, especially in showing all working using formulas and explaining your answers fully.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down the formula before substituting numbers, label your values as a, b, and c, and calculate the discriminant first. This shows working and can earn method marks even if the final answer is incorrect.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The quadratic formula gives the answers for the equation.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Use the quadratic formula by substituting a, b, and c into \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\) to find x.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>First identify coefficients a, b, and c from the equation. Calculate the discriminant \(b^2-4ac\). Substitute these into the quadratic formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\), compute both roots, and clearly state the exact solutions.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It shows clear method steps and formula use.</li>  
<li>Includes calculation and interpretation of the discriminant.</li>  
<li>States both roots explicitly for completeness.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam practice</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write down the coefficient a in the quadratic equation \(3x^2 - 2x + 5 = 0\).</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve \(x^2 - 9 = 0\) by factorising.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Use the quadratic formula to solve \(2x^2 + 3x - 2 = 0\). Show full working, including discriminant.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> The graph of \(y = x^2 + 6x + 11\) is a parabola. Find the vertex of the parabola by completing the square. Show all your working.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

<strong>Q1:</strong> a = 3.  

<strong>Q2:</strong> Factorise: \(x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3) = 0\). So roots are \(x = 3\), \(x = -3\).  

<strong>Q3:</strong>  
1. Identify a=2, b=3, c=-2.  
2. Discriminant = \(3^2 - 4 \times 2 \times (-2) = 9 + 16 = 25\).  
3. Apply formula: \(x = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2 \times 2} = \frac{-3 \pm 5}{4}\).  
4. Roots: \(x = \frac{-3 + 5}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5\) or \(x = \frac{-3 - 5}{4} = \frac{-8}{4} = -2\).  

<strong>Q4:</strong>  
1. Rewrite \(x^2 + 6x + 11\) completing the square:  
\(x^2 + 6x + 9 + 2 = (x + 3)^2 + 2\).  
2. Vertex is at \((-3, 2)\).  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Quadratic</strong> – An expression or equation where the highest power of x is 2 (ax² + bx + c).  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The numerical factor multiplying a variable (e.g., a, b, c in a quadratic).  
<strong>Factorise</strong> – To write an expression as a product of its factors.  
<strong>Roots</strong> – The solutions to a quadratic equation where it equals zero.  
<strong>Discriminant</strong> – The part under the square root in the quadratic formula, \(b^2 - 4ac\), indicating number of roots.  
<strong>Vertex</strong> – The turning point of the parabola represented by the quadratic graph.  
<strong>Axis of symmetry</strong> – The vertical line passing through the vertex dividing the parabola into mirror images.  
<strong>Quadratic formula</strong> – \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), a formula to find roots.  
<strong>Complete the square</strong> – A method rewriting quadratics as a perfect square plus constant.  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find all solutions or roots of the quadratic equation.
