9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Causes of WW1</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that WW1 was caused not by one single event, but a combination of long-term tensions, short-term crises, and a triggering incident.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Long-term causes built slowly over decades, creating rivalries and suspicion between countries.</li>  
<li>Short-term causes increased tension and hostility just before 1914, making war more likely.</li>  
<li>The assassination in June 1914 triggered existing tensions into an unstoppable chain of war declarations.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Militarism (building armies and navies) → caused an arms race → lead to fear and readiness for war.</li>  
<li>Alliances (like the Triple Entente and Triple Alliance) → created two opposing blocs → increased risk of larger war spreading.</li>  
<li>Imperialism → competition for colonies → caused rivalries and conflicts overseas.</li>  
<li>Nationalism → pride and desire for independence → increased tension in multi-ethnic empires like Austria-Hungary.</li>  
<li>Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand → Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia → war declarations followed in a domino effect.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering “Explain why WW1 broke out” questions, follow cause → mechanism → effect chains clearly and use specific examples (e.g., assassination in Sarajevo, alliances between countries).</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Exam questions on WW1 causes often ask 'Explain why…' or 'How far do you agree…'. Understanding the difference between long-term and short-term causes, plus the trigger, helps you structure clear, balanced answers.</strong></p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always start your answer by naming a cause, then explain <em>why</em> it caused tension or conflict, and follow with the effect it had on making war more likely. Use precise terminology and specific examples for top marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 started because of the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 started because Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated, which made Austria-Hungary angry and led to war.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>WW1 broke out because the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in June 1914 triggered Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia. This act activated alliances, causing a chain reaction of countries declaring war due to military commitments, all within a context of long-term tensions like militarism and nationalism that made conflict more likely.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly links cause → mechanism → effect.</li>  
<li>Uses specific event names and dates.</li>  
<li>Explains context, showing depth and balance.</li>  
</ul>

Answer:

<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

2. This caused an arms race, especially between Britain and Germany, increasing fear and competition (mechanism).  
3. As a result, countries were ready for war and suspicious of each other, making conflict more likely to start (effect).

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  
<p>Try these exam questions:</p>  
<ol>  
<li><strong>(1 mark)</strong> Name the event that triggered the outbreak of WW1.</li>  
<li><strong>(2 marks)</strong> Give two long-term causes of WW1.</li>  
<li><strong>(3 marks)</strong> Explain how alliances contributed to the start of WW1.</li>  
<li><strong>(4 marks)</strong> How far do you agree that militarism was the main cause of WW1? Use evidence to support your answer.</li>  
</ol>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model answers for Q3 and Q4</summary>  

<strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Alliances caused countries to promise to defend each other. This meant that when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia, Russia defended Serbia. Because Russia was involved, Germany declared war on Russia, and then France and Britain became involved due to their alliances. Therefore, alliances turned a small conflict into a full-scale war.  

<strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Militarism was a main cause of WW1 because European powers were building huge armies and navies, leading to an arms race that made war more likely. However, other factors like alliances and nationalism were also important because they created tension and rivalries. Overall, while militarism increasingly prepared countries for war, it worked alongside other causes to trigger WW1.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Militarism</strong> – The policy of building up strong armed forces to prepare for war.  
<strong>Alliance</strong> – An agreement between countries to support each other in case of war.  
<strong>Imperialism</strong> – The competition to build empires by taking control of territories.  
<strong>Nationalism</strong> – Pride in one’s country or a desire for independence.  
<strong>Trigger</strong> – The specific event that directly causes a larger event to happen.  
<strong>Assassination</strong> – The murder of a prominent person, often for political reasons.  
<strong>Arms race</strong> – A competition between countries to have the most powerful weapons.  
<strong>Balkan Crisis</strong> – Tensions and conflicts in Southeast Europe contributing to WW1.  
<strong>Long-term cause</strong> – A cause that builds over a long period before an event.  
<strong>Short-term cause</strong> – A cause close in time to an event that helps make it happen.
