9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Algebra</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let’s break down solving linear equations step-by-step using inverse operations.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>Solving an equation means finding the value of the variable that makes the equation true.</p>  

<h3><strong>Method → reason:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Start by ‘undoing’ the operations around the variable using inverse operations: if it’s adding, subtract; if multiplying, divide.</li>  
<li>Apply the same operation to both sides of the equation to keep it balanced.</li>  
<li>Repeat until the variable stands alone (is isolated).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Step → result:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Remember to simplify each side as you go—combine like terms and perform arithmetic.</li>  
<li>Once isolated, the number you have is the solution.</li>  
<li>Finally, substitute the solution back into the original equation to check.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Common error → correction:</strong></h3>  
<p>A common mistake is forgetting to change the sign when moving terms across the equals sign.</p>  
<p>Always apply inverse operations carefully to both sides.</p>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Show every step clearly, state the operation explicitly (e.g., "subtract 4 from both sides"), and write the final answer with units or context if needed.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake: Sign errors when rearranging</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Students often forget to change signs correctly when moving terms from one side of an equation to the other.</p>  
<p>Instead of subtracting 5 from one side, they might add it incorrectly, leading to wrong answers.</p>  
<p>Always use inverse operations carefully and check your signs at each step to avoid losing marks.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Solving a linear equation in four steps

Step 1: Identify the operation next to the variable (e.g., addition 4 in 3x + 4)  
↓  
Step 2: Apply the inverse operation to both sides (subtract 4 both sides)  
↓  
Step 3: Simplify both sides (3x = 15)  
↓  
Step 4: Divide both sides by coefficient of x (divide by 3: x = 5)  

Exam link:  
This exact method is needed for all linear equation questions on the GCSE exam.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Accurate step-by-step algebra is essential to earn method marks in GCSE exams because examiners look for clear logical working.</p>  
<p>Without showing each step, you risk losing marks even if your answer is right.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>Always write out each operation you perform on both sides explicitly (e.g., "subtract 7 from both sides") and line up your working neatly.</p>  
<p>Include substitution at the end to confirm your solution is correct.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I get x = 5 by guessing the answer.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I subtract 4 from both sides and then divide by 3 to find x = 5.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I subtract 4 from both sides of the equation 3x + 4 = 19 to get 3x = 15, then divide both sides by 3 to isolate x, giving x = 5. Finally, I check by substituting x = 5 back into the original equation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It shows each inverse operation step clearly.</li>  
<li>It uses precise maths language and notation.</li>  
<li>It demonstrates checking, confirming the solution’s validity.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Calculate the value of x in the equation 4x - 5 = 15.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Add 5 to both sides: 4x - 5 + 5 = 15 + 5  
Step 2: Simplify both sides: 4x = 20  
Step 3: Divide both sides by 4: 4x ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4  
Step 4: Simplify: x = 5  
Step 5: Check by substitution: 4 × 5 - 5 = 20 - 5 = 15 ✓  

</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
A student starts solving 3x + 6 = 18 by dividing both sides by 3 without subtracting 6 first. Why is this incorrect?

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Dividing first does not undo the addition of 6, so the variable is not isolated properly and the solution will be wrong. The correct first step is to subtract 6 from both sides, then divide by 3.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Solve x - 4 = 10.</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Solve 2x + 3 = 11. Show your working.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Solve 5(x - 2) = 20 and check your answer by substitution.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Rearrange the formula A = 3b + 7 to find b when A = 25, then calculate b.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

<strong>Q1:</strong>  
x - 4 = 10 → add 4 both sides → x = 14  

<strong>Q2:</strong>  
2x + 3 = 11  
Subtract 3 both sides → 2x = 8  
Divide both sides by 2 → x = 4  

<strong>Q3:</strong>  
5(x - 2) = 20  
Divide both sides by 5 → x - 2 = 4  
Add 2 both sides → x = 6  
Check: 5(6 - 2) = 5 × 4 = 20 ✓  

<strong>Q4:</strong>  
A = 3b + 7, A=25  
Subtract 7 both sides: 25 - 7 = 3b → 18 = 3b  
Divide both sides by 3 → b = 6  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Equation</strong> – A statement showing two expressions are equal.  
<strong>Variable</strong> – A letter representing an unknown number.  
<strong>Inverse operations</strong> – Operations that undo each other (addition ↔ subtraction, multiplication ↔ division).  
<strong>Isolate</strong> – To get the variable alone on one side of an equation.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To combine like terms and perform arithmetic.  
<strong>Substitute</strong> – To replace the variable with a number to check the solution.  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find the value of the variable that makes an equation true.  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – The number multiplied by a variable (e.g., 3 in 3x).  
<strong>Balance</strong> – The equality of both sides; maintain by doing same operations to both.  
<strong>Check</strong> – Verifying a solution by putting it back into the original equation.
