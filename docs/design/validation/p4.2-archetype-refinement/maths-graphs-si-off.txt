3 — DEFINITION

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A graph is a visual representation that shows the relationship between two variables using points, lines, or curves on a coordinate plane.</strong></p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Graphs help us understand patterns and relationships between variables quickly, which is vital in real-life problems and exams alike.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>Graphs use a coordinate system with horizontal (x) and vertical (y) axes to show how one variable changes with another.</strong></p>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key features include points plotted at (x, y), lines or curves connecting points, intercepts where graphs cross axes, gradients showing steepness, and shapes that indicate types of relationships.</strong></p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Graphs</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is understanding what graphs show and how to interpret their features.</p>  

<h3><strong>What graphs show:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The relationship between two variables, e.g., time vs temperature, distance vs speed.</li>  
<li>How one variable changes when the other changes.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Reading graphs:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Identify the axes labels and scales—what variables do they show?</li>  
<li>Find coordinates of points by reading values from axes.</li>  
<li>Look for intercepts where the graph crosses x or y axis to understand starting values.</li>  
<li>Check steepness or curvature to understand change rates.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Types of graph shapes and what they mean:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Straight line means a constant rate of change.</li>  
<li>A curve bending upwards shows increasing rate of change; downwards, decreasing.</li>  
<li>A horizontal line means no change in one variable.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When describing graphs, say: "At x=__, y=__, showing that..." Avoid vague statements like "the graph goes up" without specifying what that means in context.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is confusing the gradient with the y-intercept.</p>  
<p>👉 Remember, the gradient shows how steep the line is (rate of change), while the y-intercept tells you where the line crosses the y-axis.</p>  
<p>👉 Getting these mixed up loses marks because they represent different features and have different meanings in exam questions.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Calculating the gradient of a straight-line graph

Step 1:  
Identify two points on the line, for example (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).

↓

Step 2:  
Calculate the rise: y₂ − y₁.

↓

Step 3:  
Calculate the run: x₂ − x₁.

↓

Step 4:  
Divide rise by run: gradient = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).

Exam link:  
This calculation tells you the rate of change and is often required in exam graph questions.

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Graph questions are common on the Higher Tier paper, and examiners look for precise use of terms and clear explanations of what a graph shows.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In the exam, always link your graph description to what the variables mean and use numbers and terms like "gradient," "intercept," and "coordinate." Don’t just describe the shape.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The graph goes up then down.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The graph goes up to a maximum point then goes down because the values increase then decrease.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The graph rises from (0,2) to a maximum at (4,8), showing an increasing rate of change, then decreases after (4,8), indicated by the negative gradient after this point.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses clear coordinates and terminology.</li>  
<li>Explains the shape with cause → effect language.</li>  
<li>Links terminology (gradient, max point) to the graph’s behavior.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>

<p>Q1. (1 mark) What do the axes on a graph represent? </p>

<p>Q2. (2 marks) Calculate the gradient of a straight line between points (2,5) and (5,11).</p>

<p>Q3. (3 marks) Describe what a straight-line graph with a negative gradient shows about the relationship between two variables.</p>

<p>Q4. (4 marks) A graph shows distance (y) against time (x). Explain how to find the speed and what it means if the graph flattens out.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

Q1 Answer:  
The axes represent the two variables, with the horizontal (x) axis showing one variable and the vertical (y) axis showing the other.  

Q2 Answer:  
Gradient = (11−5) / (5−2) = 6 / 3 = 2.  

Q3 Answer:  
A negative gradient means as the x-variable increases, the y-variable decreases. This shows an inverse relationship where one value falls while the other rises.  

Q4 Answer:  
Speed is found from the gradient of the graph (change in distance ÷ change in time). If the graph flattens out, the speed is zero because distance does not change over time.  

</details>  

23 — SUMMARY

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Graphs visually show relationships between two variables using points, lines, or curves.</p>  
<p>👉 Key features to look for: coordinates, gradient, intercepts, and graph shape.</p>  
<p>👉 The gradient tells us the rate of change—positive for increasing, negative for decreasing.</p>  
<p>👉 Intercepts indicate where the graph crosses axes and can show starting or zero values.</p>  
<p>👉 Use precise language linking numbers and graph features to explain patterns in exam answers.</p>  
<p><strong>Remember:</strong> Always describe how variables relate and back up your statements with evidence from the graph.</p>  
<p>In exams, focus on cause → effect explanations when describing graph features for full marks.</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Coordinate</strong> – A pair of numbers (x, y) showing a point’s position on a graph.  
<strong>Gradient</strong> – The steepness of a line, calculated as rise over run.  
<strong>Intercept</strong> – Where a graph crosses either the x-axis or y-axis.  
<strong>Axes</strong> – The horizontal (x) and vertical (y) lines that form the coordinate plane.  
<strong>Linear</strong> – A graph that forms a straight line.  
<strong>Quadratic</strong> – A graph that forms a curve shaped like a 'U' or an inverted 'U'.  
<strong>Exponential</strong> – A graph where values increase or decrease faster and faster.  
<strong>Plot</strong> – To mark points on the graph using coordinates.  
<strong>Curve</strong> – A line on a graph that is not straight but bends.  
<strong>Rate of change</strong> – How quickly one variable changes in relation to another.
