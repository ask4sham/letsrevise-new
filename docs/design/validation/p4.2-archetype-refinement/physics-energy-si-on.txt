4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Understanding energy organisation and transfer helps us explain how devices work and how to reduce wasted energy, which saves resources and money in real life.</strong></p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<p>👉 <strong>The core GCSE model is: Energy stores → Energy transfer pathway(s) → Useful and wasted energy outputs.</strong></p>  
<p>This means energy is kept in a store (like kinetic or thermal), moves through a pathway (like heating, radiation, or work done by forces), and ends up either doing something useful or being wasted as less useful forms, usually heat.</p>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<ul>  
<li>Heating a pan of water on a stove: chemical energy → thermal energy via heating.</li>  
<li>A moving car slowing down: kinetic energy → thermal energy due to friction.</li>  
<li>An electric kettle: electrical energy → thermal energy to heat water.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Energy</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is how energy moves and changes form in systems, and why some energy is always wasted.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy is stored in particular ways, for example as kinetic energy if an object is moving.</li>  
<li>Energy transfers happen when energy leaves one store and goes into another, like when electrical energy heats a filament.</li>  
<li>Some energy becomes useful, like light from a bulb, and some becomes wasted, often as heat to the surroundings.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>An energy store holds energy—for example, chemical energy is stored in fuel molecules.</li>  
<li>Energy transfer mechanisms include heating, radiation, electrical work, or mechanical work (forces causing movement).</li>  
<li>Useful outputs achieve the system’s purpose, wasted outputs are lower quality energy usually heating the surroundings.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Use clear cause → effect chains: state the energy store, explain the transfer method (cause), say what useful and wasted outputs are (effect).</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say "energy is used up" or "energy disappears."</p>  
<p>Energy can never be used up or lost; it is always conserved and transferred from one store to another.</p>  
<p>Students should say energy is transferred, not used up, to get marks for conservation principles.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)  

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Knowing how energy is organised and transferred lets you explain everyday devices and systems clearly and accurately, which is often asked in exams.</p>  
<p>For example, explaining why energy is wasted and how to reduce that waste connects theory to real life.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exam answers, always explain cause and effect clearly. For example, don’t just say "energy changes"; say "energy transfers by heating because of friction, which increases thermal energy in the surroundings."</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy transfers from one store to another.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy transfers from one store to another because of heating or forces.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Energy in the kinetic store transfers by heating due to friction, causing thermal energy to increase in the surroundings, which is wasted energy.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the cause (friction) and effect (thermal energy increase).</li>  
<li>It uses correct terminology for stores and transfers.</li>  
<li>It shows understanding of wasted energy.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)  

Question:  
Evaluate the efficiency of a machine that inputs 200 J of energy and does 150 J of useful work but loses the rest as heat. Calculate efficiency and explain the result.  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Write the efficiency formula: Efficiency = (Useful energy output / Total energy input) × 100%  
2. Substitute values: (150 J / 200 J) × 100% = 0.75 × 100% = 75%  
3. This means 75% of the input energy is usefully used by the machine, and 25% is wasted as heat.  
4. The efficiency is less than 100% because some energy always transfers as thermal energy due to friction or other losses.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> State two forms of energy stores.</p>  
<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Describe the energy transfers in a moving car coming to a stop due to friction.</p>  
<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Calculate the efficiency of a motor that inputs 500 J of electrical energy and does 400 J of useful work, and explain why some energy is wasted.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal model answers for Q3 and Q4</summary>  

Q3: The car’s kinetic energy store decreases as it slows down. Energy transfers due to friction between the tyres and road into thermal energy stores in the car and surroundings, causing wasted heat.  

Q4: Efficiency = (400 J / 500 J) × 100% = 80%. Some energy is wasted because of friction and heat loss, so not all electrical energy becomes useful work.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<strong>Energy</strong> – The ability to do work or cause change.  
<strong>Energy store</strong> – Where energy is kept in a system (e.g., kinetic, chemical).  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – The movement of energy from one store to another.  
<strong>Useful energy</strong> – Energy transferred into the desired form.  
<strong>Wasted energy</strong> – Energy transferred to unwanted stores, often as heat.  
<strong>Efficiency</strong> – A measure of how much input energy is usefully transferred.  
<strong>Sankey diagram</strong> – A visual representation of energy transfers and waste.  
<strong>Conservation of energy</strong> – Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred.  
<strong>Heating</strong> – Energy transfer caused by temperature difference.  
<strong>Work done</strong> – Energy transfer caused by a force moving an object.
