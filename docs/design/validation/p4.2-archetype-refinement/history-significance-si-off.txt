3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The Holocaust was the systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators during World War II.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The Holocaust is significant because it shows the consequences of racism, totalitarianism, and hatred taken to an extreme, and it shaped modern ideas about human rights and genocide prevention.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 The core model to understand the Holocaust is:  
Cause (Nazi ideology + anti-Semitism + war conditions) → Event (mass persecution, ghettos, deportations, concentration and extermination camps) → Consequence (mass murder, trauma, post-war justice, memory and education).</p>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>The establishment and role of concentration camps like Auschwitz and Treblinka.</li>  
<li>The use of ghettos in cities such as Warsaw to isolate Jewish populations.</li>  
<li>The Wannsee Conference where plans for the "Final Solution" were formalised.</li>  
<li>Resistance efforts like the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising.</li>  
<li>Post-war consequences such as the Nuremberg Trials.</li>  
</ul>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying the Holocaust only affected Jewish people. </p>  
<p>In fact, other groups like Roma (Gypsies), disabled people, Poles, and others were also persecuted and murdered.</p>  
<p>This correction earns marks by showing a fuller understanding of the Holocaust’s scope beyond just one target group.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The Holocaust matters because it reveals how dangerous hatred and totalitarian power can be, leading to human rights violations on a massive scale. This knowledge helps prevent such acts in the future.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When answering why the Holocaust is significant, explain both short-term effects (e.g., millions murdered) and long-term effects (e.g., global human rights laws, Holocaust education). Always link cause → effect.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was bad because many people died.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was bad because the Nazis murdered six million Jews, causing huge loss of life.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Holocaust was historically significant because Nazi ideology and war conditions led to the organised genocide of six million Jews, which caused enormous human suffering and led to worldwide changes in human rights laws and how we remember such atrocities.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses precise terminology like "genocide".</li>  
<li>Clearly links causes (Nazi ideology + war) to consequences (mass murder + legacy).</li>  
<li>Shows understanding beyond description to impact.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Genocide</strong> – The deliberate killing of a large group of people, especially those of a particular ethnic group.</p>  
<p><strong>Anti-Semitism</strong> – Hostility or discrimination against Jewish people.</p>  
<p><strong>Final Solution</strong> – Nazi plan to systematically kill all Jews.</p>  
<p><strong>Concentration Camp</strong> – Detention centres where large numbers of people were imprisoned and killed.</p>  
<p><strong>Ghetto</strong> – Sections of cities where Jewish people were forced to live in terrible conditions.</p>  
<p><strong>Nazi Ideology</strong> – Beliefs held by Nazis including racial superiority and anti-Semitism.</p>  
<p><strong>Persecution</strong> – Harassment or ill-treatment because of race, religion, or political beliefs.</p>  
<p><strong>Resistance</strong> – Efforts by individuals or groups to fight back against Nazi oppression.</p>  
<p><strong>Liberation</strong> – The freeing of prisoners from camps at the end of WWII.</p>  
<p><strong>Nuremberg Trials</strong> – Post-war trials held to prosecute Nazi leaders for war crimes.</p>
