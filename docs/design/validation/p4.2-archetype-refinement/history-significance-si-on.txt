3 — DEFINITION

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Significance</strong> means how important or influential an event is judged to be based on its impact at the time it happened, its effects over the long term, and its lasting meaning for people today.</p>  

<p>👉 The <strong>significance of the Holocaust</strong> is about understanding why this event is seen as one of the most important and tragic in world history.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<p>👉 Understanding the significance of the Holocaust matters because it helps us learn how such horrors happened, the consequences for millions of people, and why remembering it is vital to prevent future genocides.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 To explain the significance of any event like the Holocaust, use this chain:</p>  

<ol>  
<li><strong>Importance at the time:</strong> What happened immediately and how people then responded or were affected.</li>  
<li><strong>Importance later:</strong> How historians and societies have remembered or been influenced by the event later on.</li>  
<li><strong>Overall significance:</strong> Judging how deeply and widely the event matters by considering its scale, duration, and legacy.</li>  
</ol>

<p>👉 This model lets you organise answers clearly and add depth to exam responses.</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Significance of the Holocaust</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 The key idea is to understand significance as a process of judging how much an event matters, both when it happens and in the future.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happened at the time?</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Holocaust caused horrific loss of life—over six million Jewish people murdered by the Nazis alongside millions of others (Roma, disabled people, political opponents).</li>  
<li>It shocked the world politically and socially, leading to urgent action such as the Nuremberg Trials and the creation of Israel in 1948.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Why it matters later</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Holocaust shaped global memory and law, influencing human rights policies.</li>  
<li>It serves as a warning against hatred and genocide, taught in schools worldwide.</li>  
<li>Memorials and museums help societies remember and reflect on the event’s lessons.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Overall significance judgement</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Holocaust is considered highly significant because of the number of victims, the extreme cruelty involved, and its lasting effects on law, society, and culture.</li>  
<li>Its influence is ongoing, seen in anti-genocide laws and international efforts to prevent similar crimes.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always link your points with <em>why</em> the event mattered then or later, and finish with a judgement on its overall importance.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It clearly explains cause → effect → outcome.</li>

</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why the Holocaust was significant at the time it occurred.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

1. The Holocaust was significant at the time because it led to the mass murder of six million Jews and millions of others between 1941 and 1945.  
2. This caused widespread shock as the world learned about the extreme cruelty and scale of Nazi policies.  
3. As a result, there were immediate consequences, such as the Allied forces liberating concentration camps and holding the Nuremberg Trials from 1945 to 1946 to punish Nazi leaders.  
4. Therefore, the Holocaust’s importance at the time lies in both its scale and the urgent political and social response it forced worldwide.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Significance</strong> – How important or meaningful an event is judged to be.  
<strong>Holocaust</strong> – The genocide of six million Jews and millions of others by the Nazis during WWII.  
<strong>Legacy</strong> – What remains or continues after an event has ended.  
<strong>Impact</strong> – The effects or changes caused by an event.  
<strong>Immediate</strong> – Happening straight away at the time.  
<strong>Long-term</strong> – Happening or continuing well after the event.  
<strong>Genocide</strong> – The deliberate killing of a large group, especially a particular ethnic group.  
<strong>Remembrance</strong> – The act of remembering and honouring the past.  
<strong>Nuremberg Trials</strong> – War crime trials of Nazi leaders after WWII.  
<strong>Human rights</strong> – Basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person, often protected by law.
