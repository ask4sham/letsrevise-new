9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Treaty of Versailles</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is to understand the Treaty as the official end of WWI, but also as a source of conflict that shaped the 20th century.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Treaty was negotiated mainly by the Allied powers: Britain, France, the USA (though the US rejected it later).</li>  
<li>Because the Allies had won, they wanted to punish Germany and make it weaker to prevent future wars.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Treaty included territorial losses for Germany → the country lost valuable land and resources → weakening Germany’s economy.</li>  
<li>Reparations demanded → Germany had to pay massive sums → led to economic hardship and hyperinflation.</li>  
<li>The War Guilt Clause → Germany must accept blame → caused humiliation and anger among Germans.</li>  
<li>Demilitarisation → limits on German army and no weapons in Rhineland → reduced Germany’s military power.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When answering, explain the cause → process → effect chain, such as: “Because the Treaty forced Germany to accept blame, this caused anger and political instability which helped extremist groups grow.”</p>

<p>👉 A common mistake is to say “The Treaty of Versailles started World War Two.”</p>  
<p>👉 Correct this by saying the Treaty created conditions, like German anger and economic problems, that helped cause WWII, but it did not directly start the war.</p>  
<p>👉 This correction earns marks because it shows understanding of cause and consequence rather than incorrect fact.</p>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<strong>Reparations</strong> – Money Germany had to pay after WWI to repair damage.  
<strong>War Guilt Clause</strong> – The part of the treaty that made Germany accept blame for WWI.  
<strong>Demilitarisation</strong> – Reducing or banning weapons and a military presence in a specific area.  
<strong>Territorial losses</strong> – Areas of land Germany had to give up after WWI.  
<strong>League of Nations</strong> – An international group set up after WWI to maintain peace.  
<strong>Self-determination</strong> – The idea countries should choose their own government.  
<strong>Hyperinflation</strong> – Extremely rapid inflation, which damaged Germany’s economy after WWI.  
<strong>Armistice</strong> – A ceasefire agreement that ended fighting in WWI before the Treaty.  
<strong>Diplomacy</strong> – Managing international relations and negotiations.  
<strong>Diktat</strong> – A dictated peace treaty imposed on a defeated country without negotiation.
