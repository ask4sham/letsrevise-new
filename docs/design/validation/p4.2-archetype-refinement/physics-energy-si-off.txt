9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Energy</strong></h2>  

<p><strong>👉 The key idea is</strong> energy can exist in different stores and moves from one store to another without being lost.</p>

<h3><strong>How energy stores work</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy stored in an object depends on its state — for example, a moving object stores kinetic energy due to its motion.</li>  
<li>Changing position or shape can store energy as gravitational potential or elastic potential energy.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Energy transfer process → effect</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>When energy transfers from one store to another, the total quantity remains constant — no energy is lost, just transformed.</li>  
<li>For example, when a moving object hits a wall, kinetic energy transfers to sound, heat, and deformation energy in the wall.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Understanding conservation of energy</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or stored.</li>  
<li>This principle allows us to predict how much energy will be in different stores after events happen.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When explaining energy changes, always describe which energy store loses energy and which one gains it — connect cause and effect clearly for marks.</p>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
A ball of mass 2 kg is held 5 m above the ground. Calculate the gravitational potential energy stored. (Use g = 10 N/kg)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

GPE = mass × gravitational field strength × height  
GPE = 2 kg × 10 N/kg × 5 m  
GPE = 100 J  

So, the ball stores 100 joules of gravitational potential energy.  

</details>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
Explain why a moving car’s energy is mainly in the kinetic store, but after applying brakes, the car heats up.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

When the car is moving, it has kinetic energy due to its speed. Applying the brakes transfers kinetic energy into thermal energy because the brakes heat up due to friction. So, the kinetic energy store decreases while thermal energy increases, consistent with energy conservation.  

</details>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A battery transfers stored chemical energy to electrical energy in a circuit. Explain how energy conservation applies here.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A cyclist pedals uphill, gaining gravitational potential energy. Explain fully how energy is transferred during this process, including the role of muscles and friction.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1:  
<strong>Answer:</strong> Kinetic energy or gravitational potential energy or thermal energy.

Q2:  
<strong>Answer:</strong> The object loses gravitational potential energy as it falls. This energy is transferred into kinetic energy as it speeds up.

Q3:  
<strong>Answer:</strong> The chemical energy stored in the battery is transferred to electrical energy in the circuit. According to energy conservation, no energy is lost; it is just moved from one form to another.

Q4:  
<strong>Answer:</strong> The cyclist’s muscles transfer chemical energy into kinetic energy to pedal the bike. As the cyclist goes uphill, kinetic energy is transferred into gravitational potential energy. Some energy is also lost as thermal energy due to friction between the bike and road, but the total energy is conserved, just transferred between stores.  

</details>

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<ul>  
<li>Energy is the ability to do work or cause change.</li>  
<li>Energy can be stored in several ways: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, and more.</li>  
<li>Energy transfers between different stores but the total energy is always conserved.</li>  
<li>Understanding energy stores and transfers helps explain everyday processes and exam questions.</li>  
<li>Always use precise terminology like “energy transferred” or “energy stored,” never “used up.”</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Energy</strong> – The ability to do work or cause change.  
<strong>Energy store</strong> – Where energy is kept in an object, like kinetic or potential energy.  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – The movement of energy from one store to another.  
<strong>Conservation of energy</strong> – The principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred.  
<strong>Kinetic energy</strong> – Energy of movement.  
<strong>Gravitational potential energy</strong> – Energy stored due to an object’s height above the ground.  
<strong>Elastic potential energy</strong> – Energy stored in stretched or compressed objects.  
<strong>Thermal energy</strong> – Energy related to temperature and heat.  
<strong>Work done</strong> – The energy transferred when a force moves an object.  
<strong>Efficiency</strong> – How well energy is transferred usefully without waste.
