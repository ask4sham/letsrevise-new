3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>A wave is a way of transferring energy from one place to another without the transfer of matter.</strong></p>  
<p>This means the particles or medium through which the wave travels don’t move along with the wave but vibrate around their fixed positions.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Why it matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Understanding waves helps explain everyday phenomena like sound, light, and even communication signals, allowing us to use and control energy efficiently.</strong></p>  
<p>This knowledge is essential for technologies like radios, medical imaging, and understanding natural events such as earthquakes.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>Core GCSE wave model: A wave is produced by a source → it transfers energy through a medium (for mechanical waves) or space (for electromagnetic waves) → waves have properties such as amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and speed → they interact with boundaries by reflection, refraction or absorption → detectors or observers receive the energy.</strong></p>  
<p>This outlines the flow: Source → Medium → Wave properties → Interaction with medium or boundary → Detection and measurement.</p>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Waves</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Key idea: Waves transfer energy by making particles in a medium vibrate without the particles moving with the wave.</strong></p>  

<h3><strong>What happens physically:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Particles in the medium oscillate about fixed points.</li>  
<li>This vibration passes energy to neighboring particles, creating the wave motion.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Mechanical waves (like sound and water waves) need a medium, because particles push and pull on each other to transfer energy.</li>  
<li>Electromagnetic waves (like light) do not need a medium and can travel through a vacuum.</li>  
<li>Transverse waves vibrate particles perpendicular to wave direction (e.g., light, water ripples).</li>  
<li>Longitudinal waves vibrate particles parallel to the wave direction (e.g., sound).</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When asked to explain, use cause → effect chains like: The source causes vibrations because... These vibrations transfer energy by... Therefore, the wave moves energy without matter moving.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying waves carry matter from one place to another. This is incorrect because the particles of the medium only vibrate around fixed positions and do not travel with the wave.</p>  
<p>A better way to say it is waves transfer energy through the vibrations of particles without transferring matter.</p>  
<p>This correction earns marks because it clearly distinguishes between energy transfer and matter movement, which is a key physics concept.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the detailed properties of waves and how to explain energy transfer is vital because many exam questions ask for explanations involving cause and effect, not just definitions.</p>  
<p>Linking wave behaviour to energy transfer shows deeper understanding and can earn higher-level marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When you explain wave questions, always include cause → effect reasoning using scientific terms. Start with the source causing vibrations, explain particle motion, energy transfer, and end with the effect on detectors or observers.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Waves carry energy.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Waves carry energy because particles vibrate and pass on the energy.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Waves transfer energy because the source causes particles in the medium to vibrate, and these vibrations pass energy to neighbouring particles without the particles moving along the wave, allowing energy to travel through the medium to a detector.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It includes clear cause → effect steps.</li>  
<li>It uses precise scientific terms like particles, vibrations, medium, and detector.</li>  
<li>It explains how energy moves, not just stating that it does.</li>  
</ul>

21 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

Q1 (1 mark):  
Define a wave in GCSE Physics.

Q2 (2 marks):  
Describe the difference between transverse and longitudinal waves.

Q3 (3 marks):  
Explain how a mechanical wave transfers energy through a medium without transferring matter.

Q4 (4 marks):  
A sound wave travels through air with a frequency of 500 Hz and a speed of 340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength. Then explain why the wave does not carry the air particles with it.

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1:  
A wave is a way of transferring energy without transferring matter.  

Q2:  
Transverse waves have particles vibrating perpendicular to the direction of wave travel, whereas longitudinal waves have particles vibrating parallel to the wave direction.  

Q3:  
A mechanical wave transfers energy because the vibrating source makes particles in the medium vibrate. These vibrations pass energy between particles without the particles moving along the wave. This means energy moves, but matter does not.  

Q4:  
Wavelength = wave speed ÷ frequency = 340 m/s ÷ 500 Hz = 0.68 m.  
The wave does not carry air particles because the particles only vibrate around fixed positions; they pass energy by vibration instead of moving forward.  
</details>

23 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Wave</strong> – Transfer of energy without matter moving.  
<strong>Amplitude</strong> – Maximum height of a wave from its rest position.  
<strong>Wavelength</strong> – Distance between two consecutive crests or compressions.  
<strong>Frequency</strong> – Number of waves passing a point each second (Hz).  
<strong>Wave speed</strong> – How fast the wave travels through a medium (m/s).  
<strong>Medium</strong> – Material a wave travels through (air, water, etc.).  
<strong>Transverse wave</strong> – Wave with particle vibration perpendicular to wave direction.  
<strong>Longitudinal wave</strong> – Wave with particle vibration parallel to wave direction.  
<strong>Reflection</strong> – When a wave bounces off a surface.  
<strong>Energy transfer</strong> – Movement of energy from one place to another by waves.
