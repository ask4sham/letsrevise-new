9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Trigonometry</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is: To solve right-angled triangle problems using trigonometric ratios, you must first correctly label the triangle sides relative to the angle of interest.</p>  

<h3><strong>Method steps:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Label the sides of the triangle with respect to the angle you’re working on: <strong>opposite</strong>, <strong>adjacent</strong>, and <strong>hypotenuse</strong>.</li>  
<li>Choose the correct ratio:<br />  
sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse<br />  
cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse<br />  
tan θ = opposite/adjacent  
</li>  
<li>Write the formula you will use based on the information given.</li>  
<li>If you’re finding a side, rearrange the formula to get the unknown side on its own.</li>  
<li>Substitute the values and calculate using a calculator in degrees mode.</li>  
<li>Always check your answer is sensible in size.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Write down the formula you use with correct labels, show the algebra step to isolate the unknown, substitute clearly, and state the final answer with units and appropriate accuracy.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up the sides, especially confusing the adjacent and opposite sides relative to the angle given.</p>  
<p>👉 This happens when the triangle isn’t labelled carefully with respect to the angle you’re working on.</p>  
<p>👉 To fix this, always start by clearly labelling the triangle: identify the angle in question, then mark opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse sides accordingly.</p>  
<p>👉 Students often also try to use sin when tan or cos is the correct ratio in the context.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the correct trigonometric ratios and labelling sides accurately is essential in exams because it forms the foundation for solving a wide variety of triangle problems accurately.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write the full formula with correctly labelled sides before substituting numbers. This prevents careless mistakes and gains method marks. Show every step clearly, including rearranging the formula if needed.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I used sin 30° and multiplied by 8 to get the answer.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I used sin 30° = opposite/hypotenuse and found the hypotenuse by dividing 8 by sin 30°.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I labelled the sides: adjacent = 8 cm, angle = 30°. Using cos 30° = adjacent/hypotenuse, I rearranged to hypotenuse = adjacent / cos 30°. Substituting, hypotenuse = 8 / 0.866, which gives 9.24 cm. I checked the hypotenuse is longer than the adjacent side, so the answer is reasonable.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Clearly labels sides with respect to the angle.</li>  
<li>Chooses the correct ratio (cosine instead of sine).</li>  
<li>Shows algebraic rearrangement before substitution.</li>  
<li>Includes a reasonableness check, demonstrating full understanding.</li>  
</ul>

20 — SELF-CHECK QUESTION  
Paste into: Self-check question

Question:  
A right-angled triangle has an angle of 50°, and the side opposite this angle is 7 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse. Show all working.

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

Step 1: Label sides; opposite = 7 cm, angle = 50°, hypotenuse = unknown.  
Step 2: Use sin 50° = opposite/hypotenuse.  
Step 3: Rearrange to hypotenuse = opposite / sin 50°.  
Step 4: Calculate: hypotenuse = 7 / 0.766 = 9.14 cm.  
Step 5: Check answer is longer than opposite side, reasonable.  
Final answer: 9.14 cm (to 2 decimal places).  
</details>

23 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Opposite</strong> – The side across from the angle you are working with.</p>  
<p><strong>Adjacent</strong> – The side next to the angle you are working with, not the hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Hypotenuse</strong> – The longest side of a right-angled triangle opposite the right angle.</p>  
<p><strong>Sine (sin)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Cosine (cos)</strong> – Ratio of adjacent side to hypotenuse.</p>  
<p><strong>Tangent (tan)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to adjacent side.</p>  
<p><strong>Rearrange</strong> – Changing a formula to make a different variable the subject.</p>  
<p><strong>Calculate</strong> – Use numbers in a formula to find an answer.</p>  
<p><strong>Solve</strong> – Find the value of an unknown, often using algebra and substitution.</p>  
<p><strong>Show That</strong> – A command in exams asking you to demonstrate a result by clear working.</p>
