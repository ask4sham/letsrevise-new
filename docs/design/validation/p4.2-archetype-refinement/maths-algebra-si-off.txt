9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Algebra</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 At its simplest, algebra is about expressing problems with letters instead of just numbers.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>We use letters (variables) to represent unknown numbers and then manipulate expressions and equations to find those unknowns.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>We combine like terms to simplify expressions (e.g., 2x + 5x = 7x).</li>  
<li>We expand brackets to write expressions as sums or differences (e.g., (x + 2)(x + 3) expands to x² + 5x + 6).</li>  
<li>We factorise to reverse expansion and make expressions easier to solve (e.g., x² + 5x = x(x + 5)).</li>  
<li>We solve equations by isolating the variable using inverse operations.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Expression → combine/simplify makes problems easier.</li>  
<li>Equation → solving finds unknown values.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always show clear steps: simplify first, then solve. Use correct terminology like “expand”, “factorise”, and “collect like terms” to earn method marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is forgetting to apply the inverse operation properly when solving equations, such as subtracting a number incorrectly or missing a step.</p>

<p><strong>Correction:</strong> Always perform the same operation on both sides of the equation, and check each step carefully by substituting back your solution.</p>

<p>This ensures you get full marks by showing a clear, logical solving process.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Algebra is tested in almost every GCSE maths paper and underpins many problem-solving questions, so mastering algebra techniques is key to scoring well.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>When solving equations, write down each step clearly and use the correct algebraic terms such as simplify, expand, factorise, or solve. This shows the examiner your method and can earn method marks even if the final answer is incorrect.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The answer to the equation is 3.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The answer is 3 because I solved the equation by subtracting 2 and dividing.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>I solved the equation 3x + 2 = 11 by first subtracting 2 from both sides to get 3x = 9, then dividing both sides by 3 to find x = 3. This satisfies the original equation when substituted back.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the method clearly with correct terminology.</li>  
<li>Shows step-by-step logic demonstrating understanding.</li>  
<li>Includes verification, which examiners reward.</li>  
</ul>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Solve for x: 2(x - 3) = 10

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

Step 1: Expand brackets: 2x - 6 = 10  
Step 2: Add 6 to both sides: 2x = 16  
Step 3: Divide both sides by 2: x = 8

Check: Substitute x = 8  
2(8 - 3) = 2(5) = 10 ✔

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Variable</strong> – A symbol (usually a letter) used to represent an unknown number.  
<strong>Expression</strong> – A mathematical phrase made of numbers, variables, and operations without an equals sign.  
<strong>Equation</strong> – A statement that two expressions are equal, containing an equals sign.  
<strong>Coefficient</strong> – A number multiplying a variable in an expression.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To combine like terms and write an expression in its simplest form.  
<strong>Expand</strong> – To multiply out brackets to create a polynomial expression.  
<strong>Factorise</strong> – To write an expression as a product of its factors (reverse of expanding).  
<strong>Solve</strong> – To find the value of the variable in an equation.  
<strong>Quadratic</strong> – An expression or equation involving the variable raised to the second power (x²).  
<strong>Substitute</strong> – Replacing a variable with a number to evaluate an expression.
