3 — DEFINITION  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Definition</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 A quadratic expression is an algebraic expression where the highest power of the variable is two, usually written as <strong>ax² + bx + c</strong>, where a ≠ 0.</p>  
<p>👉 A quadratic equation sets such an expression equal to zero, for example, <strong>ax² + bx + c = 0</strong>.</p>  

4 — WHY IT MATTERS  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratic functions model many real-world situations such as projectile motion, area problems, and optimisation tasks, so understanding them helps us interpret patterns and solve practical problems.</p>  

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 A quadratic function’s graph is a parabola defined by y = ax² + bx + c, where:</p>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>a</strong> controls whether it opens upwards (a > 0) or downwards (a < 0).</li>  
<li><strong>b</strong> controls the position of the vertex along the x-axis.</li>  
<li><strong>c</strong> is the y-intercept.</li>  
<li>The solutions of ax² + bx + c = 0 are the x-values where the graph crosses the x-axis (roots).</li>  
</ul>  
<p>👉 Key quadratic solving methods include:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>Factorising (where possible)</li>  
<li>Completing the square</li>  
<li>The quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a</li>  
</ul>  

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>  
<li>y = x² - 4x + 3 factorises to (x - 3)(x - 1); roots at x = 3 and x = 1.</li>  
<li>Completing the square for y = x² + 6x + 5 gives (x + 3)² - 4; vertex at (-3, -4).</li>  
<li>Using quadratic formula for 2x² - 4x - 6 = 0 gives roots x = 3 and x = -1.</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Quadratics</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is understanding the shape and solutions of quadratic equations through their graphs and algebraic forms.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The graph of y = ax² + bx + c is a curve called a parabola, opening upwards if a > 0, downwards if a < 0.</li>  
<li>The parabola’s lowest/highest point is the vertex; this tells us the minimum/maximum value of the quadratic.</li>  
<li>The roots (solutions) of the equation ax² + bx + c = 0 are points where the graph crosses the x-axis.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The coefficients a, b, and c affect the position and shape of the graph.</li>  
<li>The discriminant (b² - 4ac) determines the nature of solutions: two distinct real roots (if > 0), one real root (if = 0), or no real roots (if < 0).</li>  
<li>Solving methods reveal these roots either exactly or approximately.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 When asked to solve quadratics, always write the method clearly, calculate accurately, and interpret the roots in context (e.g., times must be positive).</p>  
<p>👉 Remember to justify the nature of roots using the discriminant for higher-tier questions.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is thinking that the quadratic formula always gives two solutions.</p>  
<p>👉 The correction is understanding the discriminant first: if it’s zero there is exactly one repeated root, if negative no real roots exist.</p>  
<p>👉 This earns marks by demonstrating understanding of solution types and avoiding incorrect answers.</p>

<p>👉 Students often confuse the roles of coefficients a and b, especially their relation to the shape of the graph.</p>  
<p>👉 A better way to say it is: <em>a</em> controls how wide and which way the parabola opens, <em>b</em> affects the horizontal position of the vertex.</p>  
<p>👉 This precision in language helps clarify reasoning for examiners.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Solving quadratic equations by factorising

Step 1:  
Write the quadratic equation equal to zero, e.g. x² - 5x + 6 = 0.

↓

Step 2:  
Factorise the quadratic into two brackets: (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0.

↓

Step 3:  
Set each bracket equal to zero: x - 2 = 0 and x - 3 = 0.

↓

Step 4:  
Solve each linear equation for x: x = 2 or x = 3.

Exam link:  
This method works when the quadratic factorises easily and gives exact roots.

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>

<p>The roots are the x-values where the quadratic graph meets the x-axis because that’s where y = 0.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The roots of the quadratic equation are the x-values at which the parabola crosses the x-axis, representing the solutions to ax² + bx + c = 0 since these points make y = 0.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses precise mathematical language like "parabola" and "solutions to the equation".</li>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> Write down the degree of the quadratic expression 4x² - 7x + 2.</p>  
<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Factorise  x² - 6x + 8 and find the roots.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Complete the square to rewrite x² + 8x + 5 in vertex form and state the vertex.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Use the quadratic formula to solve 2x² - 3x - 5 = 0. Show all steps clearly and state the nature of the roots.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: The degree is 2 because the highest power of x is 2.

Q2:  
Factorising: x² - 6x + 8 = (x - 2)(x - 4)  
Roots are x = 2 and x = 4.

Q3:  
Complete the square: x² + 8x + 5 = (x + 4)² - 16 + 5 = (x + 4)² - 11  
Vertex: (-4, -11)

Q4:  
Quadratic formula: x = [-(-3) ± √((-3)² - 4×2×(-5)) ] / 2×2  
= [3 ± √(9 + 40)] / 4  
= [3 ± √49] / 4  
= [3 ± 7] / 4  

Roots:  
x = (3 + 7)/4 = 10/4 = 2.5  
x = (3 - 7)/4 = -4/4 = -1  

Discriminant is 49 (> 0), so two distinct real roots.  
</details>  

23 — SUMMARY

<h2><strong>Summary</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Quadratic expressions are polynomials of degree 2 written as ax² + bx + c.</p>  
<p>👉 Their graphs are parabolas, shaped by the coefficient a and shifted by b and c.</p>  
<p>👉 The solutions to quadratic equations are the x-values where the parabola crosses the x-axis (roots).</p>  
<p>👉 Methods to solve include factorising, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.</p>  
<p>👉 The discriminant (b² - 4ac) determines the number and type of roots.</p>  
<p>👉 Remember to always interpret solutions in the problem context, especially for real-world applications.</p>  
<p><strong>Remember:</strong> Always write full method steps and label your answers in exams to gain maximum marks!</p>  

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Quadratic</strong> – An expression where the highest power of the variable is 2.</p>  
<p><strong>Parabola</strong> – The U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.</p>  
<p><strong>Coefficient</strong> – A number multiplying the variable in an expression, e.g., a, b, c in ax² + bx + c.</p>  
<p><strong>Roots</strong> – The x-values where a quadratic equation equals zero (the graph crosses the x-axis).</p>  
<p><strong>Vertex</strong> – The maximum or minimum point on the parabola.</p>  
<p><strong>Axis of symmetry</strong> – The vertical line through the vertex dividing the parabola into mirror images.</p>  
<p><strong>Discriminant</strong> – The value b² - 4ac showing how many real roots exist.</p>  
<p><strong>Factorise</strong> – Writing a quadratic as a product of two binomials.</p>  
<p><strong>Complete the square</strong> – A method to rewrite a quadratic in the form (x + p)² + q.</p>  
<p><strong>Quadratic formula</strong> – The formula x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a used to find roots.</p>
