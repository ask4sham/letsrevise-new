9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Treaty of Versailles</strong></h2>

<p>👉 The key idea is the Treaty was a peace settlement meant to punish Germany and prevent future conflict, but it had mixed, often unintended, consequences.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Germany lost territory, had to reduce its military, and pay reparations.</li>  
<li>These punishments led to immediate economic hardship and political instability in Germany.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>The Treaty’s terms disarmed Germany (army limited, no air force), making it weaker → which aimed to stop future aggression.</li>  
<li>Loss of key industrial land (e.g., Saar, Polish Corridor) → weakened Germany’s economy → increased unemployment and poverty.</li>  
<li>The War Guilt Clause forced Germany to accept responsibility → justified reparations → created anger and nationalist resentment.</li>  
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 To get marks, explain how a punishment (cause) leads to a consequence (effect) and then why that consequence mattered in history, linking clearly to wider outcomes.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Understanding the Treaty of Versailles is essential because it was not just a peace deal but a trigger for major 20th-century events, including the rise of Nazism and World War Two.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In your answers, don’t just list Treaty terms. Explain carefully how each term caused problems in Germany (cause → effect) and why these problems were important in the bigger historical picture.</p>  
<p>👉 Use connectives like “because”, “which led to”, and “this meant” to link ideas in your explanation.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Treaty made Germany pay money.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Treaty made Germany pay reparations, which caused economic problems.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Treaty of Versailles forced Germany to pay large reparations, which caused inflation and unemployment, leading to social unrest and a loss of support for the Weimar government.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains the cause (reparations) and effect (inflation and unrest).</li>  
<li>It uses specific historical terms like “Weimar government”.</li>  
<li>It links economic impact to political consequences, showing full understanding.</li>  
</ul>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Treaty of Versailles</strong> – The 1919 peace treaty that ended WWI and punished Germany.  
<strong>Reparations</strong> – Money Germany had to pay the Allies as compensation for war damage.  
<strong>War Guilt Clause</strong> – Part of the Treaty that blamed Germany for starting WWI.  
<strong>Disarmament</strong> – Reduction or limitation of a country’s military forces.  
<strong>Territorial losses</strong> – Land taken from Germany by the Treaty.  
<strong>League of Nations</strong> – International organisation created to keep peace after WWI.  
<strong>Demilitarisation</strong> – Removing military forces from regions (e.g., Rhineland).  
<strong>Resentment</strong> – Angry feeling caused by unfair treatment.  
<strong>Appeasement</strong> – Policy of giving in to demands to avoid conflict, used in 1930s.  
<strong>Extremism</strong> – Radical political ideas and actions outside the mainstream.
