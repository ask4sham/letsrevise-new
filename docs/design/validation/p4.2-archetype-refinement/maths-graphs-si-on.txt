9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Graphs</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea is accurately representing and interpreting relationships between variables shown on graphs.</p>

<h3><strong>Method → reason:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Start by labelling your axes with the correct variables and units.</li>
<li>Choose a suitable scale so the graph fills the space and data points are clearly spread out.</li>
<li>Plot points carefully by translating coordinate pairs onto the grid.</li>
<li>Connect points smoothly for curves or straight lines for linear relationships.</li>
<li>Identify key features such as the gradient (rate of change) and intercepts (where graph crosses axes).</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Step → result:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>To find gradient of a straight line: use gradient = (change in y) ÷ (change in x).</li>
<li>The gradient often represents a speed, rate, or other real-world quantity depending on the axes.</li>
<li>The y-intercept gives the starting value of y when x is zero.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Common error → correction:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Students often calculate ‘rise’ only for gradient and forget to divide by the ‘run’ causing incorrect slopes.</li>
<li>Always remember: gradient = vertical change ÷ horizontal change, not just vertical.</li>
<li>Also be careful to read the correct points and units off the axes to avoid scale errors.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Use precise language in your answers like “The gradient of the line is 3 because the distance increases by 3 km every hour.” Avoid vague terms like "it gets bigger".</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<h2><strong>Common mistake</strong></h2>
<p>👉 A common mistake is calculating the gradient as only the vertical change (rise) without dividing by the horizontal change (run).</p>
<p>This means the slope is often overestimated or undervalued.</p>
<p>The correct method is: gradient = change in y ÷ change in x.</p>
<p>In exams, correctly showing both the rise and run earns method marks and avoids losing accuracy points.</p>

14 — STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS  
Paste into: Step-by-step diagram (process)

Process:  
Finding the gradient of a line on a graph

Step 1:  
Identify two clear points on the straight line and write down their coordinates (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).

↓

Step 2:  
Calculate the vertical change (rise) as y₂ − y₁.

↓

Step 3:  
Calculate the horizontal change (run) as x₂ − x₁.

↓

Step 4:  
Divide rise by run to find the gradient = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁).

Exam link:  
Use this gradient to interpret rates in exam questions, like speed from distance-time graphs.

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is plotted on the y-axis of a speed-time graph?</p>

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> Find the y-intercept of the line passing through (0, 5) and (3, 11).</p>

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> A graph shows temperature rising from 10°C at 2 pm to 22°C at 6 pm. Calculate the gradient and explain what it means in context.</p>

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> A linear graph shows revenue (in £) against number of items sold. The line passes through (0, 50) and (10, 250). Calculate the gradient, find the y-intercept, and interpret both values in the context of the business.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>

Q1:  
Answer: Time (hours) is plotted on the x-axis, so speed is on the y-axis.

Q2:  
Answer:  
Since one point is (0, 5), the y-intercept is 5.

Q3:  
Answer:  
Step 1: Calculate gradient = (22 - 10) ÷ (6 - 2) = 12 ÷ 4 = 3  
Step 2: Interpretation: The temperature increases by 3°C every hour between 2 pm and 6 pm.

Q4:  
Answer:  
Step 1: Gradient = (250 - 50) ÷ (10 - 0) = 200 ÷ 10 = 20  
Step 2: Y-intercept = 50  
Step 3: Interpretation: Gradient means the business earns £20 per item sold.  
The y-intercept means the business has £50 of revenue even when no items are sold, possibly a base fee.
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Plot</strong> – To mark points on a graph according to their coordinates.  
<strong>Axes</strong> – The horizontal (x) and vertical (y) lines on a graph.  
<strong>Scale</strong> – The value represented by each unit on the axes.  
<strong>Gradient</strong> – The steepness of a line found by dividing change in y by change in x.  
<strong>Y-intercept</strong> – The point where the graph crosses the y-axis (x=0).  
<strong>Linear</strong> – A graph represented by a straight line.  
<strong>Quadratic</strong> – A curve shaped like a parabola on a graph.  
<strong>Interpret</strong> – To explain the meaning of data or features in context.  
<strong>Rise</strong> – The vertical change between two points on a graph.  
<strong>Run</strong> – The horizontal change between two points on a graph.
