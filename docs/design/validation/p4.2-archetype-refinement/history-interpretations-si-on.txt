9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Historians' interpretations of the Cold War</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that historians disagree on the Cold War’s causes and blame.</p>  

<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>One group, called <strong>Traditionalists</strong>, claim Stalin’s USSR started the Cold War by expanding communism aggressively.</li>  
<li><strong>Revisionists</strong> argue it was the USA’s fault for trying to control Europe and threatening the USSR with weapons.</li>  
<li><strong>Post-Revisionists</strong> say both sides made mistakes and misunderstood each other, so no single cause dominates.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>Interpretation claim:</strong> “The Cold War was caused by X.”</li>  
<li><strong>Evidence for:</strong> Specific Cold War events supporting X, e.g., Stalin’s actions or US policies.</li>  
<li><strong>Evidence against:</strong> Contradictory facts or alternate explanations, challenging the claim.</li>  
<li><strong>Weighed conclusion:</strong> Historian judges which cause fits best overall.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In your answer, always explain the claim, back it with clear evidence, mention what challenges it, then say which explanation is strongest and why.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is simply repeating what one historian says without explaining why or giving evidence.</p>  
<p>👉 Do NOT just say “The USA caused the Cold War because of the Marshall Plan.” Instead, explain how the Marshall Plan threatened the USSR and why that could cause tension.</p>  
<p>👉 This earns marks because it shows understanding, not just description.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Exam boards want you to show understanding of different views and explain why historians disagree about the Cold War causes. This earns higher marks by showing balance, not just fact.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 When you see “Explain why historians disagree” or “Evaluate interpretations,” always:  
<ul>  
<li>Give each interpretation’s claim clearly.</li>  
<li>Support it with detailed evidence.</li>  
<li>Mention what contradicts or challenges it.</li>  
<li>Finish by weighing the evidence and saying which view makes more sense.</li>  
</ul>  
This structure turns a basic answer into a full-mark one.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The USA caused the Cold War because it was aggressive.</p>

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The USA caused the Cold War because it introduced the Marshall Plan to control Europe and threatened the USSR, which increased tensions.</p>

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The Revisionist interpretation argues that the USA caused the Cold War because its Marshall Plan aimed to dominate Europe economically, which alarmed Stalin’s USSR. However, some evidence shows the USSR had ambitions in Eastern Europe, as Traditionalists claim. Therefore, the Post-Revisionist view that mistakes and mistrust on both sides caused the Cold War is most convincing.</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It uses precise terminology (Revisionist, Traditionalist, Post-Revisionist).</li>  
<li>Shows evidence for and against different claims.</li>  
<li>Includes a balanced judgement supported by reasons.</li>  
</ul>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why some historians blame the Soviet Union for starting the Cold War. (4 marks)

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

1. Traditionalist historians blame the Soviet Union because Stalin aimed to spread communism across Eastern Europe and beyond.  
2. Evidence includes the Red Army's control of Eastern Europe after WWII and the establishment of communist governments there.  
3. However, this view is challenged by Revisionists who argue the USA’s policies, like the Truman Doctrine, threatened the USSR.  
4. Overall, the Traditionalist view is convincing because Soviet actions directly increased tensions, but it is important to recognise other factors too.  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Interpretation</strong> – A historian’s explanation about why an event happened.  
<strong>Traditionalist</strong> – Historians who blame the Soviet Union for the Cold War.  
<strong>Revisionist</strong> – Historians who blame the USA for aggressive Cold War policies.  
<strong>Post-Revisionist</strong> – Historians who believe both sides were responsible.  
<strong>Evidence</strong> – Facts or sources used to support historical claims.  
<strong>Evaluate</strong> – To judge carefully with reasons and evidence.  
<strong>Counter-argument</strong> – Evidence or reasons opposing a claim.  
<strong>Judgement</strong> – A conclusion based on weighing evidence.  
<strong>Cause</strong> – A reason why something happened.  
<strong>Context</strong> – The background or situation around an event or interpretation.
