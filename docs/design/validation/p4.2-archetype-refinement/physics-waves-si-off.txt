<p>👉 <strong>A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without transferring matter permanently.</strong></p>

Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>
<p>👉 <strong>Waves are fundamental for communication, medical imaging, and natural phenomena like earthquakes, so understanding them is essential for technology and safety.</strong></p>

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>The core model for waves is: energy causes a disturbance → particles oscillate locally but do not travel → energy moves through the wave → energy arrives at a new place, enabling work or detection.</strong></p>

6 — KEY EXAMPLES  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<ul>

<li>Sound waves – energy moves through air as vibrating air particles, transferring sound energy to your ears.</li>
<li>Electromagnetic waves (like light) – energy travels through space without needing particles to move.</li>
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Waves</strong></h2>
<p>👉 The key idea here is: waves transfer energy without matter travelling along.</p>

<h3><strong>What happens physically:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>When a wave passes through a material (like water or air), its particles vibrate in place.</li>
<li>This vibration or oscillation passes the energy from one particle to the next.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Structure → function:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Particles do not move forward with the wave; instead, they oscillate around fixed points.</li>
<li>This means matter is not transported over long distances, but energy is.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Process → effect:</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li>Energy from a source causes particles to oscillate → oscillations pass energy through the medium → energy reaches detectors or new locations for use.</li>
</ul>

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>
<p>👉 Use phrases showing cause → effect: “Energy causes oscillations in particles, which pass energy along the wave without moving the particles themselves.”</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is saying waves carry matter or particles along from one place to another.</p>  
<p>In reality, particles only oscillate about a fixed point and do not travel with the wave.</p>  
<p>👉 This distinction is vital for exam marks and understanding wave properties correctly.</p>

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 In exam answers, don’t just name wave properties; always explain how they relate to energy transfer and particle movement. Use the cause → effect structure: "Because energy causes particles to oscillate, the wave carries energy forward without moving matter."</p>

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
Explain why waves transfer energy but do not transfer matter.

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

Waves transfer energy because they cause particles in a medium to oscillate and pass this energy on to nearby particles. The particles vibrate around fixed points without moving along the wave path, so matter is not transferred — only energy moves from one place to another.

</details>

<p>👉 Why can sound not travel through a vacuum? Explain using particle vibrations.</p>

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>

Sound requires particles to vibrate and transfer energy. In a vacuum, there are no particles, so no vibrations can occur and sound cannot travel.

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<p><strong>Amplitude</strong> – The maximum displacement of a particle from its rest position.</p>  
<p><strong>Wavelength</strong> – The distance between two corresponding points on adjacent waves.</p>  
<p><strong>Frequency</strong> – How many waves pass a point per second.</p>  
<p><strong>Period</strong> – The time taken for one wave to pass a point.</p>  
<p><strong>Wave speed</strong> – How fast the wave transfers energy through the medium.</p>  
<p><strong>Transverse wave</strong> – Wave where particles oscillate perpendicular to the energy direction.</p>  
<p><strong>Longitudinal wave</strong> – Wave where particles oscillate parallel to the energy direction.</p>  
<p><strong>Oscillation</strong> – Repeated back and forth motion of particles.</p>  
<p><strong>Medium</strong> – The material through which mechanical waves travel.</p>  
<p><strong>Energy transfer</strong> – Movement of energy from one place to another by waves, not particles.</p>
