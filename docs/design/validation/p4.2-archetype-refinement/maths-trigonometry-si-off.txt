<p>👉 <strong>Trigonometry is the study of relationships between angles and sides in right-angled triangles using the ratios sine, cosine, and tangent.</strong></p>

5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)

<p>👉 <strong>The primary trigonometric ratios are defined as:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
<li><strong>sine (sin θ)</strong> = opposite side ÷ hypotenuse</li>  
<li><strong>cosine (cos θ)</strong> = adjacent side ÷ hypotenuse</li>  
<li><strong>tangent (tan θ)</strong> = opposite side ÷ adjacent side</li>  
</ul>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Trigonometry</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Let’s break down how to use trigonometry step-by-step.</p>  

<h3><strong>Key idea:</strong></h3>  
<p>You use the sine, cosine, or tangent ratio depending on which sides and angle you know or want to find.</p>  

<h3><strong>Method → reason:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Identify the right angle in the triangle.</li>  
<li>Label the sides relative to the non-right angle you know (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse).</li>  
<li>Choose the correct trigonometric ratio to relate the known and unknown sides or angle.</li>  
<li>If you are finding a side, multiply the known side by the ratio.</li>  
<li>If you are finding an angle, use the inverse trig function on your calculator.</li>  
</ul>  

<h3><strong>Common error → correction:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Watch out: students often confuse opposite and adjacent! Always label sides first to avoid mistakes.</p>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 In the exam, write the formula you are using clearly, substitute values, rearrange neatly, then calculate your answer with units.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up the opposite and adjacent sides when using trigonometry.</p>  
<p>Students often assume the side next to the angle is the hypotenuse or the opposite side without checking carefully.</p>  
<p><strong>Correction:</strong> Always start by drawing and labelling the triangle clearly, then identify each side correctly relative to the angle you are using.</p>  
<p>Why this matters:</p>  
<ul>  
<li>Choosing the wrong sides leads to incorrect formulas and answers.</li>  
<li>The examiner checks your working method, so accurate labelling earns method marks even if the final answer is wrong.</li>  
</ul>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Trigonometry is a key tool in exams for solving real-world problems, from navigation to architecture.</p>  
<p>Understanding not just the formulas but how to pick the right ratio quickly can save time and help you get better marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always write down the formula you plan to use before substituting values. This shows the examiner your method clearly and earns method marks.</p>  
<p>👉 Use your calculator’s inverse trig buttons (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) when finding angles, and round your answer to the required degree of precision.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The side is found by using sine and dividing.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The side is found using sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, rearranged to opposite = hypotenuse × sin θ.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The side opposite the 30° angle is found using sin 30° = opposite ÷ 10, rearranged to opposite = 10 × sin 30°, which equals 5 cm.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It states the exact formula used with symbols.</li>  
<li>Shows the algebraic rearrangement clearly.</li>  
<li>Includes substitution, calculation, and final answer with units.</li>  
</ul>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam practice questions</strong></h2>  

<p>Q1 (1 mark): What is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm and 4 cm?</p>  

<p>Q2 (2 marks): Find the value of sin 30°.</p>  

<p>Q3 (3 marks): In a right-angled triangle, angle A is 45°, and the hypotenuse is 10 cm. Find the length of the opposite side.</p>  

<p>Q4 (4 marks): A ladder leans against a wall, forming an angle of 60° to the ground. The ladder length is 5 m. Calculate the height it reaches on the wall.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

<p>Q1: Using Pythagoras, hypotenuse = √(3² + 4²) = √(9+16) = √25 = 5 cm.</p>  

<p>Q2: sin 30° = 0.5 (exact value).</p>  

<p>Q3: Use sin 45° = opposite ÷ hypotenuse → opposite = 10 × sin 45° ≈ 10 × 0.707 = 7.07 cm.</p>  

<p>Q4: Use sin 60° = height ÷ 5 → height = 5 × sin 60° = 5 × 0.866 = 4.33 m.</p>  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Hypotenuse</strong> – The longest side in a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.  
<strong>Opposite</strong> – The side opposite the angle you are using in trigonometry.  
<strong>Adjacent</strong> – The side next to the angle you are using, but not the hypotenuse.  
<strong>Sine (sin)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to hypotenuse.  
<strong>Cosine (cos)</strong> – Ratio of adjacent side to hypotenuse.  
<strong>Tangent (tan)</strong> – Ratio of opposite side to adjacent side.  
<strong>Inverse sine (sin⁻¹)</strong> – Calculator function used to find an angle from sine ratio.  
<strong>Inverse cosine (cos⁻¹)</strong> – Calculator function used to find an angle from cosine ratio.  
<strong>Inverse tangent (tan⁻¹)</strong> – Calculator function used to find an angle from tangent ratio.  
<strong>Right angle</strong> – A 90° angle, defining the triangle as right-angled.
