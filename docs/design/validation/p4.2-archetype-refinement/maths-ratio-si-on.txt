5 — CORE MODEL  
Paste into: Core rule (key idea)  

<p>👉 <strong>Core method for solving ratio problems:</strong></p>  
<ol>  
<li>Simplify the ratio to smallest whole numbers.</li>  
<li>Find total parts by adding ratio numbers.</li>  
<li>Calculate value of one part by dividing total amount by total parts.</li>  
<li>Multiply one part by each ratio number to find each share.</li>  
<li>Check the sum of shares equals the total amount.</li>  
</ol>

9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Teaching: Ratio</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 <strong>Key idea:</strong></p>  
<p>Ratio problems are about dividing a total amount into parts based on a given relationship between quantities.</p>  

<h3><strong>Step 1: Simplify</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always check if the ratio can be simplified. For example, 6:8 can be simplified to 3:4 by dividing both sides by 2.</p>  

<h3><strong>Step 2: Total parts</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Add ratio numbers to find the total number of parts that the total amount is split into. E.g., ratio 3:2 has 3 + 2 = 5 parts in total.</p>  

<h3><strong>Step 3: One part value</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Divide the total quantity by total parts to find the value of one part. For example, if total is 120 and total parts is 5, one part = 120 ÷ 5 = 24.</p>  

<h3><strong>Step 4: Calculate each share</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Multiply one part by each ratio number to find each share. Using above, 3 parts = 3 × 24 = 72; 2 parts = 2 × 24 = 48.</p>  

<h3><strong>Step 5: Check your answer</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Add your calculated shares and check they equal the original total. If not, recheck your calculations.</p>  

<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 State method steps clearly, show each calculation, and write a final check sentence to gain full marks.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake  

<h2><strong>Common mistakes with ratio</strong></h2>  

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios. For example, treating 3:2 as 3 parts out of 2, instead of summing 3 + 2 parts.</p>  

<p>👉 Another error is failing to simplify the ratio first, causing more complicated calculations or wrong answers.</p>  

<p>👉 Do NOT just guess shares without dividing total by total parts; this loses method marks.</p>

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Split £100 using the ratio 2:3 by just guessing or dividing by one number only.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Divide 100 by 5, then multiply to find shares but don’t show all steps clearly.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>First simplify ratio if needed. Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5. One part = 100 ÷ 5 = 20. Then share amounts: 2 × 20 = 40, 3 × 20 = 60. Check: 40 + 60 = 100.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It shows clear method steps expected by examiners.</li>  
<li>It uses correct terminology and calculations.</li>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)  

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p>Use the ratio 3:4:5 to answer the following:</p>  

<p><strong>Q1 (1 mark):</strong> What is the total number of parts?</p>  

<p><strong>Q2 (2 marks):</strong> If the total quantity is 60, find the value of one part.</p>  

<p><strong>Q3 (3 marks):</strong> Calculate the amount corresponding to the 3 parts share and check your sums.</p>  

<p><strong>Q4 (4 marks):</strong> Alice, Bob, and Carol share money in ratio 3:4:5. Bob gives £6 to Alice. What is the new ratio?</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  
<p><strong>Q1:</strong> 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 parts</p>  
<p><strong>Q2:</strong> One part = 60 ÷ 12 = 5</p>  
<p><strong>Q3:</strong> 3 parts = 3 × 5 = 15; Check sum: 15 + 20 + 25 = 60 (correct).</p>  
<p><strong>Q4:</strong> Bob gives £6 to Alice:<br>Original shares: A=15, B=20, C=25.<br>After transfer: Alice = 15 + 6 = 21, Bob = 20 – 6 = 14.<br>New ratio: 21:14:25.<br>Simplify: divide by 7 → 3:2:25 (can't simplify 25), so final ratio is 21:14:25 (no further simplification).</p>  
</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words  

<strong>Ratio</strong> – A comparison of two or more quantities showing their relative size.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – Reducing a ratio or fraction to its smallest whole number form.  
<strong>Total parts</strong> – The sum of all the parts in a ratio.  
<strong>One part</strong> – The value of a single unit when total is divided by total parts.  
<strong>Share</strong> – Each quantity given according to ratio.  
<strong>Proportion</strong> – Part of a whole expressed as a fraction or ratio.  
<strong>Calculate</strong> – To work out a value using arithmetic.  
<strong>Solve</strong> – Find an answer to a problem.  
<strong>Show that</strong> – Prove a result using given info or working.  
<strong>Combined ratio</strong> – Ratios that involve sharing or comparing multiple groups linked together.
