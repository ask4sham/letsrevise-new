9 — CORE TEACHING  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Teaching: Ratio</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 The key idea is that ratio expresses how much of one quantity relates to another.</p>  
<h3><strong>What happens:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Ratios compare amounts directly — for example, 3 : 2 means 3 parts of one thing for every 2 parts of another.</li>  
<li>To work with ratios, simplify if possible to find the basic units of comparison.</li>  
<li>Once simplified, we can scale up or down by multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Structure → function / Process → effect:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>Express given quantities as a ratio → Simplify ratio → Scale ratio to find missing amounts.</li>  
<li>This process ensures proportions stay consistent and values relate correctly.</li>  
</ul>  
<h3><strong>Think like an examiner:</strong></h3>  
<p>👉 Always state the simplified ratio first. Use multiplication or division to scale. Give final answers with correct units and in simplest form.</p>

12 — COMMON MISTAKE  
Paste into: Common mistake

<p>👉 A common mistake is mixing up when to multiply and when to divide in ratio problems.</p>  
<p>Students often divide when they should multiply to find equivalent ratios or scale up, or vice versa.</p>  
<p>Do NOT just guess what to do—think: if you want a bigger amount, multiply; if you want to simplify or find one part, divide.</p>  
<p>This precision earns marks and avoids errors.</p>

16 — EXAM TIP  
Paste into: Exam tip (concept)

<h2><strong>🌍 Why this matters</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 <strong>Ratios often appear in real-life problem-solving contexts like cooking, mixing substances, or comparing groups.</strong> Getting the ratio right ensures you scale amounts exactly, preserving relationships.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Premium Exam Tip</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 Always simplify ratios first before multiplying or dividing. Write down each step clearly and include units in your final answer to get full marks.</p>  

<h2><strong>🎯 Answer Quality Modelling</strong></h2>  
<p>👉 This is how to move from a basic answer to a full-mark explanation.</p>  

<h3><strong>Weak answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio is 3 : 2, so the second amount is 200 ml.</p>  

<h3><strong>Better answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>The ratio of orange to apple juice is 3 : 2 and 900 ml of orange is used, so apple juice is (900 ÷ 3) × 2 = 600 ml.</p>  

<h3><strong>Full-mark answer:</strong></h3>  
<p>Given the ratio orange:apple = 3 : 2, first find 1 part by dividing 900 ml by 3, which is 300 ml. Then multiply 300 ml by 2 to find apple juice required: 600 ml, maintaining the correct ratio.</p>  

<h3><strong>Why the full-mark answer is stronger:</strong></h3>  
<ul>  
<li>It explains each step clearly with calculation details.</li>  
<li>Uses precise ratio terminology "parts".</li>  
<li>Presents a cause → process → effect chain showing understanding.</li>  
</ul>  

17 — WORKED EXAMPLE  
Paste into: Worked example (checkpoint)

Question:  
The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 5 : 6. If there are 120 boys, how many girls are there?  

Answer:  
<details>  
<summary>Reveal Answer</summary>  

The ratio is 5 : 6. One part = 120 ÷ 5 = 24.  
Girls = 6 parts = 6 × 24 = 144.  
So, there are 144 girls in the school.  

</details>

22 — EXAM PRACTICE  
Paste into: Text (concept)

<h2><strong>Exam Practice</strong></h2>  

<p>Q1 (1 mark): Simplify the ratio 15 : 20.</p>  
<p>Q2 (2 marks): In a group, the ratio of cats to dogs is 7 : 5. If there are 35 cats, how many dogs are there?</p>  
<p>Q3 (3 marks): A map uses a scale of 1 : 50,000. If two towns are 4 cm apart, what is the real distance? Show your working clearly.</p>  
<p>Q4 (4 marks): The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 3 : 4. There are 196 children in total. Find the number of boys and girls.</p>  

<details>  
<summary>Reveal Model Answers</summary>  

Q1: 15 : 20 simplified by dividing both by 5 → 3 : 4.  

Q2: One part = 35 ÷ 7 = 5 dogs per part. Dogs = 5 parts × 5 = 25 dogs.  

Q3: 1 cm on map = 50,000 cm real distance (500 m).  
4 cm on map = 4 × 50,000 = 200,000 cm = 2,000 m = 2 km.  

Q4: Ratio 3 : 4, total parts = 7 parts.  
One part = 196 ÷ 7 = 28 children.  
Boys = 3 × 28 = 84; Girls = 4 × 28 = 112.  

</details>

24 — KEY WORDS  
Paste into: Key words

<strong>Ratio</strong> – A comparison of two or more quantities showing their relative sizes.  
<strong>Simplify</strong> – To reduce numbers to their smallest whole number relationship.  
<strong>Equivalent ratio</strong> – Other ratios that have the same simplified form.  
<strong>Scale</strong> – To multiply or divide both parts of a ratio by the same number.  
<strong>Parts</strong> – Units into which quantities are divided when expressing ratios.  
<strong>Proportion</strong> – A statement that two ratios are equal.  
<strong>Divide</strong> – To split a number into equal parts.  
<strong>Multiply</strong> – To increase a number by repetitions.  
<strong>Units</strong> – Labels that describe measurements (e.g., grams, ml).  
<strong>Simplest form</strong> – The lowest whole numbers that express the ratio correctly.
